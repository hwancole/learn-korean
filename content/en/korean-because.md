Giving a reason is one of the first things you want to do once you can make simple sentences: "I'm late *because* of traffic," "Let's take a taxi *since* we're short on time." Korean has no single word for "because." Instead, you attach an ending to the verb, and there are three common choices.

> **Quick answer:** The three main ways to say "because" in Korean are **-아서/어서** (*-aseo/-eoseo*), **-(으)니까** (*-(eu)nikka*) and **때문에** (*ttaemune*). Use **-아서/어서** for everyday reasons, apologies and thanks: **늦어서 미안해요** (*Neujeoseo mianhaeyo*, "Sorry I'm late," literally "Because I'm late, sorry"). Use **-(으)니까** when the second half is a request or suggestion: **비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요** (*Biga onikka usaneul gajyeogaseyo*, "It's raining, so take an umbrella"). Use **때문에** after a noun, or **-기 때문에** after a verb, for a clearer, more formal "because of": **비 때문에** (*bi ttaemune*, "because of the rain").

In all three, the reason comes **first** and the result comes second, the opposite of the usual English order "X because Y." This lesson builds on the polite -아요/어요 style from [the present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/) and the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## The three options at a glance

| Form | Attaches to | Tone | Request or suggestion after it? | Past tense inside? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -아서/어서 | verb or adjective stem | neutral, everyday | No | No | 배고파서 먹었어요. |
| -(으)니까 | verb or adjective stem | spoken, the speaker's own reason | Yes | Yes | 추우니까 문 닫아 주세요. |
| 때문에 | noun | clear cause, often negative | Usually not | (n/a) | 시험 때문에 바빠요. |
| -기 때문에 | verb or adjective stem | formal, written, emphatic | No | Yes | 바쁘기 때문에 못 가요. |

If you only learn one today, learn **-아서/어서**. It is the most common in everyday conversation and it is used in two of the most useful phrases in Korean, "sorry for…" and "thank you for…".

## -아서/어서: the everyday "because"

**How to form it:** take the polite present form (the -아요/어요 form) and replace **요** with **서**. If you can say 가요, you can say 가서.

| Dictionary form | Polite present | + 서 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 (go) | 가요 | 가서 | gaseo | because (I) go |
| 오다 (come) | 와요 | 와서 | waseo | because (it) comes |
| 먹다 (eat) | 먹어요 | 먹어서 | meogeoseo | because (I) eat |
| 마시다 (drink) | 마셔요 | 마셔서 | masyeoseo | because (I) drink |
| 하다 (do) | 해요 | 해서 | haeseo | because (I) do |
| 바쁘다 (be busy) | 바빠요 | 바빠서 | bappaseo | because (I'm) busy |
| 아프다 (be sick) | 아파요 | 아파서 | apaseo | because (I'm) sick |
| 춥다 (be cold) | 추워요 | 추워서 | chuwoseo | because (it's) cold |
| 맛있다 (be tasty) | 맛있어요 | 맛있어서 | masisseoseo | because (it's) tasty |
| 없다 (not have) | 없어요 | 없어서 | eopseoseo | because (I) don't have |

With a noun, use **-(이)라서**: **학생이라서** (*haksaengiraseo*, "because I'm a student") after a consonant, **친구라서** (*chinguraseo*, "because they're my friend") after a vowel.

### Examples

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 배고파서 밥을 먹었어요. | Baegopaseo bapeul meogeosseoyo. | I was hungry, so I ate. |
| 피곤해서 일찍 잤어요. | Pigonhaeseo iljjik jasseoyo. | I was tired, so I went to bed early. |
| 비가 와서 집에 있었어요. | Biga waseo jibe isseosseoyo. | It rained, so I stayed home. |
| 너무 비싸서 안 샀어요. | Neomu bissaseo an sasseoyo. | It was too expensive, so I didn't buy it. |
| 아파서 학교에 못 갔어요. | Apaseo hakgyoe mot gasseoyo. | I was sick, so I couldn't go to school. |

The last two use 안 and 못 from [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/).

### Rule 1: no past tense before -아서/어서

The tense goes on the **final** verb only. Even when the reason happened in the past, -아서/어서 stays in its plain form.

- ✅ **어제 아파서 못 갔어요.** (*Eoje apaseo mot gasseoyo.*) "I was sick yesterday, so I couldn't go."
- ❌ 어제 아팠어서 못 갔어요.

### Rule 2: no requests or suggestions after it

The second half of an -아서/어서 sentence cannot be a command (-(으)세요), a request (-아/어 주세요), a suggestion (-(으)ㄹ까요?) or "let's" (-아요/어요 used as "let's"). For those, switch to -(으)니까.

- ❌ 추워서 창문을 닫아 주세요.
- ✅ **추우니까 창문을 닫아 주세요.** (*Chuunikka changmuneul dada juseyo.*) "It's cold, so please close the window."

### "Sorry for" and "thank you for"

This is where -아서/어서 shines. Apologies and thanks almost always use it, and -(으)니까 sounds wrong here.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 늦어서 미안해요. | Neujeoseo mianhaeyo. | Sorry I'm late. |
| 늦어서 죄송합니다. | Neujeoseo joesonghamnida. | I'm very sorry I'm late. (formal) |
| 도와줘서 고마워요. | Dowajwoseo gomawoyo. | Thanks for helping me. |
| 와 주셔서 감사합니다. | Wa jusyeoseo gamsahamnida. | Thank you for coming. (formal) |
| 만나서 반가워요. | Mannaseo bangawoyo. | Nice to meet you. (lit. "Because we met, I'm glad") |

You probably already know the last one from [Korean greetings](/learn-korean/en/korean-greetings/). Now you can see how it is built: 만나다 (meet) + -아서 + 반갑다 (be glad). The formal versions use honorific -(으)시-, explained in [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

## -(으)니까: "since," with requests and suggestions

**How to form it:** after a vowel stem add **-니까**; after a consonant stem add **-으니까**.

| Dictionary form | -(으)니까 | Romanization | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 가니까 | ganikka | vowel stem |
| 바쁘다 | 바쁘니까 | bappeunikka | vowel stem |
| 먹다 | 먹으니까 | meogeunikka | consonant stem |
| 맛있다 | 맛있으니까 | masisseunikka | consonant stem |
| 춥다 | 추우니까 | chuunikka | ㅂ becomes 우 |
| 듣다 | 들으니까 | deureunikka | ㄷ becomes ㄹ |
| 살다 | 사니까 | sanikka | ㄹ drops before ㄴ |
| 학생이다 | 학생이니까 | haksaenginikka | noun + 이니까 |
| 친구다 | 친구니까 | chingunikka | noun ending in a vowel |

Unlike -아서/어서, **-(으)니까 can carry the past tense**: **갔으니까** (*gasseunikka*, "since (he) went"), **먹었으니까** (*meogeosseunikka*, "since (I) ate").

### Examples

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요. | Biga onikka usaneul gajyeogaseyo. | It's raining, so take an umbrella. |
| 시간이 없으니까 택시를 탈까요? | Sigani eopseunikka taeksireul talkkayo? | We don't have time, so shall we take a taxi? |
| 오늘은 바쁘니까 내일 만나요. | Oneureun bappeunikka naeil mannayo. | I'm busy today, so let's meet tomorrow. |
| 밥을 먹었으니까 이제 커피 마셔요. | Babeul meogeosseunikka ije keopi masyeoyo. | We've eaten, so let's have coffee now. |
| 여기는 비싸니까 다른 데 가요. | Yeogineun bissanikka dareun de gayo. | It's expensive here, so let's go somewhere else. |

The second example uses [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/), and the first uses the polite command -(으)세요. Both are only possible with -(으)니까.

### The nuance of -(으)니까

-(으)니까 presents the reason as the **speaker's own judgment**, often something the listener should already see or accept: "since it's raining (you can see that), take an umbrella." That makes it perfect for persuading and planning. It also means it can sound a little pushy or like an excuse in some sentences, especially when you are answering "why?" to someone senior. When the reason is simply a fact you are reporting, -아서/어서 sounds softer.

## 때문에 and -기 때문에: "because of"

**After a noun**, just add **때문에**:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 비 때문에 경기가 취소됐어요. | Bi ttaemune gyeonggiga chwisodwaesseoyo. | The game was canceled because of the rain. |
| 시험 때문에 바빠요. | Siheom ttaemune bappayo. | I'm busy because of exams. |
| 감기 때문에 목이 아파요. | Gamgi ttaemune mogi apayo. | My throat hurts because of a cold. |
| 저 때문에 늦었어요? | Jeo ttaemune neujeosseoyo? | Were you late because of me? |

**After a verb or adjective**, add **-기 때문에** to the stem. Past tense goes before it: **-았/었기 때문에**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 시험이 있기 때문에 공부해야 해요. | Naeil siheomi itgi ttaemune gongbuhaeya haeyo. | I have an exam tomorrow, so I have to study. |
| 길이 막히기 때문에 지하철을 타요. | Giri makigi ttaemune jihacheoreul tayo. | The roads are jammed, so I take the subway. |
| 늦게 일어났기 때문에 버스를 놓쳤어요. | Neutge ireonatgi ttaemune beoseureul nochyeosseoyo. | I got up late, so I missed the bus. |

The first sentence uses [-아야/어야 해요](/learn-korean/en/korean-have-to/). -기 때문에 sounds firmer and more formal than the other two. You'll see it in news, essays and the TOPIK writing section, and people use it in speech when they want to stress the reason. Like -아서/어서, it is not normally followed by a request or suggestion.

### 때문에 vs 덕분에

Noun + 때문에 often suggests a **bad** cause. For a good cause, Koreans prefer **덕분에** (*deokbune*, "thanks to"):

- **친구 덕분에 길을 찾았어요.** (*Chingu deokbune gireul chajasseoyo.*) "Thanks to my friend, I found the way."
- **덕분에 잘 지냈어요.** (*Deokbune jal jinaesseoyo.*) "Thanks to you, I've been well." A common polite reply to "How have you been?"

## Answering "why?" with a short reason

When someone asks **왜요?** (*Waeyo?*, "Why?"), you don't need a full sentence. End the reason with **요**, or use **때문이에요**. 왜 is covered in [Korean question words](/learn-korean/en/korean-question-words/).

| Question | Short answer | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 왜 안 왔어요? | 아파서요. | Apaseoyo. | Because I was sick. |
| 왜 택시 타요? | 시간이 없으니까요. | Sigani eopseunikkayo. | Because we don't have time. |
| 왜 바빠요? | 시험 때문이에요. | Siheom ttaemunieyo. | It's because of exams. |
| 왜 공부해요? | 한국에서 일하고 싶기 때문이에요. | Hangugeseo ilhago sipgi ttaemunieyo. | It's because I want to work in Korea. |

The last answer uses [-고 싶어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/).

## Starting a sentence with "so" or "because"

Each ending has a matching word you can put at the start of the next sentence:

| Word | Romanization | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 그래서 | geuraeseo | so, therefore | 비가 왔어요. 그래서 집에 있었어요. |
| 그러니까 | geureonikka | so (then), that's why | 늦었어요. 그러니까 빨리 가요. |
| 왜냐하면 | waenyahamyeon | the reason is | 못 가요. 왜냐하면 일이 있기 때문이에요. |

**왜냐하면** is usually finished with **-기 때문이에요** or **-거든요** at the end of the sentence. In casual speech, 그러니까 is also used as a filler meaning "I mean" or "exactly."

## Which one should I use?

Ask yourself two questions:

1. **Is the second half a request, command or suggestion** (-(으)세요, -아/어 주세요, -(으)ㄹ까요, "let's")? → Use **-(으)니까**.
2. **Are you apologizing or thanking** (미안해요, 죄송합니다, 고마워요, 감사합니다)? → Use **-아서/어서**.

Otherwise, **-아서/어서** is the safe default in conversation. Use **noun + 때문에** for "because of [thing]," and **-기 때문에** when you're writing formally or want to stress the reason.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 늦었어서 미안해요. | 늦어서 미안해요. | No past tense before -아서/어서. |
| 늦었으니까 미안해요. | 늦어서 미안해요. | Apologies and thanks use -아서/어서. |
| 비가 와서 우산 가져가세요. | 비가 오니까 우산 가져가세요. | Commands need -(으)니까. |
| 바빠서 내일 만날까요? | 바쁘니까 내일 만날까요? | Suggestions need -(으)니까. |
| 비가 때문에 | 비 때문에 / 비가 오기 때문에 | 때문에 attaches directly to a noun, without 이/가. |
| 친구 때문에 합격했어요. | 친구 덕분에 합격했어요. | Use 덕분에 for a good cause. |

Then give three reasons of your own: why you're learning Korean, why you were late (or early) last time, and a suggestion for a friend using -(으)니까.

## FAQ

**What is the most common way to say "because" in Korean?** In everyday conversation, **-아서/어서** is the most common. It is neutral, polite in any speech level, and used in set phrases like 만나서 반가워요 and 늦어서 미안해요.

**Can -아서/어서 mean "and then"?** Yes. With verbs like 가다, 오다 and 만나다 it can link two actions in sequence, where the second happens at the place of the first: **친구를 만나서 영화를 봤어요** (*Chingureul mannaseo yeonghwareul bwasseoyo*, "I met a friend and (we) watched a movie"). Context tells you whether it means "because" or "and then."

**Is -(으)니까 rude?** Not by itself. It's used all the time in polite speech with 요-endings. It can sound a bit blunt when you use it to justify yourself to someone senior, because it frames the reason as obvious. In those cases, -아서요 or -기 때문이에요 sounds softer.

**Does -(으)니까 have another meaning?** Yes. With a past action in the second half, it can mean "when I did X, I found that…": **집에 가니까 아무도 없었어요** (*Jibe ganikka amudo eopseosseoyo*, "When I got home, nobody was there"). This is a discovery, not a reason.

**What's the difference between 때문에 and 때문이에요?** 때문에 links to a following clause ("because of X, Y"). 때문이에요 ends the sentence ("it's because of X"). Use 때문이에요 for short answers to 왜요?

**What's next?** Practice combining these endings with the verbs you know from [can and can't](/learn-korean/en/korean-can-cant/) and [the future tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/), for example **내일 일이 있어서 못 갈 거예요** (*Naeil iri isseoseo mot gal geoyeyo*, "I have work tomorrow, so I won't be able to go").
