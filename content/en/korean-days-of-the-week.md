The days of the week are some of the first words you need for making plans, reading opening hours or booking anything in Korea. They are also one of the easiest vocabulary sets to learn, because all seven follow the same pattern: one syllable for the day plus **요일** (*yoil*), "day of the week."

> **Quick answer:** The days of the week in Korean are **월요일** (*woryoil*, Monday), **화요일** (*hwayoil*, Tuesday), **수요일** (*suyoil*, Wednesday), **목요일** (*mogyoil*, Thursday), **금요일** (*geumyoil*, Friday), **토요일** (*toyoil*, Saturday) and **일요일** (*iryoil*, Sunday). To say "on Monday," add **에**: **월요일에** (*woryoire*). To ask what day it is, say **오늘 무슨 요일이에요?** (*Oneul museun yoirieyo?*). The answer: **오늘은 목요일이에요** (*Oneureun mogyoirieyo*, "Today is Thursday").

This lesson uses the polite -아/어요 style from [the present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/) and the particle 에 from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system, which spells words the way they are pronounced.

## The seven days at a glance

| English | Korean | Romanization | Pronounced | Short form | Literal meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 월요일 | woryoil | [워료일] | 월 | moon day |
| Tuesday | 화요일 | hwayoil | [화요일] | 화 | fire day |
| Wednesday | 수요일 | suyoil | [수요일] | 수 | water day |
| Thursday | 목요일 | mogyoil | [모교일] | 목 | wood (tree) day |
| Friday | 금요일 | geumyoil | [그묘일] | 금 | metal (gold) day |
| Saturday | 토요일 | toyoil | [토요일] | 토 | earth day |
| Sunday | 일요일 | iryoil | [이료일] | 일 | sun day |

The Korean week is usually written starting from Monday, as in the table, although many wall calendars start with Sunday (일요일).

## Why the days have these names

Each first syllable comes from a Chinese character for one of the seven visible "lights" in the sky: the moon, the sun and the five planets known in ancient times. In East Asia the five planets were linked to the five elements, so the days are named after **moon, fire, water, wood, metal, earth and sun**.

| Day | Hanja | Element or body | Planet | Native Korean word for it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 월요일 | 月 | moon | (moon) | 달 (dal) |
| 화요일 | 火 | fire | Mars | 불 (bul) |
| 수요일 | 水 | water | Mercury | 물 (mul) |
| 목요일 | 木 | wood | Jupiter | 나무 (namu) |
| 금요일 | 金 | metal, gold | Venus | 쇠 / 금 (soe / geum) |
| 토요일 | 土 | earth | Saturn | 흙 (heuk) |
| 일요일 | 日 | sun | (sun) | 해 (hae) |

If you know some European languages, the pattern may look familiar: Monday is "moon day" in English too, Tuesday is *martes* (Mars) in Spanish, and Saturday is Saturn's day. Korean, Japanese and the European languages all use the same seven-luminary week, just with different names for the gods and planets.

A simple memory trick is to picture the order as a little story: the **moon** (월) rises, a **fire** (화) is lit, **water** (수) puts it out, a **tree** (목) grows, you dig up **gold** (금) from the **earth** (토), and then the **sun** (일) comes up on the weekend.

## Pronunciation tips

Four of the seven days have a sound change, because a final consonant moves over to the vowel-initial syllable 요. Revised Romanization shows the spoken form, which is why 월요일 is written *woryoil*, not *wolyoil*.

- **월요일** sounds like [워료일]: the ㄹ links to 요 and is pronounced as a flap, close to the "dd" in American English "ladder."
- **목요일** sounds like [모교일]: the ㄱ moves over and softens toward a g sound.
- **금요일** sounds like [그묘일]: the ㅁ moves to the next syllable.
- **일요일** sounds like [이료일]: just like 월요일, the ㄹ links forward.

The ending 요일 is the same every time, so once you can say one day smoothly, the rest follow. If you still find ㄹ tricky, the [Hangul alphabet](/learn-korean/en/hangul-alphabet/) lesson covers how it changes between vowels.

## Asking and saying what day it is

The fixed question for days of the week uses **무슨** (*museun*, "what kind of"), not 뭐. You met 무슨 in [Korean question words](/learn-korean/en/korean-question-words/).

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 오늘 무슨 요일이에요? | Oneul museun yoirieyo? | What day is it today? |
| 오늘은 목요일이에요. | Oneureun mogyoirieyo. | Today is Thursday. |
| 내일은 무슨 요일이에요? | Naeireun museun yoirieyo? | What day is tomorrow? |
| 내일은 금요일이에요. | Naeireun geumyoirieyo. | Tomorrow is Friday. |
| 생일이 무슨 요일이에요? | Saengiri museun yoirieyo? | What day of the week is your birthday? |

Every day name ends in the consonant ㄹ, so "to be" is always **이에요**, never 예요: 월요일**이에요**, 금요일**이에요**.

To ask for the date instead of the weekday, use **며칠** (*myeochil*): **오늘 며칠이에요?** (*Oneul myeochirieyo?*, "What's the date today?"). It is spelled 며칠, not 몇 일, even though it means "how many days." The answer uses Sino-Korean numbers, which you can review in [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/). A full date goes from largest to smallest, with the weekday last:

**11월 12일 목요일** → 십일월 십이 일 목요일 (*sibirwol sibi il mogyoil*), "Thursday, November 12"

## "On Monday": using 에 with days

To say something happens on a particular day, attach the time particle **에** directly to the day.

| Day + 에 | Romanization | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 월요일에 | woryoire | 월요일에 학교에 가요. | I go to school on Monday. |
| 화요일에 | hwayoire | 화요일에 친구를 만나요. | I meet a friend on Tuesday. |
| 수요일에 | suyoire | 수요일에 운동해요. | I work out on Wednesday. |
| 목요일에 | mogyoire | 목요일에 한국어를 배워요. | I learn Korean on Thursday. |
| 금요일에 | geumyoire | 금요일에 시간 있어요? | Are you free on Friday? |
| 토요일에 | toyoire | 토요일에 영화를 봐요. | I watch a movie on Saturday. |
| 일요일에 | iryoire | 일요일에 집에서 쉬어요. | I rest at home on Sunday. |

One important exception: **오늘** (today), **내일** (tomorrow) and **어제** (yesterday) never take 에. Say 내일 만나요, not 내일에 만나요.

When you want to contrast one day with another, use the topic particle **은**: **토요일은 괜찮아요** (*Toyoireun gwaenchanayo*, "Saturday is fine [but another day isn't]").

## From, until and every

| Pattern | Example | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| -부터 -까지 | 월요일부터 금요일까지 일해요. | Woryoilbuteo geumyoilkkaji ilhaeyo. | I work from Monday to Friday. |
| -마다 | 토요일마다 등산해요. | Toyoilmada deungsanhaeyo. | I go hiking every Saturday. |
| 매주 + day | 매주 수요일에 수업이 있어요. | Maeju suyoire sueobi isseoyo. | I have class every Wednesday. |
| -까지 | 금요일까지 보내 주세요. | Geumyoilkkaji bonae juseyo. | Please send it by Friday. |

The last example uses **-아/어 주세요** from [please in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-please/). Note that 까지 means both "until" and "by (a deadline)."

## This week, next week, last week

Combine the days with these time words to point to a specific week. Note the spacing: **지난주** is written as one word, but **이번 주** and **다음 주** have a space.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이번 주 | ibeon ju | this week |
| 지난주 | jinanju | last week |
| 다음 주 | daeum ju | next week |
| 이번 주 금요일 | ibeon ju geumyoil | this Friday |
| 다음 주 월요일 | daeum ju woryoil | next Monday |
| 지난주 토요일 | jinanju toyoil | last Saturday |
| 주말 | jumal | weekend |
| 이번 주말 | ibeon jumal | this weekend |
| 평일 | pyeongil | weekday |
| 매일 | maeil | every day |
| 매주 | maeju | every week |

And for the days around today:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 그저께 | geujeokke | the day before yesterday |
| 어제 | eoje | yesterday |
| 오늘 | oneul | today |
| 내일 | naeil | tomorrow |
| 모레 | more | the day after tomorrow |

Here they are with the tenses you already know:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 지난주 일요일에 부산에 갔어요. | Jinanju iryoire busane gasseoyo. | I went to Busan last Sunday. |
| 다음 주 화요일에 시험이 있어요. | Daeum ju hwayoire siheomi isseoyo. | I have an exam next Tuesday. |
| 이번 주말에 뭐 할 거예요? | Ibeon jumare mwo hal geoyeyo? | What are you going to do this weekend? |
| 평일에는 바빠요. | Pyeongireneun bappayo. | I'm busy on weekdays. |
| 금요일에 만날까요? | Geumyoire mannalkkayo? | Shall we meet on Friday? |

The first uses [the past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/), the third [the future tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/), and the last [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/).

## Reading days on signs and schedules

On shop doors, menus, bus timetables and booking sites, the days are usually shortened to one syllable, often in parentheses after a date, such as 11.12(목). Ranges use a tilde.

| You see | Read as | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 월~금 09:00~18:00 | 월요일부터 금요일까지 | Open Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. |
| 토·일 휴무 | 토요일, 일요일 휴무 | Closed Saturday and Sunday |
| 매주 월요일 휴무 | 매주 월요일 휴무 | Closed every Monday |
| 정기휴무: 화 | 정기휴무 화요일 | Regular closing day: Tuesday |
| 주말·공휴일 | 주말, 공휴일 | Weekends and public holidays |
| 평일 | 평일 | Weekdays |

**휴무** (*hyumu*) means "closed (day off)," **정기휴무** (*jeonggihyumu*) is a regular weekly closing day, and **공휴일** (*gonghyuil*) is a public holiday. Many museums, palaces and restaurants in Korea close on one fixed weekday, so it is worth checking for 휴무 before you go.

## Everyday expressions with days

Two days of the week appear in common slang:

- **월요병** (*woryobyeong*), literally "Monday sickness," is the Korean version of the Monday blues: 월요병 때문에 피곤해요 (*Woryobyeong ttaemune pigonhaeyo*, "I'm tired because of the Monday blues").
- **불금** (*bulgeum*), short for 불타는 금요일 (*bultaneun geumyoil*), "burning Friday," is the Korean equivalent of TGIF, a Friday night out. You'll hear 오늘 불금이에요! (*Oneul bulgeumieyo!*, "It's Friday night!").

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 오늘 뭐 요일이에요? | 오늘 무슨 요일이에요? | The fixed phrase is 무슨 요일. |
| 월요일예요. | 월요일이에요. | After a final consonant, use 이에요. |
| 내일에 가요. | 내일 가요. | 오늘, 내일 and 어제 don't take 에. |
| 몇 일이에요? | 며칠이에요? | "What date" is always spelled 며칠. |
| 다음주, 이번주 | 다음 주, 이번 주 | Standard spacing; only 지난주 is one word. |

You'll often see 다음주 and 이번주 without a space in casual texts and social media, so don't be surprised by it, but the spaced forms are standard.

## FAQ

**What does 요일 mean?** 요일 (*yoil*) means "day of the week." The first syllable tells you which day: 월 (moon), 화 (fire), 수 (water), 목 (wood), 금 (metal), 토 (earth), 일 (sun). On its own, 요일 appears in the question 무슨 요일이에요?

**Does the Korean week start on Sunday or Monday?** Both are used. Schedules, timetables and most spoken lists start with 월요일 (Monday), while many printed wall calendars put 일요일 (Sunday) in the first column, often in red.

**How do I say "weekend" and "weekday" in Korean?** The weekend is **주말** (*jumal*) and a weekday is **평일** (*pyeongil*). Add 에 to say "on weekends" or "on weekdays": 주말에 (*jumare*), 평일에 (*pyeongire*).

**Is it 일요일 or 일욜?** 일요일 is the standard word. In casual texting you may see shortened forms like 일욜 or 월욜, but use the full forms when you speak or write politely.

**How do I say "Monday morning" or "Friday night"?** Put the time of day after the day: 월요일 아침 (*woryoil achim*, Monday morning), 금요일 저녁 (*geumyoil jeonyeok*, Friday evening), 토요일 밤 (*toyoil bam*, Saturday night). Then add 에 if needed: 금요일 저녁에 만나요 (*Geumyoil jeonyeoge mannayo*, "Let's meet Friday evening").

**What's next?** Combine days with clock times from [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/) to make full plans, such as 토요일 오후 세 시에 만날까요? (*Toyoil ohu se sie mannalkkayo?*, "Shall we meet at 3 p.m. on Saturday?"), and use [question words](/learn-korean/en/korean-question-words/) like 언제 and 몇 시 to ask others about their schedule.
