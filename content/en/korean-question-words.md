Once you can make a basic polite sentence, question words let you start real conversations: asking someone's name, where the restroom is, how much something costs, or what time it is. Korean has a small set of them, and the good news is that they are easy to use: you simply put the question word where the answer would go.

> **Quick answer:** The core Korean question words are **뭐** (*mwo*, what), **어디** (*eodi*, where), **언제** (*eonje*, when), **누구** (*nugu*, who), **왜** (*wae*, why), **어떻게** (*eotteoke*, how), **얼마** (*eolma*, how much) and **몇** (*myeot*, how many). Put the question word where the answer would be and keep a polite ending: **이름이 뭐예요?** (*Ireumi mwoyeyo?*, "What's your name?"), **화장실이 어디예요?** (*Hwajangsiri eodiyeyo?*, "Where is the restroom?"), **이거 얼마예요?** (*Igeo eolmayeyo?*, "How much is this?"). Before a noun, use **무슨** (what kind of), **어느** (which) or **어떤** (what sort of).

This lesson uses the polite -아/어요 style from [the present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/) and the particles from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## The core question words at a glance

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 뭐 / 무엇 | mwo / mueot | what | 뭐 해요? | What are you doing? |
| 어디 | eodi | where | 어디 가요? | Where are you going? |
| 언제 | eonje | when | 언제 와요? | When are you coming? |
| 누구 / 누가 | nugu / nuga | who | 누구예요? | Who is it? |
| 왜 | wae | why | 왜 늦었어요? | Why were you late? |
| 어떻게 | eotteoke | how | 어떻게 가요? | How do I get there? |
| 얼마 | eolma | how much (price) | 얼마예요? | How much is it? |
| 얼마나 | eolmana | how long, how much (degree) | 얼마나 걸려요? | How long does it take? |
| 몇 | myeot | how many (+ counter) | 몇 살이에요? | How old are you? |

## Word order: the question word stays in place

English moves question words to the front of the sentence ("Where are you going?"). Korean does not. Take the statement, replace the part you are asking about with a question word, and keep the same ending.

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| 학교에 가요. (Hakgyoe gayo.) I'm going to school. | 어디에 가요? (Eodie gayo?) Where are you going? |
| 김밥을 먹어요. (Gimbabeul meogeoyo.) I'm eating gimbap. | 뭐 먹어요? (Mwo meogeoyo?) What are you eating? |
| 내일 와요. (Naeil wayo.) I'm coming tomorrow. | 언제 와요? (Eonje wayo?) When are you coming? |
| 민수가 왔어요. (Minsuga wasseoyo.) Minsu came. | 누가 왔어요? (Nuga wasseoyo?) Who came? |

In speech, particles after question words are often dropped: 어디 가요? is more common than 어디에 가요?, and 뭐 먹어요? is more common than 뭘 먹어요?

When the question word is followed directly by "to be," attach **예요**, since all of these end in a vowel: 뭐**예요**, 어디**예요**, 누구**예요**, 언제**예요**, 얼마**예요**. After a consonant, use **이에요**: 몇 살**이에요**?

## 뭐 (what)

**뭐** (*mwo*) is the everyday spoken form of **무엇** (*mueot*). 무엇 sounds formal or written, so use 뭐 in conversation. With the object particle, 무엇을 shortens to **뭘** (*mwol*), and with the subject particle, 무엇이 becomes **뭐가** (*mwoga*).

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이름이 뭐예요? | Ireumi mwoyeyo? | What's your name? |
| 이거 뭐예요? | Igeo mwoyeyo? | What is this? |
| 이거 한국어로 뭐예요? | Igeo hangugeoro mwoyeyo? | What is this in Korean? |
| 주말에 뭐 했어요? | Jumare mwo haesseoyo? | What did you do on the weekend? |
| 뭐 먹고 싶어요? | Mwo meokgo sipeoyo? | What do you want to eat? |
| 뭐가 제일 맛있어요? | Mwoga jeil masisseoyo? | What is the most delicious (here)? |

The fifth example combines 뭐 with [-고 싶어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/). The last one is a handy question to ask restaurant staff.

## 어디 (where)

Remember that Korean uses different particles for "being somewhere" and "doing something somewhere": **에** for location and destination, **에서** for where an action happens or where you come from.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 화장실이 어디예요? | Hwajangsiri eodiyeyo? | Where is the restroom? |
| 출구가 어디예요? | Chulguga eodiyeyo? | Where is the exit? |
| 어디 가요? | Eodi gayo? | Where are you going? |
| 어디 살아요? | Eodi sarayo? | Where do you live? |
| 어디에서 왔어요? | Eodieseo wasseoyo? | Where are you from? |
| 어디에서 기다릴까요? | Eodieseo gidarilkkayo? | Where should we wait? |

어디에서 왔어요? literally asks "Where did you come from?" and is the standard way to ask someone's home country. You can answer 미국에서 왔어요 ("I'm from the US"). On the phone, 어디세요? (*eodiseyo?*) politely asks "Where are you?"

## 언제 (when) and 몇 시 (what time)

**언제** asks about a day, date or general time. For clock time, use **몇 시** (*myeot si*, "what hour").

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 생일이 언제예요? | Saengiri eonjeyeyo? | When is your birthday? |
| 언제 시간 있어요? | Eonje sigan isseoyo? | When are you free? |
| 언제 한국에 와요? | Eonje hanguge wayo? | When are you coming to Korea? |
| 지금 몇 시예요? | Jigeum myeot siyeyo? | What time is it now? |
| 몇 시에 만날까요? | Myeot sie mannalkkayo? | What time should we meet? |

Note that 언제 does not take the time particle 에 (언제에 is wrong), but 몇 시 does: 몇 시**에**. Answers use the time-telling numbers from [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/).

## 누구 and 누가 (who)

**누구** (*nugu*) means "who." When "who" is the subject of the sentence, 누구 + 가 contracts to **누가** (*nuga*). Never say 누구가.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 누구예요? | Nuguyeyo? | Who is it? / Who is that? |
| 누구세요? | Nuguseyo? | Who is it? (politely, e.g. at the door) |
| 누가 했어요? | Nuga haesseoyo? | Who did it? |
| 누구를 기다려요? | Nugureul gidaryeoyo? | Who are you waiting for? |
| 누구랑 가요? | Nugurang gayo? | Who are you going with? |
| 이거 누구 거예요? | Igeo nugu geoyeyo? | Whose is this? |

As the last line shows, "whose" is simply 누구 placed before the noun (누구 거 = "whose thing," 누구 가방 = "whose bag").

## 왜 (why)

**왜** (*wae*) asks for a reason. On its own, **왜요?** (*waeyo?*) means "Why?" but it is also a common way to say "What is it?" when someone calls you.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 왜 한국어를 공부해요? | Wae hangugeoreul gongbuhaeyo? | Why do you study Korean? |
| 왜 늦었어요? | Wae neujeosseoyo? | Why were you late? |
| 왜 안 먹어요? | Wae an meogeoyo? | Why aren't you eating? |
| 왜요? | Waeyo? | Why? / What's up? |

A bare 왜? said sharply to an older person can sound blunt, so keep the 요.

## 어떻게 and 어때요 (how)

**어떻게** (*eotteoke*) asks how something is done. **어때요?** (*eottaeyo?*), from the same verb 어떻다, asks "How is it?" about a state or opinion. Learners often swap them.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 명동에 어떻게 가요? | Myeongdonge eotteoke gayo? | How do I get to Myeongdong? |
| 이거 어떻게 먹어요? | Igeo eotteoke meogeoyo? | How do you eat this? |
| 한국어로 어떻게 말해요? | Hangugeoro eotteoke malhaeyo? | How do you say it in Korean? |
| 맛이 어때요? | Masi eottaeyo? | How does it taste? |
| 내일 어때요? | Naeil eottaeyo? | How about tomorrow? |

## 얼마, 얼마나 and 몇 (how much, how many)

- **얼마** asks about a price or amount of money: **이거 얼마예요?**
- **얼마나** asks "how much / how long / how far" as a degree: **얼마나 걸려요?** ("How long does it take?")
- **몇** asks "how many" and is always followed by a counter.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 얼마예요? | Igeo eolmayeyo? | How much is this? |
| 전부 얼마예요? | Jeonbu eolmayeyo? | How much is it altogether? |
| 시간이 얼마나 걸려요? | Sigani eolmana geollyeoyo? | How long does it take? |
| 몇 살이에요? | Myeot sarieyo? | How old are you? |
| 몇 개 있어요? | Myeot gae isseoyo? | How many are there? |
| 몇 분이세요? | Myeot buniseyo? | How many people? (staff to guests) |
| 몇 번 버스예요? | Myeot beon beoseuyeyo? | Which bus number is it? |

Because 몇 asks for a number, the answer uses the same counter: 몇 개? → 두 개. 몇 살? → 스무 살.

## 무슨, 어느, 어떤: question words before nouns

These three all come before a noun and translate as "what" or "which," but they are not interchangeable.

| Word | Romanization | Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 무슨 | museun | what (kind of), asking for identity | 무슨 요일이에요? | What day of the week is it? |
| 어느 | eoneu | which (choosing from a set) | 어느 나라 사람이에요? | Which country are you from? |
| 어떤 | eotteon | what sort of (asking about qualities) | 어떤 음식을 좋아해요? | What kind of food do you like? |

More useful phrases:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 무슨 뜻이에요? | Museun tteusieyo? | What does it mean? |
| 무슨 일이에요? | Museun irieyo? | What's going on? / What's the matter? |
| 어느 쪽이에요? | Eoneu jjogieyo? | Which way is it? |
| 어떤 사람이에요? | Eotteon saramieyo? | What kind of person are they? |

## Question words can also mean "something" or "somewhere"

In casual speech, the same words can mean "something," "someone," "somewhere" or "sometime" in a yes/no question. Context and intonation tell you which meaning is intended.

| Korean | As a question-word question | As a yes/no question |
|---|---|---|
| 어디 가요? | Where are you going? | Are you going somewhere? |
| 뭐 먹을래요? | What do you want to eat? | Do you want to eat something? |
| 누가 왔어요? | Who came? | Did someone come? |

When you want to be unmistakable, use the dedicated forms **뭔가** (something), **어딘가** (somewhere) and **누군가** (someone).

## Politeness levels

| Style | "Where are you going?" | "What is this?" | When it's used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 어디 가? (eodi ga?) | 이거 뭐야? (igeo mwoya?) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 어디 가요? (eodi gayo?) | 이거 뭐예요? (igeo mwoyeyo?) | the safe default |
| Formal | 어디 가십니까? (eodi gasimnikka?) | 이것은 무엇입니까? (igeoseun mueosimnikka?) | announcements, interviews, very formal settings |

For more on choosing a level, see [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 뭐 이름이에요? | 이름이 뭐예요? | the question word goes where the answer goes |
| 누구가 왔어요? | 누가 왔어요? | 누구 + 가 contracts to 누가 |
| 어디에 왔어요? (from where) | 어디에서 왔어요? | origin takes 에서 |
| 언제에 와요? | 언제 와요? | 언제 takes no 에 |
| 이거 몇이에요? (price) | 이거 얼마예요? | 몇 needs a counter; prices use 얼마 |
| 날씨가 어떻게예요? | 날씨가 어때요? | asking about a state uses 어때요 |

## FAQ

**What is the difference between 뭐 and 무엇?** They mean the same thing. 무엇 is the full form used in writing and formal speech (무엇입니까?), while 뭐 is the natural spoken form. In conversation, use 뭐.

**When do I use 누가 instead of 누구?** Use 누가 when "who" is the subject doing the action (누가 했어요?, "Who did it?"). Use 누구 everywhere else: before 예요, before other particles like 를 and 랑, and before a noun to mean "whose."

**What is the difference between 무슨 and 어떤?** 무슨 asks what something is (무슨 영화?, "which movie / what movie?"), while 어떤 asks what it is like (어떤 영화?, "what kind of movie?"). In everyday speech they overlap a lot, but fixed phrases such as 무슨 요일 and 무슨 뜻 always use 무슨.

**Do Korean questions need a question mark or a special ending?** In the polite style, a question uses the same -아/어요 ending as a statement. The rising intonation in speech, or the question mark in writing, makes it a question. Only the formal style has a separate question ending, -습니까/-ㅂ니까.

**What's next?** Use question words to make plans with [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/) (어디에서 만날까요?, "Where should we meet?"), and to ask for help politely with [주세요](/learn-korean/en/korean-please/). If you meet someone new, start with the phrases in [Korean greetings](/learn-korean/en/korean-greetings/) and follow up with 이름이 뭐예요? and 어디에서 왔어요?
