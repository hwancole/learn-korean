Korean has no single word that you can tack onto any sentence the way English uses "please." Instead, politeness is built into the verb. The workhorse is **주세요** (*juseyo*), the polite request form of 주다, "to give." Once you know it, you can order food, ask for directions, get a taxi to stop, and ask people to slow down when they speak.

> **Quick answer:** To ask for a **thing**, say the noun + **주세요**: **물 주세요** (*mul juseyo*, "Water, please"), **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*, "This one, please"). To ask someone to **do** something, add **-아/어 주세요** to the verb: 기다리다 → **기다려 주세요** (*gidaryeo juseyo*, "Please wait"), 도와주다 → **도와주세요** (*dowajuseyo*, "Please help me"). Add **좀** (*jom*) to soften it: **천천히 좀 말해 주세요** ("Could you speak a bit slowly?"). For "please don't", use **-지 마세요**: **가지 마세요** (*gaji maseyo*, "Please don't go").

The -아/어 part follows exactly the same vowel rule as the polite [present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), so if you can say 가요, 먹어요 and 해요, you can already make requests. Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## Asking for things: noun + 주세요

주세요 literally means "please give (me)." Put the thing you want in front of it. In everyday speech the object particle 을/를 is usually dropped, so 물을 주세요 and 물 주세요 are both correct, but 물 주세요 is what you will hear most.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 물 주세요. | Mul juseyo. | Water, please. |
| 이거 주세요. | Igeo juseyo. | This one, please. |
| 메뉴판 좀 주세요. | Menyupan jom juseyo. | Can I have the menu, please? |
| 영수증 주세요. | Yeongsujeung juseyo. | The receipt, please. |
| 봉투 주세요. | Bongtu juseyo. | A bag, please. |
| 아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요. | Aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo. | One iced americano, please. |
| 김밥 두 줄 주세요. | Gimbap du jul juseyo. | Two rolls of gimbap, please. |
| 비빔밥 하나랑 김치찌개 하나 주세요. | Bibimbap hanarang gimchijjigae hana juseyo. | One bibimbap and one kimchi stew, please. |

To say how many, put a **native Korean number** and a counter after the noun: 한 잔 (one cup), 두 개 (two items), 세 명 (three people). The numbers change shape before a counter (하나 → 한, 둘 → 두, 셋 → 세), which is explained in [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/). In restaurants, people also simply use 하나, 둘 without a counter, as in the last example.

To get a server's attention first, say **저기요** (*jeogiyo*, "Excuse me") or **여기요** (*yeogiyo*, "Over here"), then your request.

## Asking for actions: -아/어 주세요

When you want someone to do something for you, use the verb in its -아/어 form plus 주세요. The literal meaning is "please give me the favor of doing it."

| Last vowel of the stem | Add | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ㅏ or ㅗ | -아 주세요 | 닫다 → 닫아 주세요 |
| any other vowel | -어 주세요 | 열다 → 열어 주세요 |
| 하다 verbs | 해 주세요 | 말하다 → 말해 주세요 |

The easiest shortcut: take the polite present-tense form, drop the final 요, and add **주세요**. 기다려요 → 기다려 + 주세요 → **기다려 주세요**. This handles contractions and irregular verbs automatically.

| Verb | -아/어 주세요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 기다리다 (to wait) | 기다려 주세요 | gidaryeo juseyo | Please wait. |
| 도와주다 (to help) | 도와주세요 | dowajuseyo | Please help me. |
| 말하다 (to speak) | 말해 주세요 | malhae juseyo | Please tell me / say it. |
| 보여 주다 (to show) | 보여 주세요 | boyeo juseyo | Please show me. |
| 가다 (to go) | 가 주세요 | ga juseyo | Please go (to...). |
| 오다 (to come) | 와 주세요 | wa juseyo | Please come. |
| 쓰다 (to write) | 써 주세요 | sseo juseyo | Please write it down. |
| 찍다 (to take a photo) | 찍어 주세요 | jjigeo juseyo | Please take (a photo). |
| 열다 (to open) | 열어 주세요 | yeoreo juseyo | Please open it. |
| 세우다 (to stop a car) | 세워 주세요 | sewo juseyo | Please stop (here). |
| 바꾸다 (to change) | 바꿔 주세요 | bakkwo juseyo | Please change / exchange it. |
| 깎다 (to cut, lower a price) | 깎아 주세요 | kkakka juseyo | Please give me a discount. |
| 알리다 (to let know) | 알려 주세요 | allyeo juseyo | Please let me know. |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 들어 주세요 | deureo juseyo | Please listen. |
| 포장하다 (to pack) | 포장해 주세요 | pojanghae juseyo | To go, please. |

Note 듣다 → 들어 주세요: because -어 starts with a vowel, the ㄷ irregular change happens here, just as in the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) (들었어요).

On spacing: the standard spelling puts a space before 주세요 (기다려 주세요), although writing it attached (기다려주세요) is also accepted and very common online. 도와주세요 is always written as one word because 도와주다 is listed as a single verb in the dictionary.

## Survival requests by situation

These are the requests travelers and beginners use most. The word **좀** ("a little") appears often: it softens a request, a bit like adding "could you" in English.

| Situation | Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language | 천천히 말해 주세요. | Cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo. | Please speak slowly. |
| Language | 다시 한 번 말해 주세요. | Dasi han beon malhae juseyo. | Please say that again. |
| Language | 여기에 좀 써 주세요. | Yeogie jom sseo juseyo. | Could you write it here? |
| Taxi | 이 주소로 가 주세요. | I jusoro ga juseyo. | Please take me to this address. |
| Taxi | 여기서 세워 주세요. | Yeogiseo sewo juseyo. | Please stop here. |
| Restaurant | 덜 맵게 해 주세요. | Deol maepge hae juseyo. | Please make it less spicy. |
| Restaurant | 포장해 주세요. | Pojanghae juseyo. | To go, please. |
| Restaurant | 계산서 주세요. | Gyesanseo juseyo. | The bill, please. |
| Shopping | 다른 색 보여 주세요. | Dareun saek boyeo juseyo. | Please show me another color. |
| Shopping | 좀 깎아 주세요. | Jom kkakka juseyo. | Could you give me a discount? |
| Sightseeing | 사진 좀 찍어 주세요. | Sajin jom jjigeo juseyo. | Could you take a photo of us? |
| Emergency | 도와주세요! | Dowajuseyo! | Help me, please! |

Asking for a discount is normal in traditional markets but not in department stores or chain shops with fixed prices.

## 주세요 vs -(으)세요: two kinds of "please"

Beginners often mix up **-아/어 주세요** and **-(으)세요**. Both are polite, but they point in different directions.

- **-아/어 주세요** asks someone to do something **for you** (a favor).
- **-(으)세요** politely tells someone to do something that is **for their own sake** or is simply an instruction. Add **-으세요** after a consonant and **-세요** after a vowel.

| Korean | Romanization | Who benefits | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 앉으세요. | Anjeuseyo. | the listener | Please have a seat. (host to guest) |
| 앉아 주세요. | Anja juseyo. | the speaker | Please sit down. (e.g. a guide asking a group) |
| 들어오세요. | Deureooseyo. | the listener | Please come in. |
| 맛있게 드세요. | Masitge deuseyo. | the listener | Enjoy your meal. |
| 기다려 주세요. | Gidaryeo juseyo. | the speaker | Please wait (for me). |

So when a host says 앉으세요, they are offering you a seat. If you said 앉아 주세요 to a guest, it would sound like you needed them to sit for your benefit. The goodbye 안녕히 가세요 from [Korean greetings](/learn-korean/en/korean-greetings/) uses the same -(으)세요 ending.

## Softer and more polite requests

주세요 is polite and safe in nearly every situation. When you are asking a bigger favor or speaking to a stranger, older person or staff member, a question form sounds gentler.

| Korean | Romanization | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| 사진 좀 찍어 주세요. | Sajin jom jjigeo juseyo. | polite request |
| 사진 좀 찍어 주실 수 있어요? | Sajin jom jjigeo jusil su isseoyo? | "Could you...?" |
| 사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요? | Sajin jom jjigeo jusigesseoyo? | "Would you mind...?" (very polite) |
| 잠시만 기다려 주십시오. | Jamsiman gidaryeo jusipsio. | formal, used by staff and announcements |

The second line combines this lesson with [-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-can-cant/): 주시다 is the honorific form of 주다, so 주실 수 있어요 means "would you be able to give (me the favor)." The -시- shows respect to the listener, as explained in [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

You will also hear **부탁해요** (*butakaeyo*) and the more polite **부탁드려요** (*butakdeuryeoyo*), "I'm asking you a favor." People often say it after a request, or alone when handing something over: 잘 부탁드립니다 (*Jal butakdeurimnida*) is a set phrase meaning roughly "I'm counting on you" or "I look forward to working with you."

## "Please don't": -지 마세요

To ask someone not to do something, do not put 안 in front of 주세요. Instead, add **-지 마세요** directly to the verb stem. It comes from 말다, "to stop, not do."

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 가지 마세요. | Gaji maseyo. | Please don't go. |
| 걱정하지 마세요. | Geokjeonghaji maseyo. | Please don't worry. |
| 만지지 마세요. | Manjiji maseyo. | Please don't touch. |
| 늦지 마세요. | Neutji maseyo. | Please don't be late. |
| 사진 찍지 말아 주세요. | Sajin jjikji mara juseyo. | Please don't take photos. |

**-지 말아 주세요** (the last line) is a softer, request-style version that you often see on signs in shops and museums. For other negative patterns, see [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/).

## Politeness levels

| Style | Water, please / Please wait | When it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 물 줘 / 기다려 줘 (mul jwo / gidaryeo jwo) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 물 주세요 / 기다려 주세요 (mul juseyo / gidaryeo juseyo) | the safe default |
| Formal | 물 주십시오 / 기다려 주십시오 (mul jusipsio / gidaryeo jusipsio) | announcements, very formal settings |

When an older person or a customer is the one receiving, Koreans switch 주다 for the humble verb **드리다**: 제가 도와**드릴게요** (*Jega dowadeurilgeyo*, "I'll help you"). Use 주세요 when you are the one receiving the favor.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 기다리 주세요 | 기다려 주세요 | keep the -아/어 form (기다려) |
| 듣어 주세요 | 들어 주세요 | ㄷ irregular before a vowel |
| 도와 줘세요 | 도와주세요 | 주 + 세요 = 주세요 |
| 안 가 주세요 (for "please don't go") | 가지 마세요 | "please don't" uses -지 마세요 |
| 앉아 주세요 (to offer a guest a seat) | 앉으세요 | offering, not asking a favor |
| 제발 물 주세요 (for an ordinary "please") | 물 좀 주세요 | 제발 sounds like begging |

## FAQ

**Is there a word for "please" in Korean?** Not one that works like English "please." The closest single word, **제발** (*jebal*), is used for pleading ("please, I'm begging you") and sounds dramatic in ordinary requests. Everyday politeness comes from 주세요, 좀, and the polite verb endings.

**What does 주세요 mean literally?** It is 주다 ("to give") with the honorific -시- and the polite ending -어요, so it means "please give (me)." After another verb, it means "please do (it) for me."

**Should I say 물 주세요 or 물을 주세요?** Both are grammatical. In speech, the particle is usually dropped, so 물 주세요 sounds more natural. For how particles work, see [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/).

**What is the difference between 주세요 and 주시겠어요?** 주세요 is a polite request. 주시겠어요? turns it into a question ("Would you...?"), which is gentler and suits strangers, older people, or bigger favors.

**What's next?** Use 주세요 with what you learned in [-고 싶어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/) and [-아야/어야 해요](/learn-korean/en/korean-have-to/) to handle full exchanges: 이거 먹고 싶어요. 하나 주세요. ("I want to eat this. One, please.") Or suggest doing something together with [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/).
