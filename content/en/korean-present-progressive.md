When a friend texts **지금 뭐 해요?** ("What are you doing now?"), you need a way to say what is happening at this moment: "I'm eating", "I'm watching a drama", "I'm on my way". In Korean, the go-to pattern for "-ing" actions is **-고 있어요**.

> **Quick answer:** Drop **-다** from any action verb and add **-고 있어요**: 먹다 → **먹고 있어요** (*meokgo isseoyo*, "I'm eating"), 공부하다 → **공부하고 있어요** (*gongbuhago isseoyo*, "I'm studying"). There is no vowel/consonant rule and no irregular verbs: the stem never changes. For the past, use **-고 있었어요** ("I was eating"). For someone you show respect to, use **-고 계세요** (할머니가 주무시고 계세요, "Grandma is sleeping"). Write it with a space: 먹고 있어요, not 먹고있어요.

This lesson builds on the [present tense -아요/-어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) and the [future tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/). The object marker 을/를 from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/) shows up in many examples. Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## How to form -고 있어요

The formula is the same for every action verb:

**verb stem + 고 + 있어요**

있어요 is the polite present form of 있다 ("to be, to exist"), so the pattern literally means "I am (in the state of) doing".

| Verb | Stem | -고 있어요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 먹다 (to eat) | 먹 | 먹고 있어요 | meokgo isseoyo | I'm eating |
| 가다 (to go) | 가 | 가고 있어요 | gago isseoyo | I'm going / on my way |
| 보다 (to watch) | 보 | 보고 있어요 | bogo isseoyo | I'm watching |
| 하다 (to do) | 하 | 하고 있어요 | hago isseoyo | I'm doing it |
| 공부하다 (to study) | 공부하 | 공부하고 있어요 | gongbuhago isseoyo | I'm studying |
| 일하다 (to work) | 일하 | 일하고 있어요 | ilhago isseoyo | I'm working |
| 기다리다 (to wait) | 기다리 | 기다리고 있어요 | gidarigo isseoyo | I'm waiting |
| 자다 (to sleep) | 자 | 자고 있어요 | jago isseoyo | I'm sleeping |
| 읽다 (to read) | 읽 | 읽고 있어요 | ilgo isseoyo | I'm reading |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 듣 | 듣고 있어요 | deutgo isseoyo | I'm listening |
| 만들다 (to make) | 만들 | 만들고 있어요 | mandeulgo isseoyo | I'm making it |
| 찾다 (to look for) | 찾 | 찾고 있어요 | chatgo isseoyo | I'm looking for it |

Because -고 starts with a consonant, irregular verbs keep their dictionary stem: 듣다 stays **듣고** (not 들고), and 돕다 stays **돕고** (not 도와). That makes -고 있어요 one of the easiest endings in Korean.

### Pronunciation tip

The ㄱ in 고 usually sounds tense after a consonant: 먹고 is pronounced [먹꼬] (*meok-kko*) and 읽고 is [일꼬] (*il-kko*). Revised Romanization doesn't show this tensing, which is why it's written *meokgo* and *ilgo*. 있어요 is pronounced [이써요] (*i-sseo-yo*).

## Using it in sentences

Put the object, place or time before the verb as usual.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 지금 밥을 먹고 있어요. | Jigeum babeul meokgo isseoyo. | I'm eating right now. |
| 친구를 기다리고 있어요. | Chingureul gidarigo isseoyo. | I'm waiting for a friend. |
| 드라마를 보고 있어요. | Deuramareul bogo isseoyo. | I'm watching a drama. |
| 지금 집에 가고 있어요. | Jigeum jibe gago isseoyo. | I'm on my way home now. |
| 동생이 자고 있어요. | Dongsaengi jago isseoyo. | My younger sibling is sleeping. |
| 밖에 비가 오고 있어요. | Bakke biga ogo isseoyo. | It's raining outside. |
| 음악을 듣고 있어요. | Eumageul deutgo isseoyo. | I'm listening to music. |

## -고 있어요 vs the simple present

In Korean, the plain present tense can already describe what is happening now: **뭐 해요?** and **밥 먹어요** are perfectly natural answers in conversation. So when do you need -고 있어요?

| Simple present | -고 있어요 | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| 밥 먹어요. | 밥 먹고 있어요. | Both can mean "I'm eating"; -고 있어요 stresses that it's in progress right now. |
| 한국어를 배워요. | 한국어를 배우고 있어요. | "I learn Korean" vs "I'm (currently) learning Korean". |
| 서울에 살아요. | 서울에 살고 있어요. | "I live in Seoul" vs "I'm living in Seoul (these days)". |

Use -고 있어요 when you want to make the ongoing nature clear, especially with **지금** ("now") or **요즘** ("these days"). For a long-term ongoing activity, it works just like English "I'm learning": **요즘 한국어를 배우고 있어요** (*Yojeum hangugeoreul baeugo isseoyo*), "I'm learning Korean these days."

## Past and future progressive

Change 있어요 into the past or future and the rest stays the same.

| Tense | Form | Example | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present | -고 있어요 | 자고 있어요 | jago isseoyo | I'm sleeping |
| Past | -고 있었어요 | 자고 있었어요 | jago isseosseoyo | I was sleeping |
| Future / guess | -고 있을 거예요 | 자고 있을 거예요 | jago isseul geoyeyo | I'll be sleeping / (they're) probably sleeping |

A very common exchange: **아까 전화했는데 뭐 하고 있었어요?** (*Akka jeonhwahaenneunde mwo hago isseosseoyo?*), "I called earlier, what were you doing?" Answer: **샤워하고 있었어요** (*Syawohago isseosseoyo*), "I was taking a shower."

The future form also makes a guess about right now: **민수 씨는 지금 일하고 있을 거예요** (*Minsu ssineun jigeum ilhago isseul geoyeyo*), "Minsu is probably working right now."

## Negative: "I'm not doing"

There are two ways, as in [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/).

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 아직 안 자고 있어요. | Ajik an jago isseoyo. | I'm still not sleeping. (I'm still up.) | 안 before the main verb, conversational |
| 지금은 일하고 있지 않아요. | Jigeumeun ilhago itji anayo. | I'm not working right now. | -지 않아요 on 있다, a bit more formal |

## Honorific: -고 계세요

When the person doing the action is someone you respect (a parent, a teacher, a customer), replace 있어요 with **계세요**, the honorific form of 있다.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 선생님이 수업하고 계세요. | Seonsaengnimi sueophago gyeseyo. | The teacher is teaching a class. |
| 할머니가 주무시고 계세요. | Halmeoniga jumusigo gyeseyo. | Grandma is sleeping. |
| 사장님은 지금 회의하고 계세요. | Sajangnimeun jigeum hoeuihago gyeseyo. | The boss is in a meeting now. |
| 지금 뭐 하고 계세요? | Jigeum mwo hago gyeseyo? | What are you doing now? (to an elder) |

Notice that 자다 becomes its honorific verb **주무시다** too. Never use 계세요 about yourself: say **저는 일하고 있어요**, not 저는 일하고 계세요.

## Politeness levels

| Style | I'm eating | When it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 먹고 있어 (meokgo isseo) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 먹고 있어요 (meokgo isseoyo) | the safe default |
| Formal | 먹고 있습니다 (meokgo itseumnida) | announcements, presentations, the workplace |

For when to choose each one, see [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

## Wearing clothes: 입고 있어요

With verbs of putting on clothes and accessories, -고 있어요 usually describes what someone **is wearing**, not the act of getting dressed. Korean uses a different verb depending on the item.

| Item | Verb | Example | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clothes | 입다 | 코트를 입고 있어요. | Koteureul ipgo isseoyo. | (She) is wearing a coat. |
| shoes, socks | 신다 | 운동화를 신고 있어요. | Undonghwareul singo isseoyo. | (He) is wearing sneakers. |
| hat, glasses | 쓰다 | 안경을 쓰고 있어요. | Angyeongeul sseugo isseoyo. | (He) is wearing glasses. |
| ring, gloves | 끼다 | 반지를 끼고 있어요. | Banjireul kkigo isseoyo. | (She) is wearing a ring. |
| watch | 차다 | 시계를 차고 있어요. | Sigyereul chago isseoyo. | (He) is wearing a watch. |

## -고 있어요 vs -아/어 있어요

Learners often mix these up. **-고 있어요** is an action in progress. **-아/어 있어요** is a state that remains after an action is finished, used with verbs like 앉다 ("to sit down"), 서다 ("to stand up") and 열리다 ("to be opened").

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 의자에 앉아 있어요. | Uijae anja isseoyo. | (He) is sitting on the chair. |
| 문 앞에 서 있어요. | Mun ape seo isseoyo. | (She) is standing in front of the door. |
| 창문이 열려 있어요. | Changmuni yeollyeo isseoyo. | The window is open. |

So "I'm sitting" is **앉아 있어요**, not 앉고 있어요.

## Another option: -는 중이에요

**-는 중이에요** means "I'm in the middle of doing" and sounds a little more like a status update. With 하다 nouns you can also just say the noun + **중이에요**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 지금 공부하는 중이에요. | Jigeum gongbuhaneun jungieyo. | I'm in the middle of studying. |
| 회의 중이에요. | Hoeui jungieyo. | (I'm) in a meeting. |
| 통화 중이에요. | Tonghwa jungieyo. | (I'm) on the phone. / The line is busy. |

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 먹고있어요 | 먹고 있어요 | 있어요 is written as a separate word |
| 들고 있어요 (for "listening") | 듣고 있어요 | no irregular change before -고 (들고 있어요 means "holding") |
| 예쁘고 있어요 | 예뻐요 | adjectives don't take -고 있어요 |
| 앉고 있어요 (for "I'm seated") | 앉아 있어요 | a resulting state uses -아/어 있어요 |
| 저는 일하고 계세요 | 저는 일하고 있어요 | never use honorifics about yourself |

## FAQ

**Is 먹어요 wrong for "I'm eating"?** No. In conversation 지금 밥 먹어요 is completely natural. -고 있어요 simply makes it explicit that the action is in progress, so it's the clearer choice when you want to stress "right now".

**Does -고 있어요 work with every verb?** It works with action verbs. It doesn't work with adjectives (예쁘다, 춥다, 바쁘다), and for verbs like 앉다 and 서다 the "-ing" meaning usually comes from -아/어 있어요 instead.

**Why do I hear 알고 있어요?** 알다 means "to know", and **알고 있어요** (*algo isseoyo*) is a very common way to say "I know" or "I'm aware". It doesn't mean "I'm knowing"; here -고 있어요 just emphasizes that the knowledge is ongoing. 사랑하고 있어요 ("I'm in love with you") in songs works the same way.

**Is it 있어요 or 있습니다?** Both are polite. 있어요 is the everyday polite form; 있습니다 is the formal form you'll hear in news, announcements and business settings.

**What does 가고 있어요 mean in a text?** It's the standard "I'm on my way". If you're nearly there, say **거의 다 왔어요** (*Geoui da wasseoyo*), "I'm almost there."

**What's next?** Combine -고 있어요 with other endings you know: [-고 싶어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/) ("want to") uses the same -고, and [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/) lets you suggest the next thing to do together.
