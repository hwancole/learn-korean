Once you can talk about the present and the past, the next thing you need is plans: what you'll do at the weekend, where you're going on holiday, when you'll call someone back. In everyday Korean, almost all of that is done with one ending, **-(으)ㄹ 거예요**, and it's easier than the present and past tenses because there's no vowel harmony to think about.

> **Quick answer:** Drop **-다** to get the stem. If the stem ends in a **vowel**, add **-ㄹ 거예요** (*-l geoyeyo*): 가다 → **갈 거예요** ("I will go"). If it ends in a **consonant**, add **-을 거예요** (*-eul geoyeyo*): 먹다 → **먹을 거예요** ("I will eat"). If it already ends in **ㄹ**, just add **거예요**: 살다 → **살 거예요**. Every 하다 verb becomes **할 거예요**. The same ending also means "probably": 비가 올 거예요 = "It will probably rain."

This lesson follows [Korean present tense -아요/-어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/) and [Korean past tense -았어요/-었어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/), and the example sentences use the particles from [Korean particles 은/는, 이/가, 을/를](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## The rule: vowel or consonant?

Unlike the present and past tenses, the future ending doesn't care whether the last vowel is ㅏ/ㅗ or something else. The only question is how the stem ends.

| How the stem ends | Ending | Example | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| a vowel | -ㄹ 거예요 (l geoyeyo) | 가다 (to go) | 갈 거예요 (gal geoyeyo) |
| a consonant | -을 거예요 (eul geoyeyo) | 먹다 (to eat) | 먹을 거예요 (meogeul geoyeyo) |
| ㄹ | 거예요 (geoyeyo) | 살다 (to live) | 살 거예요 (sal geoyeyo) |
| 하다 verbs | 할 거예요 (hal geoyeyo) | 공부하다 (to study) | 공부할 거예요 (gongbuhal geoyeyo) |

With a vowel stem, the ㄹ is written as a final consonant (*batchim*) under the last syllable: 가 + ㄹ = **갈**. Notice the space: **갈 거예요** is two words, because 거 is a noun (short for 것, "thing"). Grammatically, the sentence is something like "it is a going-to-go thing".

### Pronunciation tip

After the ㄹ, the ㄱ of 거 is pronounced tense, so 갈 거예요 sounds like **[갈 꺼예요]** and 먹을 거예요 like **[머글 꺼예요]**. Revised Romanization doesn't mark this, so you'll see *gal geoyeyo* written, but say it with a hard *kk* sound. The 예요 part is usually pronounced like *eyo*.

| Verb | Meaning | Future | Romanization |
|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 | to go | 갈 거예요 | gal geoyeyo |
| 오다 | to come | 올 거예요 | ol geoyeyo |
| 보다 | to see, watch | 볼 거예요 | bol geoyeyo |
| 만나다 | to meet | 만날 거예요 | mannal geoyeyo |
| 마시다 | to drink | 마실 거예요 | masil geoyeyo |
| 배우다 | to learn | 배울 거예요 | baeul geoyeyo |
| 기다리다 | to wait | 기다릴 거예요 | gidaril geoyeyo |
| 쉬다 | to rest | 쉴 거예요 | swil geoyeyo |

Yes, 사다 ("to buy") and 살다 ("to live") both become **살 거예요**. Context almost always makes it clear: 옷을 살 거예요 is "I'll buy clothes", and 서울에서 살 거예요 is "I'll live in Seoul".

## Irregular verbs

Most of the irregular groups you met in the present tense also change before -을 거예요, because the ending starts with a vowel.

| Type | Verb | Future | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ㅂ irregular | 덥다 | 더울 거예요 | deoul geoyeyo | will be hot |
| ㅂ irregular | 춥다 | 추울 거예요 | chuul geoyeyo | will be cold |
| ㅂ irregular | 맵다 | 매울 거예요 | maeul geoyeyo | will be spicy |
| ㅂ irregular | 어렵다 | 어려울 거예요 | eoryeoul geoyeyo | will be difficult |
| ㅂ irregular | 돕다 | 도울 거예요 | doul geoyeyo | will help |
| ㄷ irregular | 듣다 | 들을 거예요 | deureul geoyeyo | will listen |
| ㄷ irregular | 걷다 | 걸을 거예요 | georeul geoyeyo | will walk |
| ㅅ irregular | 낫다 | 나을 거예요 | naeul geoyeyo | will get better |

The ㅂ stem turns into 우 and then takes -ㄹ: 덥 → 더우 → **더울**. The ㄷ stem turns into ㄹ and keeps -을: 듣 → 들 → **들을**.

The good news: **르 verbs are regular here**. Their stem ends in a vowel, so they simply take -ㄹ 거예요: 모르다 → **모를 거예요** (*moreul geoyeyo*), 부르다 → **부를 거예요** (*bureul geoyeyo*). And regular verbs that only look irregular stay regular: 입다 → 입을 거예요, 받다 → 받을 거예요.

## Two meanings: plans and guesses

-(으)ㄹ 거예요 does two jobs, and context tells you which one.

**1. Plans and intentions** (mostly with "I" and "you"):

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 주말에 친구를 만날 거예요. | Jumare chingureul mannal geoyeyo. | I'm going to meet a friend at the weekend. |
| 다음 주에 부산에 갈 거예요. | Daeum jue busane gal geoyeyo. | I'm going to Busan next week. |
| 한국어를 계속 배울 거예요. | Hangugeoreul gyesok baeul geoyeyo. | I'm going to keep learning Korean. |
| 내년에 한국에서 일할 거예요. | Naenyeone hangugeseo ilhal geoyeyo. | I'm going to work in Korea next year. |

**2. Guesses and predictions** (often with "probably", **아마** *ama*):

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 비가 올 거예요. | Naeil biga ol geoyeyo. | It will (probably) rain tomorrow. |
| 그 영화 재미있을 거예요. | Geu yeonghwa jaemiisseul geoyeyo. | That movie will probably be fun. |
| 지금 바쁠 거예요. | Jigeum bappeul geoyeyo. | He's probably busy right now. |
| 아마 괜찮을 거예요. | Ama gwaenchaneul geoyeyo. | It'll probably be fine. |

The second meaning works for the present, not just the future. 지금 바쁠 거예요 is a guess about right now.

### "Will be" and "probably is": 일 거예요

The copula 이다 ("to be") becomes **일 거예요** after a noun, whether it ends in a vowel or a consonant. It's mostly used for guesses.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 아마 학생일 거예요. | Ama haksaengil geoyeyo. | She's probably a student. |
| 아마 의사일 거예요. | Ama uisail geoyeyo. | He's probably a doctor. |
| 내일은 금요일일 거예요. | Naeireun geumyoiril geoyeyo. | Tomorrow should be Friday. |

## Questions and negatives

A question uses the same form with rising intonation. These are some of the most common questions in Korean small talk:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 주말에 뭐 할 거예요? | Jumare mwo hal geoyeyo? | What are you going to do at the weekend? |
| 언제 한국에 갈 거예요? | Eonje hanguge gal geoyeyo? | When are you going to Korea? |
| 저녁에 뭐 먹을 거예요? | Jeonyeoge mwo meogeul geoyeyo? | What are you going to eat for dinner? |

For negatives, put **안** (*an*, "won't") or **못** (*mot*, "won't be able to") before the verb. With noun + 하다 verbs, the negative goes right before 할 거예요.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 안 갈 거예요. | Naeil an gal geoyeyo. | I'm not going tomorrow. |
| 고기를 안 먹을 거예요. | Gogireul an meogeul geoyeyo. | I won't eat meat. |
| 내일 못 갈 거예요. | Naeil mot gal geoyeyo. | I won't be able to go tomorrow. |
| 오늘은 공부 안 할 거예요. | Oneureun gongbu an hal geoyeyo. | I'm not going to study today. |

## Politeness levels

| Style | 가다 | 먹다 | 하다 | When it's used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formal polite | 갈 겁니다 (gal geomnida) | 먹을 겁니다 (meogeul geomnida) | 할 겁니다 (hal geomnida) | presentations, announcements, formal speech |
| Polite (this lesson) | 갈 거예요 (gal geoyeyo) | 먹을 거예요 (meogeul geoyeyo) | 할 거예요 (hal geoyeyo) | everyday talk with strangers, coworkers, older people |
| Casual | 갈 거야 (gal geoya) | 먹을 거야 (meogeul geoya) | 할 거야 (hal geoya) | close friends, younger people, family |

거예요 is the spoken contraction of **것이에요**, and 겁니다 comes from **것입니다**. You'll see the full forms in careful writing.

## Other ways to talk about the future

-(으)ㄹ 거예요 is the all-purpose choice, but you'll hear three other patterns all the time.

**The present tense** works for fixed plans when there's a time word: 내일 가요 (*Naeil gayo*), "I'm going tomorrow." This is just as natural as 내일 갈 거예요.

**-(으)ㄹ게요** is a promise or offer to the listener ("I'll do it for you", "I'll do it, okay?"). It's only used about yourself, and never in questions.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 제가 할게요. | Jega halgeyo. | I'll do it. |
| 이따가 전화할게요. | Ittaga jeonhwahalgeyo. | I'll call you later. |
| 먼저 갈게요. | Meonjeo galgeyo. | I'll head off first. |
| 잘 먹을게요. | Jal meogeulgeyo. | I'll enjoy it. (when someone gives you food) |

Compare 이따가 전화할 거예요 ("I'm going to call later", just stating a plan) with 이따가 전화할게요 ("I'll call you later", a promise to the person you're talking to). It's spelled 게요, though in speech it sounds like *kke-yo*.

**-겠-** appears in fixed polite phrases such as **알겠습니다** (*algetseumnida*, "Understood") and **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meokgetseumnida*, "I'll enjoy this meal", said before eating). For now, learn these as set expressions.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 가을 거예요 | 갈 거예요 | vowel stem, so just -ㄹ |
| 먹ㄹ 거예요 / 먹를 거예요 | 먹을 거예요 | consonant stem needs -을 |
| 살을 거예요 | 살 거예요 | ㄹ stem, add nothing |
| 덥을 거예요 | 더울 거예요 | ㅂ irregular |
| 듣을 거예요 | 들을 거예요 | ㄷ irregular |
| 갈거예요 | 갈 거예요 | 거 is a separate word |
| 갈 거에요 | 갈 거예요 | the standard spelling is 거예요 |
| 내일 전화할게요? | 내일 전화할 거예요? | -(으)ㄹ게요 can't be a question |

## FAQ

**Is it 거예요 or 거에요?** The standard spelling is **거예요**. You'll see 거에요 online, but it's a common misspelling. The rule: after a vowel (거 ends in a vowel) the copula is 예요, after a consonant it's 이에요.

**What's the difference between "will" and "going to" in Korean?** There isn't one. -(으)ㄹ 거예요 covers both, and the present tense with a time word also works for plans.

**What's the difference between -(으)ㄹ 거예요 and -(으)ㄹ게요?** -(으)ㄹ 거예요 states a plan or makes a guess. -(으)ㄹ게요 is a promise or decision made with the listener in mind, often in response to them, and only for things you yourself will do. If someone asks who'll pay, **제가 낼게요** (*Jega naelgeyo*, "I'll pay") is the natural answer.

**Can I use -(으)ㄹ 거예요 about other people?** Yes, but about other people (and the weather, prices, and so on) it usually sounds like a guess: 친구가 올 거예요 is "My friend will probably come." With "I", it sounds like a plan.

**Why does 갈 거예요 sound like "gal kkeoyeyo"?** After the future ㄹ, the next ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ or ㅈ is pronounced tense. It's a regular sound rule, so 할 수 있어요 ("I can do it") also sounds like [할 쑤 이써요].

**What's next?** Now that you have present, past and future, a natural next step is asking and suggesting with -(으)ㄹ까요? (갈까요?, "Shall we go?"), which uses the same vowel/consonant rule you just learned.
