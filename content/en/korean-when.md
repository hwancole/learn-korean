"When I was a child", "when you have time", "when I'm tired": sentences like these need a way to point to a moment. Korean has no separate conjunction for "when". Instead, you turn the verb into a describing form and put the noun **때** ("time, moment") after it.

> **Quick answer:** Drop **-다** and add **-ㄹ 때** if the stem ends in a vowel, or **-을 때** if it ends in a consonant. Stems ending in ㄹ just add **때**. 가다 → **갈 때** (*gal ttae*, "when (I) go"), 먹다 → **먹을 때** (*meogeul ttae*, "when (I) eat"), 살다 → **살 때** (*sal ttae*). For a finished event in the past, use **-았/었을 때**: **한국에 갔을 때** (*Hanguge gasseul ttae*), "when I went to Korea." After a noun, just add 때: **방학 때** ("during the school break"). To *ask* "when?", use **언제**, not 때.

This lesson builds on the [present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) and the [future tense -(으)ㄹ 거예요](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/), which uses the same -(으)ㄹ form. If you've read [if in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-if/), you already know that -(으)면 can also translate as "when"; the difference is explained below. Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## How to form -(으)ㄹ 때

Look at the last letter of the stem (the verb or adjective minus 다).

| Stem ends in | Add | Verb | -(으)ㄹ 때 form | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a vowel | -ㄹ 때 | 가다 (to go) | 갈 때 | gal ttae | when (I) go |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 때 | 보다 (to see, watch) | 볼 때 | bol ttae | when (I) watch |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 때 | 자다 (to sleep) | 잘 때 | jal ttae | when (I) sleep |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 때 | 하다 (to do) | 할 때 | hal ttae | when (I) do |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 때 | 바쁘다 (to be busy) | 바쁠 때 | bappeul ttae | when (I'm) busy |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 때 | 피곤하다 (to be tired) | 피곤할 때 | pigonhal ttae | when (I'm) tired |
| ㄹ | 때 | 살다 (to live) | 살 때 | sal ttae | when (I) live |
| ㄹ | 때 | 만들다 (to make) | 만들 때 | mandeul ttae | when (I) make |
| ㄹ | 때 | 놀다 (to play, hang out) | 놀 때 | nol ttae | when (we) hang out |
| a consonant | -을 때 | 먹다 (to eat) | 먹을 때 | meogeul ttae | when (I) eat |
| a consonant | -을 때 | 읽다 (to read) | 읽을 때 | ilgeul ttae | when (I) read |
| a consonant | -을 때 | 있다 (to have, be there) | 있을 때 | isseul ttae | when (I) have |
| a consonant | -을 때 | 없다 (to not have) | 없을 때 | eopseul ttae | when (I) don't have |
| a consonant | -을 때 | 좋다 (to be good) | 좋을 때 | joeul ttae | when (it's) good |

The ㄹ-stem rule is the one to watch: it's **만들 때**, never 만들을 때. This is exactly how the future tense works too (만들 거예요), so if you know one, you know the other.

### Pronunciation tip

After the ㄹ ending, 때 keeps its tense *tt* sound, so 갈 때 is simply [갈 때]. When -을 follows a consonant, that consonant slides over: 먹을 때 sounds like [머글 때], 있을 때 like [이쓸 때] and 없을 때 like [업쓸 때]. The ㅎ of 좋다 goes silent: 좋을 때 sounds like [조을 때].

## Irregular verbs

The usual irregular groups change their stem before -을.

| Type | Verb | -(으)ㄹ 때 form | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ㄷ irregular | 듣다 (to listen) | 들을 때 | deureul ttae | when (I) listen |
| ㄷ irregular | 걷다 (to walk) | 걸을 때 | georeul ttae | when (I) walk |
| ㅂ irregular | 춥다 (to be cold) | 추울 때 | chuul ttae | when (it's) cold |
| ㅂ irregular | 덥다 (to be hot) | 더울 때 | deoul ttae | when (it's) hot |
| ㅂ irregular | 어렵다 (to be difficult) | 어려울 때 | eoryeoul ttae | when (it's) difficult |
| 르 verb | 모르다 (to not know) | 모를 때 | moreul ttae | when (I) don't know |

## Past events: -았/었을 때

For something that already happened and was completed, put the past marker before -을 때. Form it like the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/): 갔어요 → **갔을 때**, 먹었어요 → **먹었을 때**, 했어요 → **했을 때**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 어렸을 때 부산에 살았어요. | Eoryeosseul ttae Busane sarasseoyo. | When I was young, I lived in Busan. |
| 처음 만났을 때 기억나요? | Cheoeum mannasseul ttae gieongnayo? | Do you remember when we first met? |
| 한국에 갔을 때 친구를 만났어요. | Hanguge gasseul ttae chingureul mannasseoyo. | When I went to Korea, I met a friend. |
| 시험에 합격했을 때 정말 기뻤어요. | Siheome hapgyeokaesseul ttae jeongmal gippeosseoyo. | When I passed the exam, I was really happy. |

### 갈 때 vs 갔을 때

This pair trips up a lot of learners, because both can be "when I went" in English. **갈 때** is the moment of going, on the way. **갔을 때** is after you arrived, while you were there.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 한국에 갈 때 비행기에서 영화를 봤어요. | Hanguge gal ttae bihaenggieseo yeonghwareul bwasseoyo. | On my way to Korea, I watched a movie on the plane. |
| 한국에 갔을 때 비빔밥을 먹었어요. | Hanguge gasseul ttae bibimbabeul meogeosseoyo. | When I was in Korea, I ate bibimbap. |

**어릴 때** and **어렸을 때** ("when I was little") are both correct and mean the same thing; 어렸을 때 is slightly more common in speech.

## Nouns + 때

After a noun, simply add 때 with a space.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 방학 때 | banghak ttae | during the school break |
| 휴가 때 | hyuga ttae | on vacation, during time off |
| 생일 때 | saengil ttae | on (my) birthday |
| 시험 때 | siheom ttae | at exam time |
| 고등학생 때 | godeunghaksaeng ttae | when (I) was a high school student |
| 크리스마스 때 | keuriseumaseu ttae | at Christmas |

Not every time word takes 때. Days, parts of the day and words like 주말 ("weekend") use the particle **에** instead: **주말에** (*jumare*), **월요일에** (*woryoire*), never 주말 때. See [days of the week in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-days-of-the-week/) and [telling time](/learn-korean/en/korean-telling-time/) for those.

Also remember two common one-word forms: **그때** (*geuttae*, "then, at that time") and **이때** (*ittae*, "at this time").

## Everyday sentences with -(으)ㄹ 때

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 밥 먹을 때 휴대폰을 안 봐요. | Bap meogeul ttae hyudaeponeul an bwayo. | I don't look at my phone when I eat. |
| 피곤할 때 커피를 마셔요. | Pigonhal ttae keopireul masyeoyo. | I drink coffee when I'm tired. |
| 한국어를 공부할 때 드라마를 많이 봐요. | Hangugeoreul gongbuhal ttae deuramareul mani bwayo. | I watch a lot of dramas when I study Korean. |
| 시간이 있을 때 전화해 주세요. | Sigani isseul ttae jeonhwahae juseyo. | Please call me when you have time. |
| 길을 건널 때 조심하세요. | Gireul geonneol ttae josimhaseyo. | Be careful when you cross the street. |
| 추울 때는 따뜻한 차가 좋아요. | Chuul ttaeneun ttatteutan chaga joayo. | When it's cold, warm tea is nice. |

The topic particle 는 after 때 (때는) adds a contrast: "when it's cold (as opposed to other times)". For 주세요, see [please in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-please/).

## 때마다 and 때부터

Two small particles after 때 give you very useful meanings.

| Pattern | Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 때마다 "every time" | 이 노래를 들을 때마다 기분이 좋아져요. | I noraereul deureul ttaemada gibuni joajyeoyo. | Every time I hear this song, I feel better. |
| 때마다 "every time" | 서울에 갈 때마다 명동에 가요. | Seoure gal ttaemada Myeongdonge gayo. | Every time I go to Seoul, I go to Myeongdong. |
| 때부터 "since" | 어렸을 때부터 한국 음식을 좋아했어요. | Eoryeosseul ttaebuteo hanguk eumsigeul joahaesseoyo. | I've liked Korean food since I was little. |
| 때부터 "since" | 고등학생 때부터 케이팝을 들었어요. | Godeunghaksaeng ttaebuteo keipabeul deureosseoyo. | I've listened to K-pop since high school. |

## 언제 vs 때

English uses "when" for both questions and statements. Korean splits them. **언제** asks the question; **-(으)ㄹ 때** describes the moment in a statement. More question words are in [question words in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-question-words/).

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 언제 한국에 가요? | Eonje hanguge gayo? | When are you going to Korea? |
| 한국에 갈 때 연락할게요. | Hanguge gal ttae yeollakalgeyo. | I'll get in touch when I go to Korea. |

## -(으)ㄹ 때 vs -(으)면

Both can be "when". **-(으)ㄹ 때** points to a moment or period and works for past, present and future. **-(으)면** is a condition ("if / whenever this happens, then that") and can't describe a single past event. So "When I went to Korea, I ate tteokbokki" must be 한국에 갔을 때 떡볶이를 먹었어요, not 한국에 가면. Full details are in [if in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-if/).

## Politeness and honorifics

-(으)ㄹ 때 has no politeness level of its own; the final verb carries it. When the subject deserves respect, add **-시** first: 가다 → **가실 때**, 오다 → **오실 때**. You'll see this on signs and hear it in shops: **들어오실 때 신발을 벗어 주세요** (*Deureoosil ttae sinbareul beoseo juseyo*), "Please take off your shoes when you come in." See [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/) for choosing the right level.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 먹을때 | 먹을 때 | 때 is a separate noun, so it takes a space |
| 먹는 때 | 먹을 때 | "when" uses the -(으)ㄹ form, not -는 |
| 만들을 때 | 만들 때 | ㄹ stems just add 때 |
| 듣을 때 | 들을 때 | ㄷ irregular: ㄷ becomes ㄹ before 으 |
| 춥을 때 | 추울 때 | ㅂ irregular: ㅂ becomes 우 |
| 주말 때 | 주말에 | days and 주말 take the particle 에 |
| 때 한국에 가요? | 언제 한국에 가요? | questions use 언제 |

## FAQ

**Is there a space before 때?** Yes. 때 is a noun, so write 갈 때, 먹을 때, 방학 때. The exceptions are fixed words written as one, such as 그때, 이때 and 저녁때.

**What's the difference between 때 and 동안?** 때 points to a time ("at vacation time"); **동안** (*dongan*) stresses the whole length of a period ("during the entire vacation"): **방학 동안 매일 운동했어요**, "I exercised every day during the break."

**How do I say "while" for two actions at once?** Use **-(으)면서**: **음악을 들으면서 공부해요** (*Eumageul deureumyeonseo gongbuhaeyo*), "I study while listening to music." -(으)ㄹ 때 sets the time; -(으)면서 links two simultaneous actions by the same person.

**Can I use -(으)ㄹ 때 for the future?** Yes. The 때 clause stays the same and the final verb shows the future: **한국에 갈 때 선물을 살 거예요**, "I'll buy a present when I go to Korea." The future tense lesson covers [-(으)ㄹ 거예요](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/).

**What's next?** Combine 때 with [want to](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/) (심심할 때 영화를 보고 싶어요, "When I'm bored, I want to watch a movie") and with [because](/learn-korean/en/korean-because/) to tell longer stories about your day.
