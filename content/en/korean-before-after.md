"Before I go to bed", "after class", "three years ago", "in ten minutes": Korean builds all of these with two nouns, **전** ("before, earlier") and **후** ("after, later"), plus the particle **에**. What changes is the form you put in front of them.

> **Quick answer:** For "before doing", add **-기 전에** to the verb stem: 자다 → **자기 전에** (*jagi jeone*, "before sleeping"), 먹다 → **먹기 전에** (*meokgi jeone*). For "after doing", add **-ㄴ 후에** after a vowel or **-은 후에** after a consonant: 보다 → **본 후에** (*bon hue*), 먹다 → **먹은 후에** (*meogeun hue*). After a noun, just add 전에 or 후에: **수업 전에** ("before class"), **수업 후에** ("after class"). With a length of time they mean "ago" and "in": **3년 전에** ("three years ago"), **10분 후에** ("in ten minutes").

This lesson follows [when in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-when/), which uses the same idea of a verb form plus a time noun. It also builds on the [present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/) and [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## "Before": -기 전에

-기 turns a verb into a noun ("sleeping", "eating"), and 전에 means "before". There is only one form, so you never need to check whether the stem ends in a vowel or a consonant.

| Verb | -기 전에 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 자다 (to sleep) | 자기 전에 | jagi jeone | before sleeping |
| 가다 (to go) | 가기 전에 | gagi jeone | before going |
| 먹다 (to eat) | 먹기 전에 | meokgi jeone | before eating |
| 읽다 (to read) | 읽기 전에 | ilgi jeone | before reading |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 듣기 전에 | deutgi jeone | before listening |
| 만들다 (to make) | 만들기 전에 | mandeulgi jeone | before making |
| 출발하다 (to depart) | 출발하기 전에 | chulbalhagi jeone | before leaving |
| 결혼하다 (to get married) | 결혼하기 전에 | gyeolhonhagi jeone | before getting married |

Irregular verbs stay regular here, because -기 starts with a consonant: it's **듣기 전에**, not 들기 전에.

### -기 전에 never takes the past tense

English says "before I *went*", but Korean keeps -기 전에 tenseless and lets the final verb carry the time. So it's **한국에 가기 전에 한국어를 배웠어요**, never 갔기 전에.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 자기 전에 이를 닦아요. | Jagi jeone ireul dakkayo. | I brush my teeth before I go to bed. |
| 밥 먹기 전에 손을 씻어요. | Bap meokgi jeone soneul ssiseoyo. | I wash my hands before I eat. |
| 한국에 가기 전에 한국어를 배웠어요. | Hanguge gagi jeone hangugeoreul baewosseoyo. | I learned Korean before I went to Korea. |
| 출발하기 전에 전화해 주세요. | Chulbalhagi jeone jeonhwahae juseyo. | Please call me before you leave. |
| 늦기 전에 집에 가요. | Neutgi jeone jibe gayo. | Let's go home before it gets late. |

### Pronunciation tip

After a consonant, the ㄱ of -기 becomes tense: 먹기 sounds like [먹끼], 듣기 like [듣끼] and 늦기 like [늗끼]. In 전에, the ㄴ slides into the next syllable, so it sounds like [저네].

## "After": -(으)ㄴ 후에

For "after doing", use the past describing form -(으)ㄴ plus 후에. Look at the last letter of the stem.

| Stem ends in | Add | Verb | Form | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a vowel | -ㄴ 후에 | 보다 (to see, watch) | 본 후에 | bon hue | after watching |
| a vowel | -ㄴ 후에 | 자다 (to sleep) | 잔 후에 | jan hue | after sleeping |
| a vowel | -ㄴ 후에 | 마시다 (to drink) | 마신 후에 | masin hue | after drinking |
| a vowel | -ㄴ 후에 | 졸업하다 (to graduate) | 졸업한 후에 | joreopan hue | after graduating |
| a consonant | -은 후에 | 먹다 (to eat) | 먹은 후에 | meogeun hue | after eating |
| a consonant | -은 후에 | 씻다 (to wash) | 씻은 후에 | ssiseun hue | after washing |
| a consonant | -은 후에 | 읽다 (to read) | 읽은 후에 | ilgeun hue | after reading |
| ㄹ (drops) | -ㄴ 후에 | 만들다 (to make) | 만든 후에 | mandeun hue | after making |
| ㄹ (drops) | -ㄴ 후에 | 살다 (to live) | 산 후에 | san hue | after living |
| ㄷ irregular | -은 후에 | 듣다 (to listen) | 들은 후에 | deureun hue | after listening |
| ㄷ irregular | -은 후에 | 걷다 (to walk) | 걸은 후에 | georeun hue | after walking |

The ㄹ-stem rule is the classic trap: the ㄹ disappears before -ㄴ, so 만들다 becomes **만든 후에** and 놀다 becomes **논 후에**. Like -기 전에, this pattern doesn't add 았/었; the -(으)ㄴ already means the action is finished.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 수업이 끝난 후에 도서관에 가요. | Sueobi kkeunnan hue doseogwane gayo. | After class ends, I go to the library. |
| 영화를 본 후에 저녁을 먹었어요. | Yeonghwareul bon hue jeonyeogeul meogeosseoyo. | After watching the movie, we had dinner. |
| 졸업한 후에 한국에서 일하고 싶어요. | Joreopan hue hangugeseo ilhago sipeoyo. | After I graduate, I want to work in Korea. |
| 손을 씻은 후에 드세요. | Soneul ssiseun hue deuseyo. | Please eat after washing your hands. |

## 후에, 다음에 or 뒤에?

All three mean "after" with -(으)ㄴ. **후에** is the neutral, slightly more written choice. **다음에** (*daeume*, "next") is very common in conversation. **뒤에** (*dwie*, "behind") is also used in speech.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 운동한 후에 샤워해요. | Undonghan hue syawohaeyo. | I shower after working out. |
| 운동한 다음에 샤워해요. | Undonghan daeume syawohaeyo. | I shower after working out. |
| 운동한 뒤에 샤워해요. | Undonghan dwie syawohaeyo. | I shower after working out. |

A fourth option is **-고 나서**, covered in [and in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-and/): **숙제를 하고 나서 게임을 해요** (*Sukjereul hago naseo geimeul haeyo*), "After I finish my homework, I play games." It stresses that the first action is completely done before the next starts.

## Nouns + 전에 / 후에

After a noun, simply add 전에 or 후에 with a space. Words for activities and events work best.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 수업 전에 | sueop jeone | before class |
| 수업 후에 | sueop hue | after class |
| 식사 전에 | siksa jeone | before the meal |
| 출근 전에 | chulgeun jeone | before work (before commuting in) |
| 퇴근 후에 | toegeun hue | after work (after leaving the office) |
| 시험 후에 | siheom hue | after the exam |

Clock times work too: **세 시 전에** (*se si jeone*), "before three o'clock." See [telling time in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-telling-time/) for reading the hours.

### 식전 and 식후 on medicine bags

In Korea, you'll often see the short forms **식전** (*sikjeon*, "before meals") and **식후** (*sikhu*, "after meals") on pharmacy labels. **식후 30분** (*sikhu samsip bun*) means "30 minutes after a meal."

## "Ago" and "in": time + 전에 / 후에

Put a length of time in front, and 전에 means "ago" while 후에 means "in" or "later." This is where [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/) come in: minutes, days and years use Sino-Korean numbers (일, 이, 삼).

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 10분 후에 전화할게요. | Sip bun hue jeonhwahalgeyo. | I'll call you in ten minutes. |
| 3일 전에 도착했어요. | Samil jeone dochakaesseoyo. | I arrived three days ago. |
| 일주일 후에 시험이 있어요. | Iljuil hue siheomi isseoyo. | I have an exam in a week. |
| 2년 전에 한국어를 시작했어요. | I nyeon jeone hangugeoreul sijakaesseoyo. | I started Korean two years ago. |
| 조금 전에 왔어요. | Jogeum jeone wasseoyo. | I came a little while ago. |

## Linking sentences: 그 전에 and 그 다음에

To say "before that" or "after that" at the start of a new sentence, put **그** ("that") in front.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 그 전에 | geu jeone | before that |
| 그 후에 | geu hue | after that |
| 그 다음에 | geu daeume | after that, next |

Example: **먼저 숙제를 해요. 그 다음에 친구를 만나요.** (*Meonjeo sukjereul haeyo. Geu daeume chingureul mannayo.*) "First I do my homework. After that, I meet my friend."

## Politeness and honorifics

These patterns have no politeness level of their own; the final verb carries it. When the subject deserves respect, add **-시** first: 가다 → **가시기 전에**, 드시다 → **드신 후에**. You'll hear this in shops and clinics: **드시기 전에 흔들어 주세요** (*Deusigi jeone heundeureo juseyo*), "Please shake before drinking." See [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/) for choosing the right level.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 갔기 전에 | 가기 전에 | -기 전에 never takes a past marker |
| 먹은 전에 | 먹기 전에 | "before" uses -기, not -(으)ㄴ |
| 먹기 후에 | 먹은 후에 | "after" uses -(으)ㄴ, not -기 |
| 만들은 후에 | 만든 후에 | ㄹ stems drop ㄹ before -ㄴ |
| 듣은 후에 | 들은 후에 | ㄷ irregular: ㄷ becomes ㄹ before 으 |
| 먹었은 후에 | 먹은 후에 | -(으)ㄴ already marks a finished action |
| 수업전에 | 수업 전에 | 전 and 후 are nouns, so they take a space |

## FAQ

**Can I drop 에 and just say 전 or 후?** Yes, especially in casual speech and on signs: **자기 전 스트레칭** ("stretching before bed"). Adding 에 is the standard sentence form. To emphasize or contrast, use **전에는** and **후에는**: **결혼하기 전에는 서울에 살았어요**, "Before I got married, I lived in Seoul."

**What does 다음에 mean on its own?** Without a verb in front, **다음에** means "next time": **다음에 봐요** (*Daeume bwayo*), "See you next time." 나중에 (*najunge*) means "later" in a vaguer way: **나중에 연락할게요**, "I'll get in touch later."

**How do I say "since" or "it's been a year since"?** Use **-(으)ㄴ 지** plus a length of time and 되다: **한국에 온 지 1년 됐어요** (*Hanguge on ji illyeon dwaesseoyo*), "It's been a year since I came to Korea." Note there is no 후에 here.

**Does 전 always mean "before"?** As a separate word in front of a noun, 전 means "former": **전 남자친구** ("ex-boyfriend"). After a verb or time, it means "before" or "ago."

**What's next?** Combine these with [when](/learn-korean/en/korean-when/) and [want to](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/) to describe your daily routine: 퇴근한 후에 운동하고 싶어요, "After work, I want to exercise."
