You can now say what you do, did and will do, and what you don't do. The next thing you'll want in real conversations is to make plans with people: "Shall we get coffee?", "Shall I open the window?", "What should we eat?" In Korean, one ending covers all of these: **-(으)ㄹ까요?**

> **Quick answer:** Drop **-다** from the verb and add **-ㄹ까요?** if the stem ends in a vowel (가다 → **갈까요?**, *galkkayo*, "Shall we go?") or **-을까요?** if it ends in a consonant (먹다 → **먹을까요?**, *meogeulkkayo*, "Shall we eat?"). Stems ending in ㄹ just add **-까요?** (만들다 → **만들까요?**). It has three uses: suggesting something together (**같이 갈까요?**, "Shall we go together?"), offering to do something (**제가 할까요?**, "Shall I do it?"), and wondering aloud (**내일 비가 올까요?**, "Do you think it'll rain tomorrow?"). To say yes to a suggestion, answer **네, 좋아요** or repeat the verb in the present tense: **네, 가요.**

This lesson uses the same vowel/consonant rule as the [future tense -(으)ㄹ 거예요](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/), so if you know 갈 거예요 and 먹을 거예요, you already know how to build 갈까요 and 먹을까요. The answers use the [present tense -아요/-어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), and the politeness section builds on [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## How to form -(으)ㄹ까요

There are only three cases, and they depend on the last letter of the stem (the verb minus -다).

| Stem ends in | Add | Example | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a vowel | -ㄹ까요 | 가다 → 갈까요? | galkkayo | Shall we go? |
| a consonant | -을까요 | 먹다 → 먹을까요? | meogeulkkayo | Shall we eat? |
| ㄹ | -까요 | 만들다 → 만들까요? | mandeulkkayo | Shall we make it? |

The ㄹ in -ㄹ까요 attaches to the stem as a final consonant (가 + ㄹ = 갈), exactly like in 갈 거예요. The 까 is always pronounced as a tense *kk*.

### More regular verbs

| Verb | -(으)ㄹ까요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 보다 (to see, watch) | 볼까요? | bolkkayo | Shall we watch it? |
| 마시다 (to drink) | 마실까요? | masilkkayo | Shall we drink something? |
| 타다 (to ride, take) | 탈까요? | talkkayo | Shall we take it? |
| 만나다 (to meet) | 만날까요? | mannalkkayo | Shall we meet? |
| 하다 (to do) | 할까요? | halkkayo | Shall we do it? |
| 기다리다 (to wait) | 기다릴까요? | gidarilkkayo | Shall I wait? |
| 앉다 (to sit) | 앉을까요? | anjeulkkayo | Shall we sit? |
| 찍다 (to take a photo) | 찍을까요? | jjigeulkkayo | Shall I take it? |
| 닫다 (to close) | 닫을까요? | dadeulkkayo | Shall I close it? |
| 놀다 (to hang out) | 놀까요? | nolkkayo | Shall we hang out? |
| 열다 (to open) | 열까요? | yeolkkayo | Shall I open it? |

Watch out for **사다** ("to buy") and **살다** ("to live"): both become **살까요?** Context makes the meaning clear: 이거 살까요? is "Should I buy this?", while 서울에서 살까요? is "Should we live in Seoul?"

### Irregular verbs

Because -을까요 starts with a vowel, the irregular verbs you met in the present tense change here too.

| Type | Verb | -(으)ㄹ까요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ㄷ irregular | 듣다 (to listen) | 들을까요? | deureulkkayo | Shall we listen? |
| ㄷ irregular | 걷다 (to walk) | 걸을까요? | georeulkkayo | Shall we walk? |
| ㅂ irregular | 돕다 (to help) | 도울까요? | doulkkayo | Shall I help? |
| ㅂ irregular | 춥다 (to be cold) | 추울까요? | chuulkkayo | Do you think it'll be cold? |
| ㅅ irregular | 짓다 (to build) | 지을까요? | jieulkkayo | Shall we build it? |

닫다 and 받다 are regular ㄷ verbs, so they stay 닫을까요 and 받을까요.

## Use 1: "Shall we...?" (suggesting)

The most common use is suggesting something you and the listener do together. Adding **같이** (*gachi*, "together") makes the invitation clear.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 같이 점심 먹을까요? | Gachi jeomsim meogeulkkayo? | Shall we have lunch together? |
| 커피 한잔할까요? | Keopi hanjanhalkkayo? | Shall we grab a coffee? |
| 주말에 영화 볼까요? | Jumare yeonghwa bolkkayo? | Shall we see a movie this weekend? |
| 택시 탈까요? | Taeksi talkkayo? | Shall we take a taxi? |
| 여기 앉을까요? | Yeogi anjeulkkayo? | Shall we sit here? |
| 좀 쉴까요? | Jom swilkkayo? | Shall we take a little break? |

### With question words: "What / where / when should we...?"

Put a question word in front, and -(으)ㄹ까요 asks the listener to help decide.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 뭐 먹을까요? | Mwo meogeulkkayo? | What should we eat? |
| 어디에서 만날까요? | Eodieseo mannalkkayo? | Where should we meet? |
| 몇 시에 만날까요? | Myeot sie mannalkkayo? | What time should we meet? |
| 언제 갈까요? | Eonje galkkayo? | When should we go? |
| 어떻게 갈까요? | Eotteoke galkkayo? | How should we get there? |

## Use 2: "Shall I...?" (offering)

When the subject is you, -(으)ㄹ까요 offers to do something for the listener. **제가** (*jega*, "I") is often added for clarity. For favors, Koreans commonly use **-아/어 드릴까요?**, the humble "do for you" form.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 창문 열까요? | Changmun yeolkkayo? | Shall I open the window? |
| 제가 계산할까요? | Jega gyesanhalkkayo? | Shall I pay? |
| 여기에서 기다릴까요? | Yeogieseo gidarilkkayo? | Shall I wait here? |
| 제가 도와드릴까요? | Jega dowadeurilkkayo? | Shall I help you? |
| 사진 찍어 드릴까요? | Sajin jjigeo deurilkkayo? | Shall I take a photo for you? |

You'll hear 사진 찍어 드릴까요? at tourist spots, and shop staff often ask **포장해 드릴까요?** (*Pojanghae deurilkkayo?*, "Shall I wrap it for you?").

### Asking for advice: "Should I...?"

The same "I" form also asks someone else's opinion about what you should do.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 살까요? | Igeo salkkayo? | Should I buy this? |
| 어떤 걸 고를까요? | Eotteon geol goreulkkayo? | Which one should I choose? |
| 갈까요, 말까요? | Galkkayo, malkkayo? | Should I go or not? |

The pattern **-(으)ㄹ까요, 말까요?** ("should I or shouldn't I?") uses 말다, "to stop / not do".

## Use 3: "Do you think...?" (wondering)

When the subject is a third person, the weather or a situation, -(으)ㄹ까요 asks for the listener's guess. It works with adjectives here too.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 비가 올까요? | Naeil biga olkkayo? | Do you think it'll rain tomorrow? |
| 이 식당 맛있을까요? | I sikdang masisseulkkayo? | Do you think this restaurant is good? |
| 길이 막힐까요? | Giri makilkkayo? | Do you think there'll be traffic? |
| 날씨가 추울까요? | Nalssiga chuulkkayo? | Do you think it'll be cold? |
| 민수 씨가 벌써 도착했을까요? | Minsu ssiga beolsseo dochakaesseulkkayo? | Do you think Minsu has arrived already? |

For a guess about the past, put -(으)ㄹ까요 after the past marker -았/었-: 도착했다 → **도착했을까요?** A natural answer to these questions uses the future ending as "probably": **아마 올 거예요** (*Ama ol geoyeyo*), "It'll probably rain."

## How to answer

Answering a suggestion with -(으)ㄹ까요 again sounds like you're just echoing the question. Use one of these instead.

### Saying yes or no to a suggestion

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 네, 좋아요. | Ne, joayo. | Sure, sounds good. |
| 네, 그래요. | Ne, geuraeyo. | Yes, let's. |
| 네, 같이 가요. | Ne, gachi gayo. | Yes, let's go together. |
| 미안해요, 오늘은 좀 바빠요. | Mianhaeyo, oneureun jom bappayo. | Sorry, I'm a bit busy today. |
| 다음에 같이 가요. | Daeume gachi gayo. | Let's go together next time. |

The present tense -아요/-어요 doubles as "let's" when you agree, so 네, 가요 means "Yes, let's go." To turn down an invitation politely, use **못**, as explained in [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/): 오늘은 못 가요.

### Accepting or declining an offer

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 네, 감사합니다. | Ne, gamsahamnida. | Yes, thank you. |
| 네, 부탁해요. | Ne, butakaeyo. | Yes, please. |
| 네, 열어 주세요. | Ne, yeoreo juseyo. | Yes, please open it. |
| 아니요, 괜찮아요. | Aniyo, gwaenchanayo. | No, it's fine. / No, thanks. |

## -(으)ㄹ까요 vs -(으)ㄹ래요

**-(으)ㄹ래요?** looks similar but asks about the listener's wishes, while -(으)ㄹ까요? proposes a shared decision.

| Korean | Romanization | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| 뭐 먹을까요? | Mwo meogeulkkayo? | What should we eat? (let's decide together) |
| 뭐 먹을래요? | Mwo meogeullaeyo? | What do you want to eat? (your choice) |
| 커피 마실까요? | Keopi masilkkayo? | Shall we have coffee? |
| 커피 마실래요? | Keopi masillaeyo? | Do you want some coffee? |

Both are friendly ways to invite someone. Only -(으)ㄹ까요 works for offers about yourself: **제가 할까요?** is "Shall I do it?", but 제가 할래요 is a statement, "I want to do it."

## Politeness levels

| Style | Shall we go? | When it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 갈까? (galkka?) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 갈까요? (galkkayo?) | the safe default |
| Honorific polite | 가실까요? (gasilkkayo?) | inviting someone older or senior |

In casual speech, just drop -요: **뭐 먹을까?**, **같이 갈까?** The formal "let's" ending **-(으)ㅂ시다** (갑시다, *gapsida*) exists, but it sounds like a command to people older or more senior than you, so with them **같이 가실까요?** is the polite choice.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 만들을까요? | 만들까요? | ㄹ stems add only -까요 |
| 듣을까요? | 들을까요? | ㄷ irregular: ㄷ becomes ㄹ |
| 돕을까요? | 도울까요? | ㅂ irregular: ㅂ becomes 우 |
| 올 까요? | 올까요? | -(으)ㄹ까요 is written as one word with the stem |
| 갈까요? → 네, 갈까요. | 네, 가요. / 네, 좋아요. | answer with the present tense, not another question |
| 선생님, 같이 갑시다. | 선생님, 같이 가실까요? | -(으)ㅂ시다 sounds bossy to seniors |

## FAQ

**Is -(으)ㄹ까요 polite enough for strangers?** Yes. It ends in -요, so it's polite, and suggesting with a question sounds softer than telling. With someone clearly older or senior, add the honorific -시-: 가실까요?, 드실까요? (from 드시다, the honorific "to eat").

**How do I know if 갈까요 means "Shall we" or "Shall I"?** From context and from words like 같이 ("together") or 제가 ("I"). 같이 갈까요? is clearly "Shall we go together?", and 제가 갈까요? is "Shall I go?"

**Is "Shall we" the only translation?** No. Depending on context it can be "Should we", "How about we", "Do you want to", "Should I" or "Do you think". The core idea is asking the listener's opinion about an action or situation.

**What does 할까 해요 mean?** **-(으)ㄹ까 하다** means "I'm thinking of doing": **주말에 영화를 볼까 해요** (*Jumare yeonghwareul bolkka haeyo*), "I'm thinking of watching a movie this weekend." It's an extension of the wondering use.

**Why is it *kka* and not *ga*?** The ending is spelled with ㄲ, the tense (double) consonant, so it's always a sharp *kk* sound: 갈까요 is *gal-kka-yo*, and 먹을까요 is *meo-geul-kka-yo*.

**What's next?** Now that you can make plans, a good next step is learning to say what you want with -고 싶어요 (가고 싶어요, "I want to go"), and practicing times and dates with [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/) so you can answer 몇 시에 만날까요?
