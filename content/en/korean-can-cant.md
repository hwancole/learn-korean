After learning to say what you [want to do](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/), the next question is whether you *can* do it: "Can you speak English?", "I can't eat spicy food", "Can we meet tomorrow?". Korean uses one pattern for all of these: **-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요** for "can" and **-(으)ㄹ 수 없어요** for "can't".

> **Quick answer:** Take the verb stem (the verb minus -다). If it ends in a vowel, add **-ㄹ 수 있어요**: 가다 → **갈 수 있어요** (*gal su isseoyo*, "I can go"). If it ends in a consonant, add **-을 수 있어요**: 먹다 → **먹을 수 있어요** (*meogeul su isseoyo*, "I can eat"). For "can't", swap 있어요 for **없어요**: **갈 수 없어요** (*gal su eopseoyo*). Ask with a rising tone: **내일 만날 수 있어요?** ("Can we meet tomorrow?"). In everyday speech, "can't" is often just **못** before the verb: **못 가요**.

The word **수** here is a noun meaning "way" or "means", so the pattern literally says "a way to go exists" or "no way to go exists". The ending 있어요/없어요 is the polite [present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), and 못 is covered in more depth in [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## How to form -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요

There are two endings, chosen by the last letter of the stem, plus a few predictable exceptions.

| Stem ends in | Add | Example | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a vowel | -ㄹ 수 있어요 | 가다 → 갈 수 있어요 | gal su isseoyo | I can go |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 수 있어요 | 보다 → 볼 수 있어요 | bol su isseoyo | I can see / watch |
| a vowel | -ㄹ 수 있어요 | 하다 → 할 수 있어요 | hal su isseoyo | I can do it |
| a consonant | -을 수 있어요 | 먹다 → 먹을 수 있어요 | meogeul su isseoyo | I can eat |
| a consonant | -을 수 있어요 | 읽다 → 읽을 수 있어요 | ilgeul su isseoyo | I can read |
| a consonant | -을 수 있어요 | 찍다 → 찍을 수 있어요 | jjigeul su isseoyo | I can take (a photo) |
| ㄹ | add only 수 있어요 | 만들다 → 만들 수 있어요 | mandeul su isseoyo | I can make |
| ㄹ | add only 수 있어요 | 살다 → 살 수 있어요 | sal su isseoyo | I can live |

Verbs whose stem already ends in ㄹ (만들다, 살다, 열다, 놀다) keep it and take nothing extra: 만들 수 있어요, not 만들을 수 있어요.

### Irregular verbs

Because -을 starts with a vowel, the same irregular changes you met in the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) appear here.

| Verb | Change | -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 듣다 (to listen) | ㄷ → ㄹ | 들을 수 있어요 | deureul su isseoyo | I can hear / listen |
| 걷다 (to walk) | ㄷ → ㄹ | 걸을 수 있어요 | georeul su isseoyo | I can walk |
| 돕다 (to help) | ㅂ → 우 | 도울 수 있어요 | doul su isseoyo | I can help |
| 줍다 (to pick up) | ㅂ → 우 | 주울 수 있어요 | juul su isseoyo | I can pick it up |

Regular verbs like 받다 ("to receive") and 입다 ("to wear") don't change: 받을 수 있어요, 입을 수 있어요.

### Pronunciation tips

- **수** after ㄹ is pronounced tense: 할 수 있어요 sounds like [할 쑤 이써요], *hal ssu isseoyo*. Romanization doesn't show this.
- **있어요** is [이써요] and **없어요** is [업써요], *eop-sseo-yo*.
- In fast speech the whole thing blends: 갈 수 있어요 often sounds like *gal-ssu-isseoyo* in one breath.

## Saying what you can and can't do

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 한국어를 조금 할 수 있어요. | Hangugeoreul jogeum hal su isseoyo. | I can speak a little Korean. |
| 한글을 읽을 수 있어요. | Hangeureul ilgeul su isseoyo. | I can read Hangul. |
| 김치찌개를 만들 수 있어요. | Gimchijjigaereul mandeul su isseoyo. | I can make kimchi stew. |
| 매운 음식을 먹을 수 없어요. | Maeun eumsigeul meogeul su eopseoyo. | I can't eat spicy food. |
| 내일은 갈 수 없어요. | Naeireun gal su eopseoyo. | I can't go tomorrow. |
| 여기서 지하철역까지 걸어갈 수 있어요. | Yeogiseo jihacheollyeokkkaji georeogal su isseoyo. | You can walk from here to the subway station. |

The object particle 을/를 from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/) marks what you can do. For emphasis, Koreans often add 가 to 수: **할 수가 없어요** (*hal suga eopseoyo*) is "I just can't do it."

## Asking "Can you...?"

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 영어 할 수 있어요? | Yeongeo hal su isseoyo? | Can you speak English? |
| 내일 만날 수 있어요? | Naeil mannal su isseoyo? | Can we meet tomorrow? |
| 여기서 사진 찍을 수 있어요? | Yeogiseo sajin jjigeul su isseoyo? | Can I take photos here? |
| 운전할 수 있어요? | Unjeonhal su isseoyo? | Can you drive? |
| 혼자 갈 수 있어요? | Honja gal su isseoyo? | Can you go by yourself? |

Answer with the same verb: **네, 할 수 있어요** ("Yes, I can") or **아니요, 못 해요** ("No, I can't").

### Polite requests

To ask someone to do something for you, combine the pattern with **-아/어 주다** ("to do for someone") and the honorific -시-. This is one of the most useful travel sentences.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 천천히 말해 주실 수 있어요? | Cheoncheonhi malhae jusil su isseoyo? | Could you speak slowly, please? |
| 사진 좀 찍어 주실 수 있어요? | Sajin jom jjigeo jusil su isseoyo? | Could you take a photo for us? |
| 다시 한번 말해 주실 수 있어요? | Dasi hanbeon malhae jusil su isseoyo? | Could you say that once more? |

With someone older or senior, make 있어요 honorific too: **오실 수 있으세요?** (*Osil su isseuseyo?*, "Would you be able to come?").

## "Can't": 수 없어요 or 못?

Both mean "can't". The difference is style.

| Korean | Romanization | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| 못 가요. | Mot gayo. | everyday, conversational |
| 가지 못해요. | Gaji motaeyo. | a bit more formal or written |
| 갈 수 없어요. | Gal su eopseoyo. | clear and firm; "there's no way I can go" |

In conversation, **못** is the most natural choice, especially when declining an invitation. **-(으)ㄹ 수 없어요** sounds more definite and is common in signs, rules and announcements, like **여기에 주차할 수 없습니다** (*Yeogie juchahal su eopseumnida*, "You cannot park here").

## Past and future

Change 있어요/없어요, never the -(으)ㄹ 수 part.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 표를 살 수 있었어요. | Pyoreul sal su isseosseoyo. | I was able to buy a ticket. |
| 어제는 잠을 잘 수 없었어요. | Eojeneun jameul jal su eopseosseoyo. | I couldn't sleep last night. |
| 다음 주에는 갈 수 있을 거예요. | Daeum jueneun gal su isseul geoyeyo. | I'll be able to go next week. |

The last example uses -(으)ㄹ 거예요 from the [future tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/): 있다 → 있을 거예요.

## "Know how to": -(으)ㄹ 줄 알아요

For learned skills, Korean has a second pattern: **-(으)ㄹ 줄 알아요** ("I know how to") and **-(으)ㄹ 줄 몰라요** ("I don't know how to"). It's formed the same way, with 줄 instead of 수.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 수영할 줄 알아요. | Suyeonghal jul arayo. | I know how to swim. |
| 운전할 줄 몰라요. | Unjeonhal jul mollayo. | I don't know how to drive. |
| 한국 요리 할 줄 알아요? | Hanguk yori hal jul arayo? | Do you know how to cook Korean food? |

Use 줄 알아요 only for skills you learn. For circumstances ("I can come tomorrow"), use 수 있어요: 내일 올 수 있어요, never 내일 올 줄 알아요.

## "May I?": permission is a different pattern

English uses "can" for permission ("Can I sit here?"). Korean usually doesn't. To ask permission, use **-아/어도 돼요?** ("Is it okay if I...?").

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 여기 앉아도 돼요? | Yeogi anjado dwaeyo? | Can I sit here? |
| 사진 찍어도 돼요? | Sajin jjigeodo dwaeyo? | May I take a photo? |
| 들어가도 돼요? | Deureogado dwaeyo? | Can I come in? |

여기서 사진 찍을 수 있어요? asks whether photos are possible or allowed in this place; 사진 찍어도 돼요? asks the person in front of you for permission. In shops, the shortest way to ask if something works is **돼요?**: **카드 돼요?** (*Kadeu dwaeyo?*, "Do you take cards?").

## "It could happen": -(으)ㄹ 수도 있어요

Add **도** to 수 to talk about possibility: "might", "could".

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 비가 올 수도 있어요. | Naeil biga ol sudo isseoyo. | It might rain tomorrow. |
| 늦을 수도 있어요. | Neujeul sudo isseoyo. | I might be late. |
| 그럴 수 있어요. | Geureol su isseoyo. | That can happen. / Fair enough. |

**그럴 수 있어요** ("that's possible") is a common, kind way to say "it's understandable" when someone apologizes or explains a mistake.

### Intermediate: -(으)ㄹ 수밖에 없어요

**-(으)ㄹ 수밖에 없어요** literally means "there's no way except to...", or "I have no choice but to". 택시를 탈 수밖에 없었어요 (*Taeksireul tal subakke eopseosseoyo*) means "I had no choice but to take a taxi."

## Politeness levels

| Style | I can do it | When it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 할 수 있어 (hal su isseo) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 할 수 있어요 (hal su isseoyo) | the safe default |
| Formal | 할 수 있습니다 (hal su itseumnida) | presentations, interviews, announcements |

**할 수 있어!** ("You can do it!" or "I can do it!") is a classic phrase of encouragement, often followed by 화이팅. See [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/) for when to switch levels.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 먹ㄹ 수 있어요 | 먹을 수 있어요 | consonant stems take -을 |
| 만들을 수 있어요 | 만들 수 있어요 | ㄹ stems add nothing extra |
| 듣을 수 있어요 | 들을 수 있어요 | ㄷ irregular: ㄷ → ㄹ before a vowel |
| 갈수있어요 | 갈 수 있어요 | 수 and 있어요 are separate words |
| 갈 수 있었어요 for "I can go" | 갈 수 있어요 | only change tense on 있다 |
| 내일 올 줄 알아요 for "I can come tomorrow" | 내일 올 수 있어요 | 줄 알다 is only for skills |
| 여기 앉을 수 있어요? to ask permission | 여기 앉아도 돼요? | use -아/어도 돼요 for permission |

## FAQ

**What does 수 mean?** It's a dependent noun meaning "way" or "means". It can't stand alone, so it always follows a verb in the -(으)ㄹ form: 갈 수, 먹을 수.

**Is 갈 수 없어요 rude?** No, but it sounds firm. When turning down an invitation, 못 가요 or 못 갈 것 같아요 (*mot gal geot gatayo*, "I don't think I can go") sounds softer.

**What's the difference between 할 수 있어요 and 할 줄 알아요?** 할 수 있어요 is general ability or possibility; 할 줄 알아요 is specifically knowing how to do a learned skill. For skills, both are correct: 수영할 수 있어요 and 수영할 줄 알아요 both mean "I can swim."

**How do I say "I'm good at" or "I'm bad at"?** Use 잘하다 and 못하다: **한국어를 잘해요** ("I'm good at Korean"), **노래를 못해요** ("I'm bad at singing"). Written together, 못하다 means "to be bad at"; with a space, 못 해요 means "can't do."

**Why does 할 수 있어요 sound like "hal ssu"?** After the ㄹ of the -(으)ㄹ form, the next ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ or ㅈ is pronounced tense. The same rule turns 갈 거예요 into [갈 꺼예요].

**What's next?** Combine this with [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/) to ask about times and amounts (몇 시에 만날 수 있어요?, "What time can we meet?"), and with [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/) to make plans.
