"Try this!", "Can I try it on?", "Have you ever been to Busan?" These come up constantly when you travel, eat out or make friends in Korea, and all of them use the same small helper verb: **보다**, which normally means "to see." Attached to another verb, it means "to try doing something and see how it goes."

> **Quick answer:** To say "try doing", take the polite present form of a verb, drop **요**, and add a form of **보다**: 먹다 → 먹어요 → **먹어 보세요** (*meogeo boseyo*, "Try eating it / Give it a try"). "Can I try it on?" is **입어 봐도 돼요?** (*ibeo bwado dwaeyo?*). In the past tense it means "I've tried" or "I've been": **가 봤어요** (*ga bwasseoyo*, "I've been there"). "Have you ever...?" is **-아/어 본 적 있어요?**: **한국에 가 본 적 있어요?** (*Hanguge ga bon jeok isseoyo?*). "Try to" in the sense of "attempt" is a different pattern: **-(으)려고 했어요** ("I tried to...").

This lesson builds on the polite -아/어요 form from [the present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/) and [the past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/). If you can make 먹어요, 가요 and 해요, you already know the hard part. Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system, which spells words the way they sound.

## How to form -아/어 보다

Start from the polite present form, remove **요**, and add **보다**. The first verb carries the meaning, and 보다 takes all the endings (tense, politeness, requests).

| Verb | Polite form | + 보다 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 먹다 (to eat) | 먹어요 | 먹어 보다 | meogeo boda | to try eating |
| 가다 (to go) | 가요 | 가 보다 | ga boda | to try going, to visit |
| 하다 (to do) | 해요 | 해 보다 | hae boda | to try doing |
| 입다 (to wear clothes) | 입어요 | 입어 보다 | ibeo boda | to try on (clothes) |
| 마시다 (to drink) | 마셔요 | 마셔 보다 | masyeo boda | to try drinking |
| 오다 (to come) | 와요 | 와 보다 | wa boda | to come and see, to visit |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 들어요 | 들어 보다 | deureo boda | to try listening |
| 쓰다 (to write, to wear a hat/glasses) | 써요 | 써 보다 | sseo boda | to try writing, to try on (a hat) |
| 부르다 (to sing) | 불러요 | 불러 보다 | bulleo boda | to try singing |

Irregular verbs keep their usual irregular change: 듣다 becomes 들어, 부르다 becomes 불러, and 돕다 becomes 도와. If you know the polite form, you never have to think about it again.

**Spacing:** the standard spelling puts a space between the two verbs (먹어 보다), and writing them together (먹어보다) is also allowed. You'll see both in real life.

### Conjugating 보다

Once the first verb is fixed, you only change 보다. That's why this pattern combines so easily with everything you've learned so far.

| Meaning | Ending on 보다 | Example | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polite request | 보세요 | 먹어 보세요. | Meogeo boseyo. | Try it. |
| Past / experience | 봤어요 | 먹어 봤어요. | Meogeo bwasseoyo. | I've tried it. |
| Promise | 볼게요 | 먹어 볼게요. | Meogeo bolgeyo. | I'll try it. |
| Plan | 볼 거예요 | 먹어 볼 거예요. | Meogeo bol geoyeyo. | I'm going to try it. |
| Want | 보고 싶어요 | 먹어 보고 싶어요. | Meogeo bogo sipeoyo. | I want to try it. |
| Suggestion | 볼까요? | 먹어 볼까요? | Meogeo bolkkayo? | Shall we try it? |
| Permission | 봐도 돼요? | 먹어 봐도 돼요? | Meogeo bwado dwaeyo? | Can I try it? |

The want and suggestion forms come from [want to](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/) and [shall we](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/). The permission form, -아/어도 돼요, is explained in [can and can't](/learn-korean/en/korean-can-cant/).

## "Try this": recommending something

**-아/어 보세요** is the friendly way to recommend something. You'll hear it from shop staff, restaurant servers and friends who want you to experience something.

| Korean | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 한번 먹어 보세요. | Igeo hanbeon meogeo boseyo. | Give this a try. |
| 이 노래 들어 보세요. | I norae deureo boseyo. | Listen to this song. |
| 한번 해 보세요. | Hanbeon hae boseyo. | Have a go. |
| 다시 해 보세요. | Dasi hae boseyo. | Try again. |
| 경복궁에 꼭 가 보세요. | Gyeongbokgunge kkok ga boseyo. | You really should visit Gyeongbokgung. |
| 조금만 기다려 보세요. | Jogeumman gidaryeo boseyo. | Just wait a little and see. |

**한번** (*hanbeon*) literally means "once", but here it works like "give it a go." When it means "try", it's written as one word: 한번. When you're counting "one time" versus "two times", it's spelled with a space: 한 번.

In restaurants, servers often use the honorific form of 먹다, **드시다**: **드셔 보세요** (*deusyeo boseyo*, "Please try it"). You'd answer with the plain verb, since you don't use honorifics about yourself: **네, 먹어 볼게요** (*Ne, meogeo bolgeyo*, "Okay, I'll try it").

## "Can I try it on?" Shopping phrases

Korean has different verbs for wearing different things, so "try on" changes with the item. Add **-아/어 봐도 돼요?** to the right one.

| Item | Verb | "Can I try it on?" | Romanization |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clothes (shirt, dress, pants) | 입다 | 입어 봐도 돼요? | Ibeo bwado dwaeyo? |
| Shoes, socks | 신다 | 신어 봐도 돼요? | Sineo bwado dwaeyo? |
| Hats, glasses, masks | 쓰다 | 써 봐도 돼요? | Sseo bwado dwaeyo? |
| Rings, gloves | 끼다 | 껴 봐도 돼요? | Kkyeo bwado dwaeyo? |
| Food samples | 먹다 | 먹어 봐도 돼요? | Meogeo bwado dwaeyo? |

Other useful lines in a shop:

- **탈의실이 어디예요?** (*Taruisiri eodiyeyo?*) "Where's the fitting room?"
- **다른 사이즈도 입어 볼 수 있어요?** (*Dareun saijeudo ibeo bol su isseoyo?*) "Can I try another size too?"
- **생각해 볼게요.** (*Saenggakhae bolgeyo.*) "I'll think about it." This is the standard polite way to leave a shop without buying.

To "taste" something, Korean also has the single verb **맛보다** (*matboda*), literally "to see the taste." Free food samples in markets and supermarkets are called **시식** (*sisik*).

## "I've been there": experience with -아/어 봤어요

In the past tense, -아/어 보다 often means you have done something at least once. This is how Koreans usually say "I've been to..." or "I've tried...".

| Korean | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| 제주도에 가 봤어요? | Jejudoe ga bwasseoyo? | Have you been to Jeju Island? |
| 네, 작년에 가 봤어요. | Ne, jangnyeone ga bwasseoyo. | Yes, I went there last year. |
| 아니요, 아직 안 가 봤어요. | Aniyo, ajik an ga bwasseoyo. | No, I haven't been yet. |
| 막걸리 마셔 봤어요? | Makgeolli masyeo bwasseoyo? | Have you tried makgeolli? |
| 한 번도 안 마셔 봤어요. | Han beondo an masyeo bwasseoyo. | I've never tried it, not even once. |
| 기회가 없어서 못 가 봤어요. | Gihoega eopseoseo mot ga bwasseoyo. | I haven't had a chance to go. |

Notice where the negative goes: **안** and **못** come before the main verb, not before 보다. So it's 안 가 봤어요, not 가 안 봤어요. **못** suggests you wanted to but couldn't, while **안** is neutral. Both are explained in [negation in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/).

## "Have you ever...?": -(으)ㄴ 적이 있어요

For "have you ever" questions, Korean uses **적** (*jeok*, "a time when") with 있다 or 없다:

- **-(으)ㄴ 적이 있어요** = "I have (done it before)"
- **-(으)ㄴ 적이 없어요** = "I have never (done it)"

The verb takes the past modifier **-(으)ㄴ**: stems ending in a vowel add -ㄴ (가다 → 간), and stems ending in a consonant add -은 (먹다 → 먹은). In speech, the particle 이 is often dropped: 간 적 있어요.

It's very common to combine both patterns into **-아/어 본 적이 있어요**, "have you ever tried / been":

| Korean | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| 한국에 가 본 적 있어요? | Hanguge ga bon jeok isseoyo? | Have you ever been to Korea? |
| 김치를 만들어 본 적이 있어요. | Gimchireul mandeureo bon jeogi isseoyo. | I've made kimchi before. |
| 번지점프를 해 본 적이 없어요. | Beonjijeompeureul hae bon jeogi eopseoyo. | I've never done a bungee jump. |
| 한국 드라마를 본 적 있어요? | Hanguk deuramareul bon jeok isseoyo? | Have you ever watched a Korean drama? |

### When to use -(으)ㄴ 적 without 보다

Because 보다 suggests choosing to try something, it doesn't fit accidents or things that just happened to you. For those, use plain **-(으)ㄴ 적이 있어요**:

| Natural | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| 지하철에서 길을 잃은 적이 있어요. | Jihacheoreseo gireul ireun jeogi isseoyo. | I've gotten lost in the subway before. |
| 여권을 잃어버린 적이 있어요. | Yeogwoneul ireobeorin jeogi isseoyo. | I've lost my passport before. |
| 감기에 걸린 적이 없어요. | Gamgie geollin jeogi eopseoyo. | I've never caught a cold. |

Saying 길을 잃어 본 적이 있어요 would sound like you got lost on purpose, as an experiment.

### 봤어요 or 본 적이 있어요?

Both can mean "I've done it." **가 봤어요** is shorter and more casual, and it can also just mean "I went (to check it out)." **가 본 적이 있어요** puts the focus on whether the experience has ever happened in your life. For a simple "Have you been to...?", both are natural.

## "Try to" is a different verb: -(으)려고 하다

English "try" has two meanings, and Korean splits them.

- **Try doing** (do it and see what happens) = **-아/어 보다**
- **Try to** (make an attempt, often unsuccessfully) = **-(으)려고 하다** or **-(으)려고 노력하다**

Form -(으)려고 by adding **-려고** to stems ending in a vowel (자다 → 자려고) and **-으려고** to stems ending in a consonant (먹다 → 먹으려고). With ㄹ stems, add just -려고 (만들다 → 만들려고).

| Korean | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| 일찍 자려고 했는데 못 잤어요. | Iljjik jaryeogo haenneunde mot jasseoyo. | I tried to sleep early, but I couldn't. |
| 매일 한국어를 공부하려고 노력해요. | Maeil hangugeoreul gongbuharyeogo noryeokhaeyo. | I try to study Korean every day. |
| 전화해 봤는데 안 받아요. | Jeonhwahae bwanneunde an badayo. | I tried calling, but there's no answer. |
| 전화하려고 했는데 잊어버렸어요. | Jeonhwaharyeogo haenneunde ijeobeoryeosseoyo. | I meant to call, but I forgot. |

Compare the last two. 전화해 봤어요 means you actually called. 전화하려고 했어요 means you intended to and didn't. The **-는데** ending in these sentences works like "but" here; see ["but" in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-but/).

**노력하다** (*noryeokhada*, "to make an effort") is for real, ongoing effort: diets, studying, habits.

## Verbs that already include 보다

A few common verbs have absorbed 보다 so completely that they're written as one word and listed in dictionaries on their own. You'll hear them every day:

| Verb | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 물어보다 | mureoboda | to ask (a question) |
| 알아보다 | araboda | to look into, to find out |
| 찾아보다 | chajaboda | to look up, to search for |
| 맛보다 | matboda | to taste |

- **직원한테 물어볼게요.** (*Jigwonhante mureobolgeyo.*) "I'll ask a staff member."
- **인터넷에서 찾아보세요.** (*Inteoneseseo chajaboseyo.*) "Look it up online."

## Formal and casual versions

The ending on 보다 changes with the [speech level](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

| Speech level | Request | Experience |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | 드셔 보십시오 (deusyeo bosipsio) | 가 봤습니다 (ga bwatseumnida) |
| Polite | 먹어 보세요 (meogeo boseyo) | 가 봤어요 (ga bwasseoyo) |
| Casual | 먹어 봐 (meogeo bwa) | 가 봤어 (ga bwasseo) |

With friends, **먹어 봐!** ("Try it!") and **가 봤어?** ("Have you been?") are what you'll hear most.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 먹다 보세요 | 먹어 보세요 | Use the -아/어 form before 보다, not the dictionary form. |
| 가 안 봤어요 | 안 가 봤어요 | 안 and 못 go before the main verb. |
| 예뻐 봐요 ("try being pretty") | 예뻐 보여요 ("it looks pretty") | Adjectives don't take -아/어 보다. 보여요 describes appearance; see ["I think"](/learn-korean/en/korean-i-think/). |
| 길을 잃어 본 적이 있어요 | 길을 잃은 적이 있어요 | 보다 implies trying on purpose, which doesn't fit accidents. |
| 공부해 봤는데 시험에 떨어졌어요 (meaning "I tried hard") | 열심히 공부했는데 시험에 떨어졌어요 | -아/어 보다 means "try out", not "make a big effort." |
| 봐 보세요 for "take a look" | 한번 보세요 | 봐 보세요 is heard in speech, but 한번 보세요 sounds more natural. |

## FAQ

**What does 보다 mean on its own?** "To see" or "to watch": 영화를 봐요 (*yeonghwareul bwayo*, "I watch a movie"). After another verb in the -아/어 form, it becomes a helper verb meaning "try doing and see."

**Is 먹어 보세요 polite?** Yes. -세요 is the polite request ending, the same one you met in [please in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-please/). For someone senior, a server might say 드셔 보세요. With friends, use 먹어 봐.

**How do I say "I've been to Korea"?** **한국에 가 봤어요** (*Hanguge ga bwasseoyo*) or **한국에 가 본 적이 있어요** (*Hanguge ga bon jeogi isseoyo*). Both are natural.

**What's the difference between 한번 and 한 번?** 한번 (one word) means "give it a try" or "sometime": 한번 해 보세요. 한 번 (two words) counts one time: 한 번 더 해 보세요, "Try one more time."

**How do I say "I'll try my best"?** Use 열심히 ("hard") or 최선 ("best"), not 보다: **열심히 할게요** (*Yeolsimhi halgeyo*) or, more formally, **최선을 다하겠습니다** (*Choeseoneul dahagetseumnida*).

**What's next?** Combine -아/어 보다 with [if](/learn-korean/en/korean-if/) to give advice: **힘들면 다시 해 보세요**, "If it's hard, try again."
