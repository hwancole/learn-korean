Once you can say what you do, did and will do, you need the opposite: "I don't eat meat", "I didn't go", "I can't come tomorrow". Korean builds almost every negative sentence from just two little words, **안** and **못**, and each one has a short and a long form.

> **Quick answer:** Put **안** (*an*) before a verb for "don't / not": 가요 → **안 가요** ("I don't go"). Put **못** (*mot*) before it for "can't": **못 가요** ("I can't go"). The longer, slightly more formal versions add an ending to the stem: **가지 않아요** (= 안 가요) and **가지 못해요** (= 못 가요). With noun + 하다 verbs, 안 and 못 go right before 하다: **공부 안 해요**, not 안 공부해요. Three verbs have their own negative word instead: 있어요 → **없어요**, 알아요 → **몰라요**, 이에요/예요 → **아니에요**.

This lesson builds on [Korean present tense -아요/-어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), [past tense -았어요/-었어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) and [future tense -(으)ㄹ 거예요](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/), and the examples use the particles from [Korean particles 은/는, 이/가, 을/를](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## The four negative forms at a glance

| Meaning | Short form | Long form | Example (가다, to go) |
|---|---|---|---|
| don't / not (choice or fact) | 안 + verb | stem + 지 않아요 | 안 가요 / 가지 않아요 (an gayo / gaji anayo) |
| can't (ability or circumstances) | 못 + verb | stem + 지 못해요 | 못 가요 / 가지 못해요 (mot gayo / gaji motaeyo) |

Short and long forms mean the same thing. The short forms are what you'll hear most in conversation. The long forms are common in writing, in formal speech and with longer verbs, and they sound a little more measured.

## 안: "don't" and "not"

**안** is a separate word placed directly before the conjugated verb or adjective. Nothing about the verb itself changes, so if you can conjugate a verb, you can already negate it.

| Positive | Negative | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 가요 | 안 가요 | an gayo | I don't go / I'm not going |
| 먹어요 | 안 먹어요 | an meogeoyo | I don't eat |
| 마셔요 | 안 마셔요 | an masyeoyo | I don't drink |
| 봐요 | 안 봐요 | an bwayo | I don't watch |
| 비싸요 | 안 비싸요 | an bissayo | It's not expensive |
| 매워요 | 안 매워요 | an maewoyo | It's not spicy |
| 바빠요 | 안 바빠요 | an bappayo | I'm not busy |
| 좋아해요 | 안 좋아해요 | an joahaeyo | I don't like it |

안 works with adjectives too: 안 비싸요, 안 매워요. If there's an object, it stays in front of 안: **고기를 안 먹어요** (*Gogireul an meogeoyo*), "I don't eat meat."

### Past and future with 안

The tense goes on the verb, and 안 stays in front.

| Tense | Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | 안 먹어요 | an meogeoyo | I don't eat |
| Past | 안 먹었어요 | an meogeosseoyo | I didn't eat |
| Future | 안 먹을 거예요 | an meogeul geoyeyo | I'm not going to eat |

## The long form: -지 않아요

Drop **-다** from the dictionary form and add **-지 않아요**. There's no vowel harmony and no batchim rule: every stem takes the same ending.

| Verb | Long negative | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 가지 않아요 | gaji anayo | I don't go |
| 먹다 | 먹지 않아요 | meokji anayo | I don't eat |
| 듣다 | 듣지 않아요 | deutji anayo | I don't listen |
| 덥다 | 덥지 않아요 | deopji anayo | It's not hot |
| 크다 | 크지 않아요 | keuji anayo | It's not big |
| 공부하다 | 공부하지 않아요 | gongbuhaji anayo | I don't study |

A nice side effect: **irregular verbs stay regular** in the long form, because -지 starts with a consonant. 듣다 is 들어요 in the present tense, but 듣지 않아요 in the long negative.

Tense goes on 않다: **가지 않았어요** (*gaji anasseoyo*, "I didn't go"), **가지 않을 거예요** (*gaji aneul geoyeyo*, "I'm not going to go"). The ㅎ in 않 is silent, so 않아요 sounds like [아나요].

## 못: "can't"

**못** means you can't do something, either because you lack the ability or because something is stopping you. Like 안, it goes directly before the verb.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 못 가요. | Naeil mot gayo. | I can't go tomorrow. |
| 매운 음식을 못 먹어요. | Maeun eumsigeul mot meogeoyo. | I can't eat spicy food. |
| 어제 잠을 못 잤어요. | Eoje jameul mot jasseoyo. | I couldn't sleep last night. |
| 수영을 못 해요. | Suyeongeul mot haeyo. | I can't swim. |
| 한자를 못 읽어요. | Hanjareul mot ilgeoyo. | I can't read Chinese characters. |

The long form is **-지 못해요**: 가지 못해요 (*gaji motaeyo*), 먹지 못했어요 (*meokji motaesseoyo*, "I couldn't eat").

### 안 or 못?

The difference is the reason. **안** is "I don't / I won't", often by choice. **못** is "I can't", because of ability or circumstances. Choosing the wrong one can change how polite you sound.

| Korean | Romanization | What it implies |
|---|---|---|
| 파티에 안 가요. | Patie an gayo. | I'm not going to the party. (my decision) |
| 파티에 못 가요. | Patie mot gayo. | I can't make it to the party. (something prevents me) |
| 술을 안 마셔요. | Sureul an masyeoyo. | I don't drink. (I choose not to) |
| 술을 못 마셔요. | Sureul mot masyeoyo. | I can't drink. (my body can't handle it, or I'm not allowed) |

When you turn down an invitation, **못 가요** is the softer choice: it suggests you'd like to go but can't. 안 가요 can sound like you simply don't want to.

**못 is not used with adjectives.** You can't "be unable to be expensive", so 못 비싸요 is wrong. Use 안 with adjectives: 안 비싸요.

### Pronunciation of 못

못 is written with ㅅ but its final sound is a *t*, which changes the next sound:

| Written | Sounds like | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 못 가요 | [몯 까요] | the next ㄱ becomes tense |
| 못 먹어요 | [몬 머거요] | t becomes n before ㅁ |
| 못 해요 | [모태요] | t + ㅎ becomes ㅌ |

## 하다 verbs: where do 안 and 못 go?

With **noun + 하다** verbs (공부하다, 운동하다, 요리하다), 안 and 못 go between the noun and 하다. Putting them in front of the whole verb is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

| Verb | Negative | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 공부하다 | 공부 안 해요 | gongbu an haeyo | I don't study |
| 운동하다 | 운동 안 해요 | undong an haeyo | I don't exercise |
| 요리하다 | 요리 못 해요 | yori mot haeyo | I can't cook |
| 전화하다 | 전화 안 했어요 | jeonhwa an haesseoyo | I didn't call |
| 일하다 | 일 안 할 거예요 | il an hal geoyeyo | I'm not going to work |

You can also add the object particle: **운동을 안 해요**. Both are natural.

This only applies when the part before 하다 is a noun you could use on its own. For other 하다 words, especially adjectives, 안 goes in front: **안 좋아해요** ("I don't like it"), **안 피곤해요** ("I'm not tired"), **안 깨끗해요** ("It's not clean"). The long form avoids the question entirely: 공부하지 않아요, 피곤하지 않아요.

### 못 해요 vs 못해요

Spacing matters here. **못 해요** (two words) is "can't do": 오늘은 숙제를 못 했어요, "I couldn't do my homework today." **못해요** (one word, from 못하다) means "to be bad at": 노래를 못해요, "I'm bad at singing." They sound the same, so in speech context decides.

## Verbs with their own negative

Three very common words don't use 안 at all. Instead, they switch to a different word.

| Positive | Negative | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 있어요 | 없어요 | isseoyo / eopseoyo | there is, I have / there isn't, I don't have |
| 알아요 | 몰라요 | arayo / mollayo | I know / I don't know |
| 학생이에요 | 학생이 아니에요 | haksaengieyo / haksaengi anieyo | I'm a student / I'm not a student |

- **안 있어요** and **안 알아요** are wrong. Use **없어요** and **몰라요**.
- With **아니에요**, the noun takes the subject particle 이/가: **의사가 아니에요** (*Uisaga anieyo*, "I'm not a doctor"), **학생이 아니에요**.
- Many adjectives built on 있다 follow the same pattern: 맛있어요 → **맛없어요** (*madeopseoyo*, "It doesn't taste good"), 재미있어요 → **재미없어요** (*jaemieopseoyo*, "It's not fun").

## "Don't do it": -지 마세요

안 and 못 can't make negative requests. To tell someone not to do something, add **-지 마세요** to the stem.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 가지 마세요. | Gaji maseyo. | Please don't go. |
| 걱정하지 마세요. | Geokjeonghaji maseyo. | Don't worry. |
| 사진 찍지 마세요. | Sajin jjikji maseyo. | Please don't take photos. |
| 만지지 마세요. | Manjiji maseyo. | Please don't touch. |

You'll see 사진 찍지 마세요 and 만지지 마세요 on signs in museums and shops. Among friends, the casual version is **-지 마**: 가지 마, "Don't go."

## Politeness levels

| Style | don't go | can't go | When it's used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal polite | 가지 않습니다 / 안 갑니다 | 가지 못합니다 / 못 갑니다 | presentations, announcements, formal speech |
| Polite (this lesson) | 안 가요 / 가지 않아요 | 못 가요 / 가지 못해요 | everyday talk with strangers, coworkers, older people |
| Casual | 안 가 / 가지 않아 | 못 가 / 가지 못해 | close friends, younger people, family |

## Answering negative questions

Korean 네 and 아니요 answer "is what you said right?", not "is the answer positive?". This is the opposite of English with negative questions.

| Question | Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 고기 안 먹어요? (Don't you eat meat?) | 네, 안 먹어요. (Ne, an meogeoyo.) | Right, I don't eat meat. |
| 고기 안 먹어요? | 아니요, 먹어요. (Aniyo, meogeoyo.) | No, I do eat it. |

So if you agree that you *don't* eat meat, you start with **네**.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 안 공부해요 | 공부 안 해요 | 안 goes before 하다 in noun + 하다 verbs |
| 안 있어요 | 없어요 | 있다 has its own negative |
| 안 알아요 | 몰라요 | 알다 has its own negative |
| 학생 안이에요 | 학생이 아니에요 | 이다 becomes 아니다 |
| 않 가요 | 안 가요 | 안 (short) goes before the verb |
| 가지 안아요 | 가지 않아요 | the long form is spelled 않 |
| 못 비싸요 | 안 비싸요 | 못 doesn't go with adjectives |
| 안 가세요! (as "Don't go!") | 가지 마세요 | requests use -지 마세요 |

## Practice

Make each sentence negative with **안**.

| Sentence | Answer |
|---|---|
| 커피를 마셔요. (I drink coffee.) | 커피를 안 마셔요. (Keopireul an masyeoyo.) |
| 운동했어요. (I exercised.) | 운동 안 했어요. (Undong an haesseoyo.) |
| 김치가 매워요. (The kimchi is spicy.) | 김치가 안 매워요. (Gimchiga an maewoyo.) |
| 내일 일할 거예요. (I'll work tomorrow.) | 내일 일 안 할 거예요. (Naeil il an hal geoyeyo.) |
| 시간이 있어요. (I have time.) | 시간이 없어요. (Sigani eopseoyo.) |

Now choose **안** or **못** and translate:

| English | Answer |
|---|---|
| I can't speak Japanese. | 일본어를 못 해요. (Ilboneoreul mot haeyo.) |
| I don't watch TV. | 텔레비전을 안 봐요. (Tellebijeoneul an bwayo.) |
| I couldn't sleep yesterday. | 어제 못 잤어요. (Eoje mot jasseoyo.) |
| I don't know. | 몰라요. (Mollayo.) |
| I'm not a teacher. | 선생님이 아니에요. (Seonsaengnimi anieyo.) |
| Please don't worry. | 걱정하지 마세요. (Geokjeonghaji maseyo.) |

Then rewrite the first three answers with the long forms -지 않아요 and -지 못해요 (for example, 일본어를 하지 못해요).

## FAQ

**Is 안 가요 or 가지 않아요 more polite?** Both end in -요, so both are polite. The long form sounds a bit more formal and careful, which is why it's common in writing and news, but in conversation 안 가요 is completely normal and not rude.

**What's the difference between 안 and 않?** They're pronounced almost the same but used differently. **안** is a separate word before the verb (안 가요). **않** is part of the long ending after -지 (가지 않아요). 안 is short for 아니, and 않다 comes from 아니하다.

**Can I say 안 좋아요?** Yes. **안 좋아요** (*an joayo*) means "it's not good" and is very common for things like health or weather. "I don't like it" is **안 좋아해요** or **싫어요** (*sireoyo*).

**Why is 몰라요 used instead of 안 알아요?** 알다 ("to know") has its own negative verb, 모르다 ("to not know"), and Korean always uses it. 몰라요 is a 르 irregular form, like 불러요 from 부르다. A softer way to say "I'm not sure" is **잘 모르겠어요** (*jal moreugesseoyo*).

**Is 못 always about ability?** No. It's about anything that makes something impossible: lack of skill (수영을 못 해요), a busy schedule (내일 못 가요) or a rule. If you could do it but choose not to, use 안.

**What's next?** With positive and negative forms in all three tenses, a natural next step is asking and suggesting with -(으)ㄹ까요? (갈까요?, "Shall we go?").
