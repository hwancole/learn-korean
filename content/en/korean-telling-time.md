Telling time is where Korean's two number systems meet in a single phrase, which is why so many learners get it wrong at first. The good news is that there is only one rule to remember, and once it clicks, every time on the clock follows the same pattern.

> **Quick answer:** Use **native Korean numbers for the hour** + **시** (*si*), and **Sino-Korean numbers for the minutes** + **분** (*bun*). So 3:20 is **세 시 이십 분** (*se si isip bun*). Half past is **반** (*ban*): 3:30 is **세 시 반** (*se si ban*). Put **오전** (*ojeon*, a.m.) or **오후** (*ohu*, p.m.) in front: **오후 세 시** (*ohu se si*), 3 p.m. To ask the time, say **지금 몇 시예요?** (*Jigeum myeot siyeyo?*, "What time is it now?").

This lesson builds on [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/), so review both number systems there first if they are new to you. Example sentences use the polite -아/어요 style from [the present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## Hours: native numbers + 시

The hour always uses native Korean numbers. Four of them (하나, 둘, 셋, 넷) and 스물 drop their last sound before a counter word, so one o'clock is 한 시, not 하나 시.

| Time | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 1:00 | 한 시 | han si |
| 2:00 | 두 시 | du si |
| 3:00 | 세 시 | se si |
| 4:00 | 네 시 | ne si |
| 5:00 | 다섯 시 | daseot si |
| 6:00 | 여섯 시 | yeoseot si |
| 7:00 | 일곱 시 | ilgop si |
| 8:00 | 여덟 시 | yeodeol si |
| 9:00 | 아홉 시 | ahop si |
| 10:00 | 열 시 | yeol si |
| 11:00 | 열한 시 | yeolhan si |
| 12:00 | 열두 시 | yeoldu si |

In speech, the 시 after 다섯, 여섯, 여덟 and 몇 sounds tense, like [씨]: 다섯 시 is pronounced [다섣씨] and 몇 시 [멷씨]. Revised Romanization does not show this tensing, so just let your ear guide you.

## Minutes: Sino-Korean numbers + 분

Minutes use the Sino-Korean numbers 일, 이, 삼 and so on, followed by **분** (*bun*).

| Minutes | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | 오 분 | o bun |
| 10 minutes | 십 분 | sip bun |
| 15 minutes | 십오 분 | sibo bun |
| 20 minutes | 이십 분 | isip bun |
| 25 minutes | 이십오 분 | isibo bun |
| 30 minutes | 삼십 분 / 반 | samsip bun / ban |
| 40 minutes | 사십 분 | sasip bun |
| 45 minutes | 사십오 분 | sasibo bun |
| 50 minutes | 오십 분 | osip bun |
| 55 minutes | 오십오 분 | osibo bun |

Now put the hour and the minutes together, largest unit first:

| Time | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 1:05 | 한 시 오 분 | han si o bun |
| 2:15 | 두 시 십오 분 | du si sibo bun |
| 4:30 | 네 시 반 / 네 시 삼십 분 | ne si ban / ne si samsip bun |
| 6:45 | 여섯 시 사십오 분 | yeoseot si sasibo bun |
| 9:10 | 아홉 시 십 분 | ahop si sip bun |
| 11:52 | 열한 시 오십이 분 | yeolhan si osibi bun |

Seconds work like minutes: Sino-Korean number + **초** (*cho*), as in 삼십 초 (*samsip cho*, 30 seconds).

Korean has no word for "quarter" on the clock. A quarter past three is simply 세 시 십오 분, and a quarter to four is 세 시 사십오 분 (or 네 시 십오 분 전, below).

## Half past, sharp, almost and "to"

| Word | Romanization | Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 반 | ban | half past | 일곱 시 반 | 7:30 |
| 정각 | jeonggak | exactly, sharp | 여덟 시 정각 | 8:00 sharp |
| 쯤 | jjeum | about, around | 다섯 시쯤 | around 5 |
| 전 | jeon | before, to | 열 시 오 분 전 | five to ten (9:55) |
| 거의 | geoui | almost | 거의 열두 시예요. | It's almost 12. |

**쯤** attaches directly to the time with no space: 세 시쯤, 삼십 분쯤. **전** follows the minutes, so **두 시 십 분 전** means "ten minutes before two," that is, 1:50. In everyday speech, Koreans more often just say 한 시 오십 분 for 1:50, but you will hear the 전 form, especially for five or ten minutes before the hour.

## A.m., p.m. and times of day

**오전** (*ojeon*) and **오후** (*ohu*) go **before** the time, never after it: 오전 아홉 시 (9 a.m.), 오후 네 시 (4 p.m.). In conversation, people often use a more specific word for the time of day instead.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning | Example | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 새벽 | saebyeok | early morning, before dawn | 새벽 세 시 | 3 a.m. |
| 아침 | achim | morning | 아침 일곱 시 | 7 a.m. |
| 오전 | ojeon | a.m., morning | 오전 열한 시 | 11 a.m. |
| 낮 | nat | daytime, midday | 낮 열두 시 | noon |
| 오후 | ohu | p.m., afternoon | 오후 두 시 | 2 p.m. |
| 저녁 | jeonyeok | evening | 저녁 여섯 시 | 6 p.m. |
| 밤 | bam | night | 밤 열 시 | 10 p.m. |

**새벽** covers the hours from midnight until early morning, so 새벽 한 시 is 1 a.m. Midnight itself is usually **밤 열두 시** (*bam yeoldu si*) or the more formal **자정** (*jajeong*), and noon is **낮 열두 시** or **정오** (*jeongo*). 아침 and 저녁 also mean breakfast and dinner, and 점심 (*jeomsim*) means lunch or lunchtime: 점심시간 (*jeomsimsigan*) is the lunch break.

## The 24-hour clock

Train and bus timetables, flight boards, broadcast schedules and official notices in Korea often use the 24-hour clock. When it is read aloud in announcements, the hour is usually read with **Sino-Korean** numbers: 14:00 is **십사 시** (*sipsa si*) and 18:30 is **십팔 시 삼십 분** (*sippal si samsip bun*). In ordinary conversation, people convert to the 12-hour clock with 오후: 14:00 becomes 오후 두 시.

## Asking and answering "What time is it?"

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 지금 몇 시예요? | Jigeum myeot siyeyo? | What time is it now? |
| 세 시예요. | Se siyeyo. | It's three o'clock. |
| 세 시 반이에요. | Se si banieyo. | It's half past three. |
| 세 시 이십 분이에요. | Se si isip bunieyo. | It's 3:20. |
| 몇 시에 만날까요? | Myeot sie mannalkkayo? | What time shall we meet? |
| 오후 여섯 시에 만나요. | Ohu yeoseot sie mannayo. | Let's meet at 6 p.m. |

Watch the ending: 시 ends in a vowel, so it takes **예요** (세 시예요), while 분 and 반 end in a consonant, so they take **이에요** (이십 분이에요, 반이에요). This is the same rule you saw with the [days of the week](/learn-korean/en/korean-days-of-the-week/).

To say something happens **at** a time, add the particle **에** to the last word: 일곱 시**에**, 일곱 시 반**에**, 일곱 시 십 분**에**. 몇 시에 만날까요? uses [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/) to suggest a plan.

## Daily routine and schedules

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 보통 아침 일곱 시에 일어나요. | Botong achim ilgop sie ireonayo. | I usually get up at 7 a.m. |
| 여덟 시 반에 회사에 가요. | Yeodeol si bane hoesae gayo. | I go to work at 8:30. |
| 수업은 아홉 시부터 열두 시까지예요. | Sueobeun ahop sibuteo yeoldu sikkajiyeyo. | Class is from 9 to 12. |
| 영화가 몇 시에 시작해요? | Yeonghwaga myeot sie sijakaeyo? | What time does the movie start? |
| 몇 시까지 해요? | Myeot sikkaji haeyo? | Until what time are you open? |
| 밤 열 시까지 해요. | Bam yeol sikkaji haeyo. | We're open until 10 p.m. |
| 어젯밤 열한 시에 잤어요. | Eojetbam yeolhan sie jasseoyo. | I went to bed at 11 last night. |

**몇 시까지 해요?** is the most natural way to ask a shop, café or restaurant how late it is open. On signs you will see the same idea written as **영업시간** (*yeongeopsigan*, business hours), often with a tilde: 10:00~22:00. The [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) lesson explains 잤어요.

## Hours vs. o'clock: 시 and 시간

**시** marks a point on the clock. **시간** (*sigan*) measures a length of time. Both use native numbers for the hour count, while minutes stay Sino-Korean.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 두 시 | du si | two o'clock |
| 두 시간 | du sigan | two hours |
| 두 시간 반 | du sigan ban | two and a half hours |
| 한 시간 이십 분 | han sigan isip bun | one hour twenty minutes |
| 몇 시 | myeot si | what time |
| 몇 시간 | myeot sigan | how many hours |

Useful phrases for durations:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 얼마나 걸려요? | Eolmana geollyeoyo? | How long does it take? |
| 한 시간쯤 걸려요. | Han siganjjeum geollyeoyo. | It takes about an hour. |
| 집에서 학교까지 삼십 분 걸려요. | Jibeseo hakgyokkaji samsip bun geollyeoyo. | It takes 30 minutes from home to school. |
| 두 시간 동안 공부했어요. | Du sigan dongan gongbuhaesseoyo. | I studied for two hours. |
| 십 분 후에 출발해요. | Sip bun hue chulbalhaeyo. | We leave in ten minutes. |
| 오 분 전에 도착했어요. | O bun jeone dochakaesseoyo. | I arrived five minutes ago. |

Notice the two uses of 전. After a clock time, **세 시 오 분 전** means "five to three" (2:55). On its own with a length of time and 에, **오 분 전에** means "five minutes ago." 후에 (*hue*) is the opposite: "after" or "in."

## Writing times with numerals

In texts, signs and schedules, times are usually written with digits: **3시 30분** or simply **3:30**. You still read them aloud with the correct number system: 3시 30분 is 세 시 삼십 분. With Arabic numerals, writing 3시 30분 without spaces is acceptable, but when you spell numbers out in Hangul, keep the spaces: 세 시 삼십 분.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 일 시 | 한 시 | Hours use native numbers. |
| 하나 시 | 한 시 | 하나 shortens to 한 before 시. |
| 세 시 서른 분 | 세 시 삼십 분 | Minutes use Sino-Korean numbers. |
| 세 시 오후 | 오후 세 시 | 오전 and 오후 go first. |
| 삼 시간 | 세 시간 | Counting hours also uses native numbers. |
| 몇 시간이에요? (for the time) | 몇 시예요? | 몇 시간 asks "how many hours." |
| 세 시 반예요. | 세 시 반이에요. | 반 ends in a consonant, so use 이에요. |

## FAQ

**Why are hours native Korean but minutes Sino-Korean?** There is no grammatical reason; it is simply the convention. Native numbers are used with many everyday counters, including 시 and 시간, while Sino-Korean numbers are used for 분 and 초, as well as for dates and money. Remember it as "hours native, minutes Sino."

**How do I say "half past" in Korean?** Add **반** (*ban*, half) after the hour: 두 시 반 (*du si ban*) is 2:30. 두 시 삼십 분 is also correct, but 반 is more common in speech.

**How do I say "at" a time?** Use the particle **에** after the time: 세 시에 (*se sie*), "at three." With a range, use 부터 and 까지 instead: 세 시부터 다섯 시까지, "from three to five."

**Is it 열두 시 or 십이 시 for 12 o'clock?** In everyday speech, 열두 시. Sino-Korean hours like 십이 시 or 십사 시 appear mainly when 24-hour times are read in announcements.

**How do I ask if someone has time?** Say **시간 있어요?** (*Sigan isseoyo?*, "Do you have time? / Are you free?"). Add a day or time to be specific: 내일 오후에 시간 있어요? (*Naeil ohue sigan isseoyo?*, "Are you free tomorrow afternoon?").

**What's next?** Combine times with the [days of the week](/learn-korean/en/korean-days-of-the-week/) to make complete plans, such as 토요일 오후 세 시 반에 만날까요? (*Toyoil ohu se si bane mannalkkayo?*, "Shall we meet at 3:30 on Saturday afternoon?"), and use [question words](/learn-korean/en/korean-question-words/) like 언제 (when) to ask about other people's schedules.
