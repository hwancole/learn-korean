Once you can make plans with [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/), the next thing you'll want to say is what *you* want: "I want to go to Seoul", "I want to eat tteokbokki", "I miss you". In Korean, all of these use one easy ending: **-고 싶어요**.

> **Quick answer:** Drop **-다** from the verb and add **-고 싶어요** (*-go sipeoyo*). There is no vowel or consonant rule: 가다 → **가고 싶어요** (*gago sipeoyo*, "I want to go"), 먹다 → **먹고 싶어요** (*meokgo sipeoyo*, "I want to eat"), 하다 → **하고 싶어요** (*hago sipeoyo*, "I want to do"). Ask with the same words and a rising tone: **뭐 먹고 싶어요?** ("What do you want to eat?"). Say "I don't want to" with **가고 싶지 않아요**, "I wanted to" with **가고 싶었어요**, and "he/she wants to" with **가고 싶어 해요**. **보고 싶어요** means both "I want to see it" and "I miss you."

The ending **-아요/-어요** on 싶어요 is the polite [present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), the negative uses the -지 않아요 pattern from [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/), and the politeness section builds on [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## How to form -고 싶어요

This is one of the friendliest patterns in Korean. Take the verb stem (the verb minus -다) and add -고 싶어요. The stem never changes, so even irregular verbs stay regular here.

| Verb | -고 싶어요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 (to go) | 가고 싶어요 | gago sipeoyo | I want to go |
| 먹다 (to eat) | 먹고 싶어요 | meokgo sipeoyo | I want to eat |
| 하다 (to do) | 하고 싶어요 | hago sipeoyo | I want to do |
| 보다 (to see, watch) | 보고 싶어요 | bogo sipeoyo | I want to see / I miss you |
| 마시다 (to drink) | 마시고 싶어요 | masigo sipeoyo | I want to drink |
| 사다 (to buy) | 사고 싶어요 | sago sipeoyo | I want to buy |
| 쉬다 (to rest) | 쉬고 싶어요 | swigo sipeoyo | I want to rest |
| 자다 (to sleep) | 자고 싶어요 | jago sipeoyo | I want to sleep |
| 만나다 (to meet) | 만나고 싶어요 | mannago sipeoyo | I want to meet |
| 배우다 (to learn) | 배우고 싶어요 | baeugo sipeoyo | I want to learn |
| 살다 (to live) | 살고 싶어요 | salgo sipeoyo | I want to live |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 듣고 싶어요 | deutgo sipeoyo | I want to listen |
| 돕다 (to help) | 돕고 싶어요 | dopgo sipeoyo | I want to help |
| 공부하다 (to study) | 공부하고 싶어요 | gongbuhago sipeoyo | I want to study |

Compare 듣다 and 돕다 with the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) (들었어요, 도왔어요): because -고 starts with a consonant, the ㄷ and ㅂ irregular changes don't happen.

### Pronunciation tips

- **싶어요** is pronounced [시퍼요], *si-peo-yo*: the ㅍ moves over to the next syllable.
- The ㄱ in -고 often becomes a tense *kk* after a consonant: 먹고 sounds like [먹꼬], 듣고 like [듣꼬]. Romanization doesn't show this, but your ear will.
- In the negative, 싶지 sounds like [십찌], *sip-jji*.

## Saying what you want

Put the object (with the object particle 을/를 from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/)) or a place in front of the verb.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 집에 가고 싶어요. | Jibe gago sipeoyo. | I want to go home. |
| 떡볶이 먹고 싶어요. | Tteokbokki meokgo sipeoyo. | I want to eat tteokbokki. |
| 한국어를 배우고 싶어요. | Hangugeoreul baeugo sipeoyo. | I want to learn Korean. |
| 오늘은 집에서 쉬고 싶어요. | Oneureun jibeseo swigo sipeoyo. | Today I want to rest at home. |
| 친구를 만나고 싶어요. | Chingureul mannago sipeoyo. | I want to meet my friend. |
| 콘서트에 가고 싶어요. | Konseoteue gago sipeoyo. | I want to go to the concert. |
| 일찍 자고 싶어요. | Iljjik jago sipeoyo. | I want to go to bed early. |

In casual speech the object particle is often dropped, as in 떡볶이 먹고 싶어요. You may also hear 이/가 on the object (떡볶이가 먹고 싶어요), which puts extra emphasis on the thing you're craving. Both are correct; 을/를 is the safe default.

### "I miss you": 보고 싶어요

Literally "I want to see (you)", **보고 싶어요** is the everyday way to say "I miss you." Its past form **보고 싶었어요** (*bogo sipeosseoyo*) means "I missed you" and is what people say when they finally meet again. You'll hear both constantly in K-dramas and K-pop lyrics, often in the casual form **보고 싶어** (*bogo sipeo*).

### "I want to try": -아/어 보고 싶어요

Add **-아/어 보다** ("to try") before -고 싶어요 to say you'd like to try something for the first time. It's perfect for travel plans.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 한국에 가 보고 싶어요. | Hanguge ga bogo sipeoyo. | I'd like to visit Korea (someday). |
| 김치찌개를 먹어 보고 싶어요. | Gimchijjigaereul meogeo bogo sipeoyo. | I want to try kimchi stew. |
| 한복을 입어 보고 싶어요. | Hanbogeul ibeo bogo sipeoyo. | I want to try on a hanbok. |

## Asking what someone wants

Use the same sentence with a question word and a rising tone.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 뭐 먹고 싶어요? | Mwo meokgo sipeoyo? | What do you want to eat? |
| 어디 가고 싶어요? | Eodi gago sipeoyo? | Where do you want to go? |
| 뭐 하고 싶어요? | Mwo hago sipeoyo? | What do you want to do? |
| 누구 만나고 싶어요? | Nugu mannago sipeoyo? | Who do you want to meet? |
| 언제 가고 싶어요? | Eonje gago sipeoyo? | When do you want to go? |
| 커피 마시고 싶어요? | Keopi masigo sipeoyo? | Do you want to drink coffee? |

To answer, just replace the question word: 뭐 먹고 싶어요? → **비빔밥 먹고 싶어요** (*Bibimbap meokgo sipeoyo*, "I want to eat bibimbap").

With someone older or senior, use the honorific form **-고 싶으세요?** (*-go sipeuseyo?*): **어디 가고 싶으세요?** For eating, the honorific verb 드시다 is more polite still: **뭐 드시고 싶으세요?** (*Mwo deusigo sipeuseyo?*).

## "I don't want to"

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 가고 싶지 않아요. | Gago sipji anayo. | I don't want to go. |
| 먹고 싶지 않아요. | Meokgo sipji anayo. | I don't want to eat. |
| 아무것도 하고 싶지 않아요. | Amugeotdo hago sipji anayo. | I don't want to do anything. |
| 오늘은 안 나가고 싶어요. | Oneureun an nagago sipeoyo. | I'd rather not go out today. |
| 가기 싫어요. | Gagi sireoyo. | I don't want to go. (stronger) |

**-고 싶지 않아요** is the neutral, standard "don't want to." Putting 안 in front (안 나가고 싶어요) is common in speech and means "I want to *not* do it." **-기 싫어요** uses 싫다 ("to dislike") and sounds blunter, closer to "I really don't want to," so use it with friends rather than with your boss.

## "I wanted to": past tense

Put the past marker on 싶다: 싶어요 → **싶었어요**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 정말 보고 싶었어요. | Jeongmal bogo sipeosseoyo. | I really missed you. |
| 어렸을 때 의사가 되고 싶었어요. | Eoryeosseul ttae uisaga doego sipeosseoyo. | When I was little, I wanted to be a doctor. |
| 그 영화 보고 싶었는데 못 봤어요. | Geu yeonghwa bogo sipeonneunde mot bwasseoyo. | I wanted to see that movie, but I couldn't. |

Note **되고 싶어요**: "to become" uses the verb 되다 with 이/가 on the noun. **가수가 되고 싶어요** (*Gasuga doego sipeoyo*) is "I want to be a singer."

## "He wants" or "she wants": -고 싶어 해요

Korean grammar treats other people's feelings as something you can only observe, not state directly. So -고 싶어요 is for **I** (and for asking **you**), while for a third person you add **-어 하다** ("to show signs of"): **-고 싶어 해요**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 저는 쉬고 싶어요. | Jeoneun swigo sipeoyo. | I want to rest. |
| 민수 씨는 쉬고 싶어 해요. | Minsu ssineun swigo sipeo haeyo. | Minsu wants to rest. |
| 동생이 강아지를 키우고 싶어 해요. | Dongsaengi gangajireul kiugo sipeo haeyo. | My younger sibling wants to have a puppy. |
| 친구가 한국에 가고 싶어 했어요. | Chinguga hanguge gago sipeo haesseoyo. | My friend wanted to go to Korea. |

Standard spelling puts a space before 해요 here (싶어 해요), though you'll often see it written together online.

## Wanting things (nouns)

-고 싶어요 always needs a verb, so you can't say "커피 싶어요." Pick the verb that fits:

| What you mean | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| I want a coffee (to drink). | 커피 마시고 싶어요. | Keopi masigo sipeoyo. |
| I want a new phone (to have). | 새 휴대폰을 갖고 싶어요. | Sae hyudaeponeul gatgo sipeoyo. |
| A coffee, please. (ordering) | 커피 주세요. | Keopi juseyo. |

**갖고 싶어요** (from 갖다/가지다, "to have") is the go-to for possessions and gifts. The verb **원하다** ("to want") exists but sounds formal or written in everyday conversation; in shops and restaurants, **주세요** ("please give me") is what you need.

### Wanting to *be* something (adjectives)

Adjectives like 예쁘다 ("pretty") or 건강하다 ("healthy") can't take -고 싶어요 directly. Change them to "become" with **-아/어지다** first: **건강해지고 싶어요** (*Geonganghaejigo sipeoyo*, "I want to get healthier"). By contrast, **한국어를 잘하고 싶어요** (*Hangugeoreul jalhago sipeoyo*, "I want to be good at Korean") is fine as it is, because 잘하다 ("to do well") is a verb.

## Politeness levels

| Style | I want to go | When it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 가고 싶어 (gago sipeo) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 가고 싶어요 (gago sipeoyo) | the safe default |
| Formal | 가고 싶습니다 (gago sipseumnida) | presentations, interviews, announcements |

In a job interview you might say **이 회사에서 일하고 싶습니다** (*I hoesaeseo ilhago sipseumnida*, "I want to work at this company").

## -고 싶어요 vs -(으)ㄹ래요 vs -(으)ㄹ까요

| Korean | Romanization | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| 뭐 먹고 싶어요? | Mwo meokgo sipeoyo? | What do you feel like eating? (your desire) |
| 뭐 먹을래요? | Mwo meogeullaeyo? | What will you have? (your choice, now) |
| 뭐 먹을까요? | Mwo meogeulkkayo? | What should we eat? (let's decide together) |

-고 싶어요 is about wanting; it can be a wish that never happens (한국에 살고 싶어요, "I'd love to live in Korea"). -(으)ㄹ래요 is a decision you're making right now.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 먹어 싶어요 | 먹고 싶어요 | the connector is always -고 |
| 가고싶어요 | 가고 싶어요 | write a space before 싶어요 |
| 엄마는 가고 싶어요. | 엄마는 가고 싶어 해요. | use -고 싶어 해요 for other people |
| 커피 싶어요. | 커피 마시고 싶어요. / 커피 주세요. | 싶다 needs a verb |
| 예쁘고 싶어요. | 예뻐지고 싶어요. | adjectives need -아/어지다 first |
| 선생님, 뭐 먹고 싶어요? | 선생님, 뭐 드시고 싶으세요? | use honorifics with seniors |

## FAQ

**Is 싶어요 a verb or an adjective?** 싶다 is a helper adjective in Korean grammar, which is why it conjugates like one (싶어요, 싶었어요, 싶지 않아요) and why the third-person form needs -어 하다, just like 좋다 → 좋아하다.

**Can I use -고 싶어요 to ask "Do you want to...?"** Yes. 커피 마시고 싶어요? asks about the listener's desire. To invite someone, though, -(으)ㄹ래요? (커피 마실래요?) or -(으)ㄹ까요? (커피 마실까요?) sounds more natural.

**How do I say "I want you to..."?** That uses a different pattern, **-았/었으면 좋겠어요**: 같이 갔으면 좋겠어요 (*Gachi gasseumyeon jokesseoyo*), "I'd like us to go together" or "I wish you'd come with me."

**Is 보고 싶어요 romantic?** Not necessarily. Koreans say it to friends, family and partners alike. Context and tone decide how romantic it sounds.

**What's the difference between 싶어요 and 싶습니다?** Only formality. 싶습니다 is the formal style used in speeches, news and interviews; 싶어요 is polite everyday speech.

**What's next?** Combine -고 싶어요 with [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/) to say how many things you want (사과 두 개 사고 싶어요, "I want to buy two apples"), and review the [future tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/) to turn wishes into plans: 가고 싶어요 → 갈 거예요.
