You can already say what you [want to do](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/) and what you [can or can't do](/learn-korean/en/korean-can-cant/). The missing piece is obligation: "I have to go now", "Do I have to change trains?", "You must take off your shoes". Korean expresses all of these with one ending: **-아야/어야 해요**.

> **Quick answer:** Take the verb stem and add **-아야 해요** if the last vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, **-어야 해요** for any other vowel, and **해야 해요** for 하다 verbs: 가다 → **가야 해요** (*gaya haeyo*, "I have to go"), 먹다 → **먹어야 해요** (*meogeoya haeyo*, "I have to eat"), 공부하다 → **공부해야 해요** (*gongbuhaeya haeyo*, "I have to study"). **-아야/어야 돼요** (*gaya dwaeyo*) means exactly the same and is very common in speech. Say "I had to" with **가야 했어요**, "I don't have to" with **안 가도 돼요**, and "you must not" with **가면 안 돼요**.

The -아/어 part follows the same vowel rule as the polite [present tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), so if you can say 가요, 먹어요 and 해요, you already know the hardest part. Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## How to form -아야/어야 해요

Drop -다 to get the stem, then look at the last vowel of the stem.

| Last vowel of the stem | Add | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ㅏ or ㅗ | -아야 해요 | 살다 → 살아야 해요 |
| any other vowel | -어야 해요 | 먹다 → 먹어야 해요 |
| 하다 verbs | 해야 해요 | 하다 → 해야 해요 |

A quick shortcut: take the present-tense form, remove the final 요, and add **야 해요**. 가요 → 가 + 야 해요 → **가야 해요**. 마셔요 → **마셔야 해요**. This also takes care of the vowel contractions and irregular verbs for you.

| Verb | -아야/어야 해요 | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 (to go) | 가야 해요 | gaya haeyo | I have to go |
| 오다 (to come) | 와야 해요 | waya haeyo | I have to come |
| 먹다 (to eat) | 먹어야 해요 | meogeoya haeyo | I have to eat |
| 하다 (to do) | 해야 해요 | haeya haeyo | I have to do |
| 있다 (to be, stay) | 있어야 해요 | isseoya haeyo | I have to be (there) |
| 마시다 (to drink) | 마셔야 해요 | masyeoya haeyo | I have to drink |
| 기다리다 (to wait) | 기다려야 해요 | gidaryeoya haeyo | I have to wait |
| 배우다 (to learn) | 배워야 해요 | baewoya haeyo | I have to learn |
| 일어나다 (to get up) | 일어나야 해요 | ireonaya haeyo | I have to get up |
| 쓰다 (to write, use) | 써야 해요 | sseoya haeyo | I have to write |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 들어야 해요 | deureoya haeyo | I have to listen |
| 돕다 (to help) | 도와야 해요 | dowaya haeyo | I have to help |
| 갈아타다 (to transfer) | 갈아타야 해요 | garataya haeyo | I have to transfer |
| 공부하다 (to study) | 공부해야 해요 | gongbuhaeya haeyo | I have to study |

Notice 듣다 → 들어야 and 돕다 → 도와야: unlike [-고 싶어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/), this ending starts with a vowel, so the ㄷ and ㅂ irregular changes happen here, exactly as in the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) (들었어요, 도왔어요).

### 해요 or 돼요?

Both **-아야/어야 해요** and **-아야/어야 돼요** mean "have to." 해요 comes from 하다 ("to do") and 돼요 from 되다 ("to become, work out"). In everyday conversation 돼요 is extremely common and can sound slightly softer; in writing, news and formal speech, 해요 and 합니다 are more typical. You can use either without changing the meaning.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이제 가야 해요. | Ije gaya haeyo. | I have to go now. |
| 이제 가야 돼요. | Ije gaya dwaeyo. | I have to go now. |

Spelling tip: it is **돼요** (되 + 어요), never 되요.

## Everyday sentences with "have to"

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 내일 일찍 일어나야 해요. | Naeil iljjik ireonaya haeyo. | I have to get up early tomorrow. |
| 숙제를 해야 돼요. | Sukjereul haeya dwaeyo. | I have to do my homework. |
| 약을 먹어야 해요. | Yageul meogeoya haeyo. | I have to take my medicine. |
| 물을 많이 마셔야 해요. | Mureul mani masyeoya haeyo. | You have to drink a lot of water. |
| 여기서 갈아타야 해요. | Yeogiseo garataya haeyo. | You have to transfer here. |
| 30분 기다려야 해요. | Samsip-bun gidaryeoya haeyo. | You have to wait 30 minutes. |
| 여권을 보여 줘야 해요. | Yeogwoneul boyeo jwoya haeyo. | You have to show your passport. |
| 한국어를 더 열심히 공부해야 해요. | Hangugeoreul deo yeolsimhi gongbuhaeya haeyo. | I have to study Korean harder. |

Korean usually leaves out the subject, so the same sentence can mean "I have to", "you have to" or "we have to" depending on context. Add 저는 or 우리는 (with the topic particle from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/)) only when you need to be clear or contrast people. For the minutes in the table above, review [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/): time units like 분 use Sino-Korean numbers.

### "I think I'd better": -아야/어야겠어요

Add **-겠어요** instead of 해요 to say you've just decided you need to do something. It's what people say when they look at the clock and realize it's time to leave.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이제 가야겠어요. | Ije gayagesseoyo. | I'd better get going now. |
| 우산을 사야겠어요. | Usaneul sayagesseoyo. | I think I'd better buy an umbrella. |
| 좀 쉬어야겠어요. | Jom swieoyagesseoyo. | I need to rest a bit. |

## Asking "Do I have to...?"

Keep the same sentence and raise your tone at the end. These are some of the most useful questions for travelers.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 신발을 벗어야 해요? | Sinbareul beoseoya haeyo? | Do I have to take off my shoes? |
| 어디에서 갈아타야 돼요? | Eodieseo garataya dwaeyo? | Where do I have to transfer? |
| 몇 시까지 와야 해요? | Myeot sikkaji waya haeyo? | By what time do I have to come? |
| 미리 예매해야 돼요? | Miri yemaehaeya dwaeyo? | Do I have to book tickets in advance? |
| 뭘 가져가야 해요? | Mwol gajyeogaya haeyo? | What do I have to bring? |
| 얼마나 기다려야 돼요? | Eolmana gidaryeoya dwaeyo? | How long do I have to wait? |

You will also hear staff use the honorific form **-(으)셔야 해요** or the formal **-(으)셔야 합니다** when telling customers what to do: **여기에 서명하셔야 합니다** (*Yeogie seomyeonghasyeoya hamnida*, "You need to sign here"). The -시- shows respect to the listener. For more on this, see [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

## "I had to" and "I will have to"

Put the tense on the final 하다/되다, not on the main verb.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 어제 늦게까지 일해야 했어요. | Eoje neutgekkaji ilhaeya haesseoyo. | I had to work late yesterday. |
| 비가 와서 집에 있어야 했어요. | Biga waseo jibe isseoya haesseoyo. | It rained, so I had to stay home. |
| 택시를 타야 됐어요. | Taeksireul taya dwaesseoyo. | I had to take a taxi. |
| 다음 주에 다시 와야 할 거예요. | Daeum jue dasi waya hal geoyeyo. | You'll probably have to come again next week. |

The last example uses the [future tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/) -(으)ㄹ 거예요 on 하다: 해야 해요 → 해야 할 거예요.

## "Don't have to" vs "must not"

This is where English speakers most often slip, because Korean uses two completely different patterns, and neither of them is simply 안 + -아야 해요.

### "Don't have to": -지 않아도 돼요 / 안 -아/어도 돼요

Literally "even if you don't do it, it's fine." This builds on the permission pattern **-아/어도 돼요** ("it's okay to") that you saw in the [can and can't lesson](/learn-korean/en/korean-can-cant/), combined with the [negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/) forms 안 and -지 않다.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 안 가도 돼요. | An gado dwaeyo. | You don't have to go. |
| 가지 않아도 돼요. | Gaji anado dwaeyo. | You don't have to go. (a bit more formal) |
| 신발을 안 벗어도 돼요. | Sinbareul an beoseodo dwaeyo. | You don't have to take off your shoes. |
| 예약 안 해도 돼요. | Yeyak an haedo dwaeyo. | You don't need a reservation. |
| 걱정 안 해도 돼요. | Geokjeong an haedo dwaeyo. | You don't have to worry. |

For 하다 verbs built on a noun, 안 goes between the noun and 하다: 예약 **안** 해도 돼요, not 안 예약해도 돼요.

### "Must not": -(으)면 안 돼요

Literally "if you do it, it's not okay." Add **-으면** after a consonant and **-면** after a vowel (or ㄹ), then **안 돼요**. You will see this on signs and hear it in museums, temples and on the subway.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 여기서 사진을 찍으면 안 돼요. | Yeogiseo sajineul jjigeumyeon an dwaeyo. | You must not take photos here. |
| 여기서 담배를 피우면 안 돼요. | Yeogiseo dambaereul piumyeon an dwaeyo. | You can't smoke here. |
| 만지면 안 돼요. | Manjimyeon an dwaeyo. | Don't touch it. / You must not touch it. |
| 늦으면 안 돼요. | Neujeumyeon an dwaeyo. | You must not be late. |

| You want to say | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| must / have to | -아야/어야 해요 | 가야 해요 (I have to go) |
| don't have to | 안 -아/어도 돼요 | 안 가도 돼요 (you don't have to go) |
| may / it's okay to | -아/어도 돼요 | 가도 돼요 (you can go) |
| must not | -(으)면 안 돼요 | 가면 안 돼요 (you must not go) |

## Adjectives and nouns

The pattern also works with adjectives, for requirements and rules.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 비밀번호는 길어야 해요. | Bimilbeonhoneun gireoya haeyo. | The password has to be long. |
| 방이 깨끗해야 돼요. | Bangi kkaekkeutaeya dwaeyo. | The room has to be clean. |
| 학생이어야 해요. | Haksaengieoya haeyo. | You have to be a student. |
| 성인이어야 해요. | Seonginieoya haeyo. | You have to be an adult. |

With nouns, use **이어야 해요** after a consonant (학생이어야) and **여야 해요** after a vowel (의사여야 해요, *uisayeoya haeyo*, "has to be a doctor").

## Politeness levels

| Style | I have to go | When it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Casual (반말) | 가야 해 / 가야 돼 (gaya hae / gaya dwae) | close friends, younger people |
| Polite | 가야 해요 / 가야 돼요 (gaya haeyo / gaya dwaeyo) | the safe default |
| Formal | 가야 합니다 / 가야 됩니다 (gaya hamnida / gaya doemnida) | announcements, presentations, work |

## "Have to" vs "should"

-아야/어야 해요 is a real obligation. For softer advice ("you should", "it's better to"), Koreans more often say **-는 게 좋아요** ("doing it is good"):

| Korean | Romanization | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| 우산을 가져가야 해요. | Usaneul gajyeogaya haeyo. | You have to take an umbrella. (necessary) |
| 우산을 가져가는 게 좋아요. | Usaneul gajyeoganeun ge joayo. | You should take an umbrella. (advice) |

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 먹야 해요 | 먹어야 해요 | keep the -아/어 vowel |
| 가야해요 | 가야 해요 | write a space before 해요/돼요 |
| 가야 되요 | 가야 돼요 | 되 + 어요 = 돼요 |
| 듣어야 해요 | 들어야 해요 | ㄷ irregular before a vowel |
| 안 가야 해요 (for "don't have to") | 안 가도 돼요 | "don't have to" uses -아/어도 돼요 |
| 가지 마야 해요 (for "must not") | 가면 안 돼요 | "must not" uses -(으)면 안 돼요 |
| 일했어야 해요 (for "I had to work") | 일해야 했어요 | put the past tense on 하다 |

## FAQ

**Is there a difference between 해야 해요 and 해야 돼요?** Not in meaning. 해야 돼요 is more common in casual conversation, and 해야 해요 or 해야 합니다 is more common in writing and formal speech. Some speakers feel 돼요 points a little more to outside rules and 해요 to personal duty, but both are used interchangeably.

**How do I say "I have to go" when leaving?** 이제 가야 돼요 or 이제 가 봐야 돼요 (*Ije ga bwaya dwaeyo*) are the natural everyday choices. 가 봐야 돼요 adds 보다 and sounds softer, a bit like "I should get going."

**What does 해야지 mean in K-dramas?** -아야/어야지 is a casual ending used to tell yourself or someone close what needs to happen: 밥 먹어야지! ("You've got to eat!"). It's built on the same -아야/어야 form you learned here.

**Can I use 필요해요 for "have to"?** 필요해요 means "is needed" and goes with nouns: 여권이 필요해요 (*Yeogwoni piryohaeyo*, "A passport is needed"). For actions, use -아야/어야 해요.

**What's next?** Combine this with [-(으)ㄹ까요](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/) to make plans (몇 시에 출발해야 할까요?, "What time should we leave?"), and practice the negative patterns from [Korean negation](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/) side by side with 안 -아/어도 돼요.
