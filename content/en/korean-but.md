After learning how to say [and](/learn-korean/en/korean-and/) and [because](/learn-korean/en/korean-because/), the next connector you'll want is "but". Just like "and", Korean splits "but" into two groups: an **ending** that joins two ideas inside one sentence, and **words** that start a new sentence.

> **Quick answer:** Inside a sentence, drop **-다** from the verb or adjective and add **-지만** (*-jiman*): 비싸다 → **비싸지만 맛있어요** (*bissajiman masisseoyo*), "it's expensive but tasty". To start a new sentence with "But...", use **하지만** (*hajiman*, neutral), **그런데** (*geureonde*, conversational, often shortened to **근데**) or **그렇지만** (*geureochiman*, a little more formal). For a softer "but" that sets up background, Korean also uses **-(으)ㄴ/는데**: **비싼데 맛있어요**.

This lesson uses the [present tense -아요/-어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), the [past tense](/learn-korean/en/korean-past-tense/) and the topic particle 은/는 from [Korean particles](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/). Romanization follows the Revised Romanization system.

## The ways to say "but" at a glance

| Form | Where it goes | Tone | Example | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -지만 | on a verb or adjective stem | all levels | 비싸지만 맛있어요 | bissajiman masisseoyo | it's expensive but tasty |
| 하지만 | start of a sentence | neutral, spoken and written | 하지만 좀 비싸요 | hajiman jom bissayo | but it's a bit expensive |
| 그렇지만 | start of a sentence | slightly formal | 그렇지만 괜찮아요 | geureochiman gwaenchanayo | but it's okay |
| 그러나 | start of a sentence | formal, written | 그러나 결과는 달랐다 | geureona gyeolgwaneun dallatda | however, the result was different |
| 그런데 / 근데 | start of a sentence | conversational / casual | 근데 너무 멀어요 | geunde neomu meoreoyo | but it's too far |
| -(으)ㄴ/는데 | on a verb or adjective stem | soft, conversational | 비싼데 맛있어요 | bissande masisseoyo | it's expensive, but it's tasty |

## -지만: "but" inside one sentence

**-지만** is the most direct translation of "but" (or "although"). It attaches to the stem of the first verb or adjective, and the second clause says something that contrasts with it.

The best part: **-지만 never changes.** It's the same after a vowel or a consonant, and irregular verbs keep their dictionary-form stem in front of it.

| Dictionary form | -지만 form | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 비싸다 (to be expensive) | 비싸지만 | bissajiman | it's expensive, but |
| 작다 (to be small) | 작지만 | jakjiman | it's small, but |
| 좋다 (to be good) | 좋지만 | jochiman | it's good, but |
| 춥다 (to be cold) | 춥지만 | chupjiman | it's cold, but |
| 듣다 (to listen) | 듣지만 | deutjiman | I listen, but |
| 맛있다 (to be tasty) | 맛있지만 | masitjiman | it's tasty, but |
| 하다 (to do) | 하지만 | hajiman | I do, but |
| 학생이다 (to be a student) | 학생이지만 | haksaengijiman | I'm a student, but |
| 의사이다 (to be a doctor) | 의사지만 | uisajiman | I'm a doctor, but |

After a noun ending in a vowel, 이지만 usually shortens to 지만 (의사이지만 → **의사지만**). After a consonant, keep 이: 학생**이**지만.

### Examples

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이 식당은 비싸지만 맛있어요. | I sikdangeun bissajiman masisseoyo. | This restaurant is expensive but tasty. |
| 한국어는 어렵지만 재미있어요. | Hangugeoneun eoryeopjiman jaemiisseoyo. | Korean is difficult but fun. |
| 방이 작지만 깨끗해요. | Bangi jakjiman kkaekkeutaeyo. | The room is small but clean. |
| 가고 싶지만 시간이 없어요. | Gago sipjiman sigani eopseoyo. | I want to go, but I don't have time. |
| 피곤하지만 숙제를 해야 해요. | Pigonhajiman sukjereul haeya haeyo. | I'm tired, but I have to do my homework. |
| 저는 학생이지만 일도 해요. | Jeoneun haksaengijiman ildo haeyo. | I'm a student, but I also work. |

가고 싶지만 combines -지만 with [-고 싶어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-want-to/), and 해야 해요 comes from [have to in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-have-to/).

### Contrast with 은/는

When you compare two things, Korean likes to mark both with the topic particle **은/는**. Together with -지만, it makes the contrast very clear.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 저는 고기는 안 먹지만 생선은 먹어요. | Jeoneun gogineun an meokjiman saengseoneun meogeoyo. | I don't eat meat, but I do eat fish. |
| 날씨는 춥지만 하늘은 맑아요. | Nalssineun chupjiman haneureun malgayo. | The weather is cold, but the sky is clear. |
| 제 동생은 키가 크지만 저는 작아요. | Je dongsaengeun kiga keujiman jeoneun jagayo. | My younger sibling is tall, but I'm short. |

For 안 먹어요, see [negation in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-negation/).

### Past and future with -지만

Unlike -고, which usually leaves the tense to the last verb, **-지만 normally carries its own tense**. Put the past marker in front of it: **-았/었지만**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 어제는 비가 왔지만 오늘은 맑아요. | Eojeneun biga watjiman oneureun malgayo. | It rained yesterday, but today it's clear. |
| 열심히 공부했지만 시험이 어려웠어요. | Yeolsimhi gongbuhaetjiman siheomi eoryeowosseoyo. | I studied hard, but the exam was difficult. |
| 밥을 먹었지만 또 배고파요. | Babeul meogeotjiman tto baegopayo. | I ate, but I'm hungry again. |

For the future or a guess, use **-겠지만** or **-(으)ㄹ 거지만**: **내일은 바쁘겠지만 전화할게요** (*Naeireun bappeugetjiman jeonhwahalgeyo*), "I'll probably be busy tomorrow, but I'll call you." The future ending itself is covered in the [future tense lesson](/learn-korean/en/korean-future-tense/).

### Pronunciation tip

After a final ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ or ㅅ/ㅆ, the ㅈ of -지만 is pronounced tense, like [ㅉ]: 먹지만 sounds like [먹찌만] and 맛있지만 like [마싣찌만]. Revised Romanization doesn't mark this, so they're written *meokjiman* and *masitjiman*. After ㅎ, the two sounds merge into ㅊ: 좋지만 is pronounced [조치만], which is why it's romanized *jochiman*.

## 하지만, 그렇지만, 그러나: "But" at the start of a sentence

When the contrast comes in a new sentence, use a connecting word instead of the ending. You can think of 하지만 as "하다 + -지만" frozen into a word.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 한국 음식을 좋아해요. 하지만 매운 음식은 못 먹어요. | Hanguk eumsigeul joahaeyo. Hajiman maeun eumsigeun mot meogeoyo. | I like Korean food. But I can't eat spicy food. |
| 이 가방은 예뻐요. 하지만 너무 무거워요. | I gabangeun yeppeoyo. Hajiman neomu mugeowoyo. | This bag is pretty. But it's too heavy. |
| 좀 늦었어요. 그렇지만 괜찮아요. | Jom neujeosseoyo. Geureochiman gwaenchanayo. | We're a bit late. But it's fine. |

- **하지만** is the safe all-purpose choice in speech and writing.
- **그렇지만** means the same and sounds slightly more careful or formal.
- **그러나** is formal and mostly written: news, essays, books, speeches. In conversation it can sound stiff.

못 먹어요 ("can't eat") is explained in [can and can't in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-can-cant/).

## 그런데 and 근데: the conversational "but"

**그런데** (*geureonde*) is extremely common in everyday speech. It can mean "but", and it's also the Korean way to change the subject, like "by the way" or "so..." in English. In casual speech it shrinks to **근데** (*geunde*).

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이 옷 예뻐요. 그런데 좀 비싸요. | I ot yeppeoyo. Geureonde jom bissayo. | These clothes are pretty. But they're a bit expensive. |
| 그런데 내일 시간 있어요? | Geureonde naeil sigan isseoyo? | By the way, are you free tomorrow? |
| 근데 왜요? | Geunde waeyo? | But why? |

근데 is fine with friends and in relaxed polite conversation, but avoid it in formal writing, emails to clients or job interviews. There, use 그런데 or 하지만.

## -(으)ㄴ/는데: the softer "but"

You'll hear **-(으)ㄴ/는데** constantly. It connects two clauses where the first one gives background, and often the second one is a contrast. It sounds softer and more natural in conversation than -지만, which can feel like a sharp "A, but B".

The form depends on the word before it:

| Word type | Ending | Example | Romanization |
|---|---|---|---|
| action verbs, 있다/없다 | -는데 | 먹는데, 가는데, 맛있는데 | meongneunde, ganeunde, masinneunde |
| adjectives ending in a vowel | -ㄴ데 | 비싼데, 바쁜데 | bissande, bappeunde |
| adjectives ending in a consonant | -은데 | 작은데, 많은데 | jageunde, maneunde |
| nouns | -인데 | 학생인데, 주말인데 | haksaenginde, jumarinde |
| past (any verb or adjective) | -았/었는데 | 갔는데, 먹었는데 | ganneunde, meogeonneunde |

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 비싼데 맛있어요. | Bissande masisseoyo. | It's expensive, but it's tasty. |
| 주말인데 일해야 해요. | Jumarinde ilhaeya haeyo. | It's the weekend, but I have to work. |
| 어제 그 식당에 갔는데 문을 닫았어요. | Eoje geu sikdange ganneunde muneul dadasseoyo. | I went to that restaurant yesterday, but it was closed. |

Because -는데 really means "background, and here's what follows", it doesn't always mean "but". **배고픈데 밥 먹을까요?** (*Baegopeunde bap meogeulkkayo?*) means "I'm hungry, so shall we eat?" Context tells you which. If you want an unmistakable "but", -지만 is the clearer choice. For 먹을까요, see [shall we in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-shall-we-eulkkayo/).

## Useful "excuse me, but" phrases

Some -지만 expressions work as polite openers, just like "Excuse me, but..." or "Sorry, but..." in English. They're very handy when traveling.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 죄송하지만 화장실이 어디예요? | Joesonghajiman hwajangsiri eodiyeyo? | Excuse me, but where's the restroom? | polite request to a stranger or staff |
| 실례지만 성함이 어떻게 되세요? | Sillyejiman seonghami eotteoke doeseyo? | Excuse me, but may I ask your name? | very polite, formal situations |
| 미안하지만 오늘은 못 가요. | Mianhajiman oneureun mot gayo. | Sorry, but I can't go today. | friends, coworkers you're close to |

죄송하지만 is more polite than 미안하지만, so use it with strangers, staff and older people.

## -지만 vs 하지만 vs -는데: which one?

| You want to... | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| say "A but B" in one sentence, clearly | -지만 | 작지만 편해요. (jakjiman pyeonhaeyo) It's small but comfortable. |
| start a new sentence with "But" | 하지만 / 그런데 | 작아요. 하지만 편해요. (jagayo. hajiman pyeonhaeyo) |
| sound soft and natural in conversation | -(으)ㄴ/는데 | 작은데 편해요. (jageunde pyeonhaeyo) |
| write formally | -지만 / 그러나 | 작지만 편하다. (jakjiman pyeonhada) |

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 비싸요지만 맛있어요. | 비싸지만 맛있어요. | -지만 goes on the stem, not on the -요 form |
| 추워지만 | 춥지만 | irregular verbs keep the dictionary stem before -지만 |
| 학생지만 | 학생이지만 | nouns ending in a consonant need 이 |
| 비싸 지만 | 비싸지만 | -지만 attaches with no space |
| 비싸지만, 하지만 맛있어요. | 비싸지만 맛있어요. | use -지만 or 하지만, not both |
| 비싼지만 | 비싸지만 | don't mix -지만 with the -ㄴ데 pattern |
| 근데 (in a formal email) | 그런데 / 하지만 | 근데 is casual speech |

Join the two ideas with -지만.

| 멀다 + 괜찮아요 | 멀지만 괜찮아요 (meoljiman gwaenchanayo), it's far but it's okay |
| 어렵다 + 재미있어요 | 어렵지만 재미있어요 (eoryeopjiman jaemiisseoyo), it's hard but fun |
| 비가 왔다 + 갔어요 | 비가 왔지만 갔어요 (biga watjiman gasseoyo), it rained, but I went |

Now translate into Korean:

| English | Answer |

| The hotel is old but clean. | 호텔이 오래됐지만 깨끗해요. (Hoteri oraedwaetjiman kkaekkeutaeyo.) |
| I like coffee. But I don't drink it at night. | 커피를 좋아해요. 하지만 밤에는 안 마셔요. (Keopireul joahaeyo. Hajiman bameneun an masyeoyo.) |

## FAQ

**What is the most common way to say "but" in Korean?** Inside a sentence, -지만 is the textbook answer and works everywhere, while -(으)ㄴ/는데 is what you'll hear most in relaxed conversation. At the start of a sentence, 하지만 is the safe choice and 그런데 or 근데 is the most common in speech.

**What's the difference between 하지만 and 그런데?** 하지만 is a clear "but". 그런데 can mean "but" too, but it's softer and is also used to switch topics ("by the way"). If you're unsure, 하지만 always works for contrast.

**Is 근데 rude?** No, it's just casual. It's normal among friends and common in everyday polite speech, but in formal writing or with someone you need to be very respectful to, use 그런데.

**How do I say "however" in Korean?** 그러나 is the closest formal equivalent, used mostly in writing. In speech, 하지만 or 그렇지만 sound more natural.

**How do I say "even though" or "even if"?** -지만 already covers "although". For "even if" or "even though" with a stronger sense, use **-아도/어도**: **비싸도 살 거예요** (*Bissado sal geoyeyo*), "Even if it's expensive, I'll buy it."

**Does -지만 change with politeness?** No. Politeness goes on the final verb only: 비싸지만 맛있어 (casual), 비싸지만 맛있어요 (polite), 비싸지만 맛있습니다 (formal). See [formal vs informal Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-speech-levels/).

**What's next?** Now you can join ideas with "and", "but", "because" and "if". Try mixing them: **비싸지만 맛있어서 자주 가요** (*Bissajiman masisseoseo jaju gayo*), "It's expensive, but it's tasty, so I go often." Review [if in Korean](/learn-korean/en/korean-if/) to add conditions too.
