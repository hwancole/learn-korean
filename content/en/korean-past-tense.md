The past tense is what lets you answer the most common small-talk questions in Korean: what did you do at the weekend, did you eat, how was the trip. The good news is that if you already know the present tense, you know almost all of the past tense too.

> **Quick answer:** Drop **-다** from the dictionary form to get the stem. If the stem's last vowel is **ㅏ or ㅗ**, add **-았어요** (*-asseoyo*): 살다 → **살았어요**. With any other vowel, add **-었어요** (*-eosseoyo*): 먹다 → **먹었어요**. Every **하다** verb becomes **했어요** (*haesseoyo*): 공부하다 → **공부했어요**. Shortcut: take the polite present form and swap the final **요** for **ㅆ어요**: 가요 → **갔어요**, 봐요 → **봤어요**, 마셔요 → **마셨어요**.

This lesson builds directly on [Korean present tense -아요/-어요](/learn-korean/en/korean-present-tense/), so review that first if the vowel rule is new to you. Examples use the particles from [Korean particles 은/는, 이/가, 을/를](/learn-korean/en/korean-particles/), and romanization follows the Revised Romanization system, written the way the words are pronounced.

## The rule: same vowel check, new ending

The past tense uses exactly the same decision as the present tense. Look at the last vowel of the stem, then choose the ending.

| Last vowel of the stem | Ending | Example | Past tense |
|---|---|---|---|
| ㅏ or ㅗ | -았어요 (asseoyo) | 살다 (to live) | 살았어요 (sarasseoyo) |
| anything else | -었어요 (eosseoyo) | 먹다 (to eat) | 먹었어요 (meogeosseoyo) |
| 하다 verbs | 했어요 (haesseoyo) | 공부하다 (to study) | 공부했어요 (gongbuhaesseoyo) |

The past marker is really **-았-/-었-** (with a double ㅆ), and **-어요** is the polite ending that follows it. When you pronounce it, the ㅆ links to the next syllable: 먹었어요 sounds like *meo-geo-sseo-yo*.

## The shortcut: start from the present tense

Because the past tense is built on the same vowel merging, the easiest method is to take a form you already know. Remove **요** from the present tense and add **ㅆ어요** to the last syllable. All the contractions and irregular changes are already done for you.

| Dictionary form | Present (-요) | Past (-ㅆ어요) | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 가요 | 갔어요 | gasseoyo | went |
| 오다 | 와요 | 왔어요 | wasseoyo | came |
| 보다 | 봐요 | 봤어요 | bwasseoyo | saw, watched |
| 만나다 | 만나요 | 만났어요 | mannasseoyo | met |
| 마시다 | 마셔요 | 마셨어요 | masyeosseoyo | drank |
| 배우다 | 배워요 | 배웠어요 | baewosseoyo | learned |
| 주다 | 줘요 | 줬어요 | jwosseoyo | gave |
| 기다리다 | 기다려요 | 기다렸어요 | gidaryeosseoyo | waited |
| 보내다 | 보내요 | 보냈어요 | bonaesseoyo | sent |
| 되다 | 돼요 | 됐어요 | dwaesseoyo | became |
| 하다 | 해요 | 했어요 | haesseoyo | did |

The one place the shortcut doesn't apply is "to be" (이다), covered below.

### Stems ending in a consonant

Nothing merges here. Just attach the ending.

| Verb | Meaning | Past tense | Romanization |
|---|---|---|---|
| 앉다 | to sit | 앉았어요 | anjasseoyo |
| 받다 | to receive | 받았어요 | badasseoyo |
| 좋다 | to be good | 좋았어요 | joasseoyo |
| 놀다 | to play, hang out | 놀았어요 | norasseoyo |
| 읽다 | to read | 읽었어요 | ilgeosseoyo |
| 입다 | to wear | 입었어요 | ibeosseoyo |
| 있다 | to have, there is | 있었어요 | isseosseoyo |
| 없다 | to not have, there isn't | 없었어요 | eopseosseoyo |
| 재미있다 | to be fun | 재미있었어요 | jaemiisseosseoyo |

### 하다 verbs

Every noun + 하다 verb ends in 했어요, so one pattern covers hundreds of verbs.

| Verb | Meaning | Past tense | Romanization |
|---|---|---|---|
| 일하다 | to work | 일했어요 | ilhaesseoyo |
| 운동하다 | to exercise | 운동했어요 | undonghaesseoyo |
| 요리하다 | to cook | 요리했어요 | yorihaesseoyo |
| 좋아하다 | to like | 좋아했어요 | joahaesseoyo |
| 전화하다 | to call | 전화했어요 | jeonhwahaesseoyo |

### Stems ending in ㅡ

The ㅡ drops, and the vowel before it decides ㅏ or ㅓ. One-syllable stems take ㅓ.

| Verb | Meaning | Past tense | Romanization |
|---|---|---|---|
| 쓰다 | to write, use | 썼어요 | sseosseoyo |
| 크다 | to be big | 컸어요 | keosseoyo |
| 바쁘다 | to be busy | 바빴어요 | bappasseoyo |
| 아프다 | to hurt, be sick | 아팠어요 | apasseoyo |
| 예쁘다 | to be pretty | 예뻤어요 | yeppeosseoyo |

## Irregular verbs

The same irregular groups from the present tense keep their changes in the past tense. If you know the present form, the shortcut handles them.

| Type | Present | Past | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ㅂ irregular | 더워요 | 더웠어요 | deowosseoyo | was hot |
| ㅂ irregular | 추워요 | 추웠어요 | chuwosseoyo | was cold |
| ㅂ irregular | 매워요 | 매웠어요 | maewosseoyo | was spicy |
| ㅂ irregular | 어려워요 | 어려웠어요 | eoryeowosseoyo | was difficult |
| ㅂ irregular | 도와요 | 도왔어요 | dowasseoyo | helped |
| ㄷ irregular | 들어요 | 들었어요 | deureosseoyo | listened |
| ㄷ irregular | 걸어요 | 걸었어요 | georeosseoyo | walked |
| 르 irregular | 몰라요 | 몰랐어요 | mollasseoyo | didn't know |
| 르 irregular | 불러요 | 불렀어요 | bulleosseoyo | sang, called |
| ㅅ irregular | 나아요 | 나았어요 | naasseoyo | got better |

Regular verbs that only look irregular stay regular: 입다 → **입었어요**, 받다 → **받았어요**, 닫다 → **닫았어요** (*dadasseoyo*, closed), 씻다 → **씻었어요** (*ssiseosseoyo*, washed).

## "Was": 이었어요 / 였어요

The copula 이다 ("to be") uses **이었어요** after a consonant and **였어요** after a vowel. Its negative is **아니었어요**.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 학생이었어요. | Haksaengieosseoyo. | I was a student. |
| 의사였어요. | Uisayeosseoyo. | He/She was a doctor. |
| 제 친구였어요. | Je chinguyeosseoyo. | It was my friend. |
| 학생이 아니었어요. | Haksaengi anieosseoyo. | I wasn't a student. |

## The past tense in real sentences

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 주말에 뭐 했어요? | Jumare mwo haesseoyo? | What did you do at the weekend? |
| 친구를 만났어요. | Chingureul mannasseoyo. | I met a friend. |
| 지난주에 부산에 갔어요. | Jinanjue busane gasseoyo. | I went to Busan last week. |
| 아침에 커피를 마셨어요. | Achime keopireul masyeosseoyo. | I drank coffee in the morning. |
| 어제 비가 왔어요. | Eoje biga wasseoyo. | It rained yesterday. |
| 영화가 정말 재미있었어요. | Yeonghwaga jeongmal jaemiisseosseoyo. | The movie was really fun. |
| 시험이 어려웠어요. | Siheomi eoryeowosseoyo. | The exam was difficult. |
| 밥 먹었어요? | Bap meogeosseoyo? | Did you eat? |

That last one deserves a note. **밥 먹었어요?** literally asks "Did you eat (rice)?", but it's often used as a friendly check-in, close to "How are you doing?" A simple **네, 먹었어요** (*Ne, meogeosseoyo*, "Yes, I did") is a fine answer.

### Questions

Just like the present tense, a polite past question uses the same form with rising intonation: **한국에 갔어요?** (*Hanguge gasseoyo?*), "Did you go to Korea?"

### Negatives: 안 and 못

Put **안** (*an*) before the verb for "didn't", and **못** (*mot*) for "couldn't". With noun + 하다 verbs, the negative goes right before 했어요.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 아침을 안 먹었어요. | Achimeul an meogeosseoyo. | I didn't eat breakfast. |
| 어제 학교에 못 갔어요. | Eoje hakgyoe mot gasseoyo. | I couldn't go to school yesterday. |
| 숙제를 안 했어요. | Sukjereul an haesseoyo. | I didn't do the homework. |
| 공부 못 했어요. | Gongbu mot haesseoyo. | I couldn't study. |
| 시간이 없었어요. | Sigani eopseosseoyo. | I didn't have time. |
| 몰랐어요. | Mollasseoyo. | I didn't know. |

## Past forms that don't mean "past" in English

A few common Korean expressions use the past tense where English uses the present, because Korean describes the completed action that led to the current state. Learn them as set phrases.

| Korean | Romanization | Natural English |
|---|---|---|
| 알았어요. | Arasseoyo. | Got it. / Okay. |
| 늦었어요. | Neujeosseoyo. | I'm late. / It's late. |
| 결혼했어요. | Gyeolhonhaesseoyo. | I'm married. (or: I got married.) |
| 잘 먹었습니다. | Jal meogeotseumnida. | Thank you for the meal. (said after eating) |

Not every state works this way: "I'm full" is **배불러요** (*baebulleoyo*) in the present tense, and "I'm hungry" is **배고파요** (*baegopayo*).

## Politeness levels in the past tense

The past marker stays the same across speech styles. Only the ending after it changes.

| Style | 가다 | 먹다 | 하다 | When it's used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formal polite | 갔습니다 (gatseumnida) | 먹었습니다 (meogeotseumnida) | 했습니다 (haetseumnida) | news, presentations, formal speeches |
| Polite (this lesson) | 갔어요 (gasseoyo) | 먹었어요 (meogeosseoyo) | 했어요 (haesseoyo) | everyday talk with strangers, coworkers, older people |
| Casual | 갔어 (gasseo) | 먹었어 (meogeosseo) | 했어 (haesseo) | close friends, younger people, family |

As in the present tense, the casual form is the polite form without **-요**.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 가았어요 | 갔어요 | ㅏ + 았 merges |
| 보았어요 (in speech) | 봤어요 | ㅗ + 았 merges in normal speech |
| 먹았어요 | 먹었어요 | 먹 has ㅓ, so -었어요 |
| 하았어요 | 했어요 | 하다 always becomes 했어요 |
| 덥었어요 | 더웠어요 | ㅂ irregular |
| 듣었어요 | 들었어요 | ㄷ irregular |
| 모르었어요 | 몰랐어요 | 르 irregular |
| 학생였어요 | 학생이었어요 | after a consonant, use 이었어요 |
| 안 공부했어요 | 공부 안 했어요 | 안 goes right before 했어요 |

## FAQ

**Is 갔어요 "I went" or "I have gone"?** Both. Korean has no separate present perfect, so -았어요/-었어요 covers "went", "have gone" and "had gone" depending on context. 한국에 가 봤어요? (*Hanguge ga bwasseoyo?*) is the usual way to ask "Have you ever been to Korea?"

**What is -았었어요/-었었어요?** It's a "double past", as in 갔었어요 (*gasseosseoyo*). It stresses that something happened in the past and is no longer the case, a bit like "I had gone (and came back)" or "I used to". Beginners can use the normal past tense for almost everything.

**Do adjectives take the past tense the same way?** Yes. 좋다 → 좋았어요 ("it was good"), 크다 → 컸어요 ("it was big"), 맛있다 → 맛있었어요 (*masisseosseoyo*, "it was delicious").

**Why is 보았어요 sometimes written in books?** 보았어요 is the full, uncontracted form, and you'll see it in some written texts and songs. In conversation and ordinary messages, 봤어요 is what people say and write. The same applies to 와요/왔어요 and 줘요/줬어요.

**How do I talk about the future?** The present tense with a time word already works (내일 가요, "I'm going tomorrow"), and the -ㄹ/을 거예요 ending (갈 거예요, *gal geoyeyo*) is the common way to say "will". That's the natural next lesson after this one.

**How do I say what time something happened?** Combine this lesson with [Korean numbers](/learn-korean/en/korean-numbers/): hours use native Korean numbers and minutes use Sino-Korean, as in 세 시에 왔어요 (*se sie wasseoyo*, "I came at three o'clock").
